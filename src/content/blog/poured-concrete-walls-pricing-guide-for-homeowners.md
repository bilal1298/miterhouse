Poured concrete walls are one of the most durable foundation and structural wall systems available, but they come at a premium compared to alternatives like concrete block or wood framing. The cost varies significantly based on wall height, thickness, site conditions, and whether you are building a foundation, a retaining wall, or above-grade structural walls.

For a standard residential foundation, poured concrete walls cost $12–$25 per square foot of wall area, or roughly $10,000–$25,000 for a typical full basement. Here is what drives those numbers and how to make informed decisions about your project.

## Poured Concrete Wall Costs: The Full Breakdown

### Foundation Walls (Most Common Residential Use)

The majority of poured concrete walls in residential construction are foundation walls — the below-grade structure that supports the house and creates a basement or crawlspace.

| Component | Cost Per Linear Foot | Cost Per Sq Ft of Wall |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Concrete (material delivered) | $8–$15 | $3–$6 |
| Formwork (rental + setup) | $6–$12 | $3–$5 |
| Rebar and reinforcement | $2–$5 | $1–$2 |
| Labor (pour crew, 4–6 workers) | $8–$15 | $4–$7 |
| Waterproofing (exterior) | $3–$8 | $1.50–$4 |
| Footing (concrete + labor) | $5–$10 | N/A (linear) |

**Total installed cost: $12–$25 per square foot of wall area.**

For a 40x30 foot house with 8-foot foundation walls, you have roughly 1,120 square feet of wall area. At $15–$20/sq ft (mid-range), that is $16,800–$22,400 for the walls alone, plus $3,500–$7,000 for the footing. Total foundation: $20,000–$30,000.

### Retaining Walls

Poured concrete retaining walls cost more per linear foot than foundation walls because they must resist lateral earth pressure without the stabilizing effect of a floor system and building above.

- **Small retaining wall (3–4 feet tall):** $50–$100 per linear foot
- **Medium retaining wall (4–6 feet tall):** $100–$200 per linear foot
- **Tall retaining wall (6–10 feet tall):** $200–$500+ per linear foot

Walls over 4 feet tall typically require engineering and permits in most jurisdictions. A structural engineer's design fee runs $500–$2,000 depending on complexity.

### Above-Grade Concrete Walls

Poured concrete walls for above-grade construction (garages, workshops, certain architectural styles) cost 15–30% more than foundation walls due to finishing requirements — foundation walls are buried and the surface quality does not matter, while above-grade walls may need a smooth finish, form liner patterns, or architectural treatment.

## Poured Concrete vs. Concrete Block: The Honest Comparison

This is the decision most homeowners face when building a foundation. Both have legitimate advantages.

**Poured concrete advantages:**
- Stronger laterally (better resistance to soil pressure and flooding)
- More watertight (monolithic pour has fewer joints than block)
- Faster construction (walls pour in a single day vs. multiple days for block)
- Better for tall walls and high water table areas
- Easier to waterproof (smooth exterior surface)

**Concrete block advantages:**
- 20–30% lower cost in most markets ($8–$18/sq ft vs. $12–$25/sq ft)
- Does not require specialized formwork equipment
- More contractors available (lower barrier to entry)
- Easier to modify after construction (cutting openings, running utilities)
- No minimum order quantity (concrete trucks have minimums)

**When poured concrete is worth the premium:** High water table, deep foundations (9+ feet), seismically active areas, passive house construction (thermal mass benefit), or when the foundation will be a finished living space (basement). Per the [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/), both systems meet structural requirements when properly designed and constructed.

## What Affects the Cost Most

### Wall Height and Thickness

Standard residential foundation walls are 8 feet tall and 8–10 inches thick. Going taller (9 or 10-foot basement walls — increasingly popular for livable basements) increases cost proportionally for concrete material but also requires taller forms, more rebar, and a larger footing.

Increasing thickness from 8" to 10" adds roughly 25% more concrete volume. Some jurisdictions and engineering requirements mandate thicker walls for deep foundations or poor soil conditions.

### Site Access

Concrete trucks and pump trucks need access to the foundation. If the truck cannot get within reach of the forms, a boom pump is required — adding $800–$2,000 for the pump truck and operator. Tight urban lots, hillside sites, or sites with existing structures all increase the access challenge and cost.

### Concrete Mix Design

Standard residential concrete is 3,000–4,000 PSI compressive strength. Higher-strength mixes (5,000+ PSI) cost more per yard but may be required for certain structural applications. Admixtures (fiber reinforcement, water reducers, accelerators for cold weather pours) add $10–$30 per cubic yard.

A typical 8-foot, 8-inch-thick wall uses approximately 0.20 cubic yards of concrete per linear foot. At $150–$180 per cubic yard delivered, that is $30–$36 in concrete material per linear foot of wall.

### Formwork

Formwork is the largest single variable in poured concrete wall pricing. Contractors with their own aluminum or steel form systems have lower per-job costs than those renting forms. Form rental runs $0.50–$1.50 per square foot of wall per day, and the forms are typically in use for 2–3 days (setup, pour, strip).

Specialty form requirements — curved walls, tall walls, or architectural form liners — increase both rental and labor costs significantly.

### Rebar and Reinforcement

Standard residential foundation walls require horizontal and vertical rebar, typically #4 or #5 bars at 12–24 inch spacing. Heavily loaded walls, tall walls, or seismically active areas require more reinforcement per engineering design.

Rebar material costs $0.70–$1.20 per pound installed. A typical foundation wall uses 1–2 pounds of rebar per square foot of wall area. Fiber-reinforced concrete (synthetic or steel fibers mixed into the concrete) is used as supplemental reinforcement in some applications — it adds $2–$5 per cubic yard.

## The Construction Process

Understanding the timeline helps you plan around the disruption and coordinate with other trades.

**Day 1: Footing pour.** The concrete footing is poured into trenches at the base of the wall location. Footings are typically 20–24 inches wide and 8–12 inches deep for standard residential construction. Keyways (grooves) or rebar dowels are placed in the top of the footing to lock the wall to it.

**Days 2–3: Footing cure.** The footing needs to reach sufficient strength before wall forms go up. In warm weather, this is 24–48 hours minimum. Cold weather extends this.

**Days 3–5: Wall form setup.** Crews assemble the forms on both sides of the wall, install rebar, set window and door bucks (openings), and place any embedded items (anchor bolts, beam pockets, utility sleeves).

**Day 5–6: Wall pour.** Concrete is delivered by truck and placed in the forms — either directly from the truck's chute, by pump truck, or occasionally by crane and bucket. The pour must be continuous — you cannot stop halfway and resume the next day without creating a cold joint (a weak plane in the wall). A typical residential foundation pours in 2–4 hours.

**Days 7–8: Cure and strip.** Forms stay in place for at least 24 hours, sometimes longer in cold weather. After stripping, the walls cure for 7+ days before backfilling and loading. Full strength is reached at 28 days.

**After strip: Waterproofing.** Exterior waterproofing is applied to the outside face — either spray-applied membrane, sheet membrane, or dimple board drainage mat. This is a critical step that prevents basement moisture problems for the life of the foundation. The [EPA lead safety guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/lead) apply when working on older foundations that may have lead-based coatings.

## Finishing and Coating Poured Concrete Walls

If the concrete walls will be exposed (basement finish, garage, architectural exterior), finishing options include:

**Painting:** The lowest-cost finish. Use masonry primer ($30–$50/gallon) followed by masonry paint or elastomeric coating ($35–$75/gallon). Two coats minimum. Allow concrete to cure 28 days before painting. Total cost for a typical basement: $200–$500 in materials, or $1,000–$3,000 professional.

**Parging:** A thin cement coating troweled over the concrete for a smoother appearance. Common on above-grade portions of foundation walls. Material cost: $2–$4/sq ft. Labor: $4–$8/sq ft.

**Form liner finishes:** Textured rubber liners placed inside the forms before pouring create patterns — stone, wood grain, brick, or custom designs — on the finished wall surface. These add $2–$6/sq ft but eliminate the need for any additional finishing. Per [CPSC safety guidelines](https://www.cpsc.gov/), any coating products used on interior concrete walls should be evaluated for off-gassing, particularly in enclosed spaces.

## Common Problems and Repair Costs

Poured concrete walls are durable, but not immune to issues:

**Shrinkage cracks:** Nearly every poured concrete wall develops hairline shrinkage cracks during curing. These are cosmetic unless they transmit water. Epoxy injection to seal water-leaking cracks: $300–$600 per crack professional, or $20–$40 per crack for DIY injection kits.

**Bowing or displacement:** Lateral soil pressure, particularly in clay soils, can push walls inward over time. Carbon fiber strap reinforcement: $400–$800 per strap installed. Steel beam bracing: $500–$1,000 per beam. Wall replacement for severe cases: $15,000–$30,000+.

**Water intrusion:** Usually caused by failed waterproofing, clogged footer drains, or cracks. Interior drainage system installation: $3,000–$8,000. Exterior excavation and re-waterproofing: $5,000–$15,000.

**Honeycombing:** Voids in the concrete surface caused by poor consolidation during the pour. Cosmetic repair with patching mortar: $5–$15/sq ft. If structural, may require engineering evaluation.

## The Bottom Line

Poured concrete walls are the premium choice for residential foundations and structural walls, costing $12–$25 per square foot installed — roughly 20–30% more than concrete block. The premium buys you superior waterproofing potential, higher lateral strength, and faster construction. For new construction with a full basement, a quality poured foundation runs $20,000–$30,000. Factor in waterproofing, backfill, and drainage, and your total below-grade system comes to $25,000–$40,000. It is a significant investment, but one that lasts the lifetime of the structure when done correctly.
