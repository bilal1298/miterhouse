Getting a quote for fence installation is the first real step toward a new fence, and it sets the tone for the entire project. A well-structured quote protects you financially, ensures you get the fence you want, and gives you leverage if anything goes wrong during construction. A vague quote does the opposite.

Here is how the quoting process works from start to finish, what fair pricing looks like, and how to tell a solid contractor from one who is going to give you headaches.

## The Quoting Process: What to Expect

### Step 1: The Initial Call

Most fence companies offer free estimates. When you call, be ready to provide:

- Your address (they may check the lot on satellite imagery before visiting)
- Approximate linear footage needed
- Fence type and height you are considering
- Whether there is an existing fence to remove
- Your timeline (urgent vs. flexible)

Some companies give a rough phone estimate based on this information. Treat this as a ballpark only. The real number comes after the site visit.

### Step 2: The Site Visit

A competent estimator does more than walk around with a measuring wheel. They should:

- **Measure the exact fence line** with a measuring wheel or laser
- **Note terrain features:** slopes, rocks, tree roots, wet spots
- **Identify obstacles:** utilities, structures, easements
- **Check soil conditions:** probing or asking about past digging experiences
- **Ask about your property line:** do you have a survey, pins, or a plat map
- **Discuss specifics:** gate placement, height changes, material preferences
- **Ask about your goals:** privacy, pet containment, security, or aesthetics

The visit should take 20 to 40 minutes for a standard residential project. If the estimator spends 5 minutes, jots a number on a card, and leaves, the quote will be equally rushed.

### Step 3: The Written Quote

A proper fence installation quote arrives within 3 to 7 business days. It should be a written document, not a text message or verbal figure. Here is what a thorough quote contains:

**Project description.** A clear summary of what is being built: "Install 275 linear feet of 6-foot tall dog-ear cedar privacy fence with three horizontal rails, 4x4 pressure-treated posts set 30 inches deep in concrete, 8 feet on center, including two 42-inch walk-through gates with self-closing hinges."

**Materials list.** Either itemized or described with enough specificity to verify: post dimensions and material, rail dimensions, picket style and dimensions, fastener type, concrete type and quantity, gate hardware, and post caps.

**Labor description.** Post hole digging, post setting, rail attachment, picket attachment, gate installation, old fence removal (if applicable), and site cleanup.

**Additional work.** Grading, brush clearing, root removal, utility avoidance measures, driveway or sidewalk protections.

**Price breakdown.** At minimum: materials subtotal, labor subtotal, permit fee, tax, and total. Better quotes itemize further.

**Schedule.** Estimated start date and completion date.

**Payment terms.** Deposit amount and timing, progress payments (if any), and final payment terms.

**Warranty.** What is covered, for how long, and what voids it.

## What Fair Pricing Looks Like

To know whether your quotes are reasonable, you need reference points. These are 2026 installed prices per linear foot for the most common residential fence types.

| Fence Type | Low | Average | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain link, 4 ft | $8 | $12 | $18 |
| Chain link, 6 ft | $12 | $18 | $25 |
| Wood privacy (PT pine), 6 ft | $15 | $22 | $30 |
| Wood privacy (cedar), 6 ft | $20 | $30 | $40 |
| Vinyl privacy, 6 ft | $22 | $32 | $45 |
| Aluminum ornamental, 4 ft | $25 | $35 | $50 |
| Composite, 6 ft | $30 | $42 | $55 |

**Gates add to the per-foot average.** A standard walk-through gate costs $200 to $400 on top of the fence price. A double-drive gate (10 to 12 feet wide for vehicles) costs $500 to $1,000.

**Old fence removal** typically costs $3 to $8 per linear foot, depending on the material being removed and whether the contractor handles disposal.

**Permit fees** range from $50 to $200 in most municipalities.

## Comparing Multiple Quotes

The key to useful comparisons is ensuring every company is quoting the same thing. Here is a systematic approach.

### Create a Specification Sheet

Write up a one-page spec that you hand to every estimator. Include:

- Total linear footage
- Fence style and height
- Material preference (or acceptable options)
- Post size and minimum depth
- Post spacing (8 ft on center is standard)
- Number and width of gates
- Existing fence removal scope
- Any known soil conditions or obstacles

Every company bids against the same spec. Now you are comparing the same fence from different builders, not three different fences from three different companies.

## Price vs. Value: What Matters Most

The cheapest quote is rarely the best value. Here is where low-cost contractors typically cut corners.

**Post depth.** Posts should be set at least one-third of their total length below grade. For a 6-foot fence using 8-foot posts, that means 24 to 30 inches deep. Budget contractors often go to 18 inches, which saves 30 percent on digging time but produces a fence that leans within 3 to 5 years.

**Post spacing.** Moving from 8-foot to 10-foot spacing reduces the number of posts by 20 percent. It also reduces structural integrity. Wind load on a 6-foot privacy panel is significant, and 10-foot spans flex noticeably.

**Concrete volume.** Each post hole should get 1 to 2 bags (80 pounds each) of concrete. Some budget contractors use half a bag per post or skip concrete entirely in favor of tamped gravel. Gravel works in dry, stable soil. In freeze-thaw climates or loose soil, it is asking for problems.

**Fastener quality.** Galvanized nails cost less than stainless steel screws but rust faster, especially in coastal or high-humidity climates. Corrosion stains the wood and weakens the joint over time.

**Wood grade.** There is a wide range within "pressure-treated" and "cedar." Premium grades have fewer knots, less warp, and more consistent dimensions. Builder-grade material costs 20 to 30 percent less but includes more defects.

## When a Quote Seems Too Good

If one quote is 30 to 40 percent below the others, investigate before celebrating. Ask the contractor directly:

- "What grade of lumber are you using?"
- "How deep are you setting the posts?"
- "Are you using concrete for every post?"
- "What is your post spacing?"
- "Is the permit included?"

Sometimes the answer is that they are efficient, buying materials in bulk, or filling a slow week. That is fine. But sometimes the answer reveals that you would be paying less for a meaningfully inferior product.

## Protecting Yourself After Signing

The quote becomes a contract once both parties sign. Make sure these protections are in place:

**Payment schedule.** Never pay more than 25 to 30 percent upfront. Typical structure: 10 to 25 percent at signing, 25 to 50 percent at material delivery, balance at completion and satisfactory walkthrough.

**Change order process.** If anything changes mid-project (additional obstacles discovered, material substitutions), it should require a written change order signed by both parties before proceeding. Verbal approvals lead to billing disputes.

**Lien waiver.** Request a lien waiver with the final payment. This protects you from claims by material suppliers if the contractor fails to pay their bills.

**Completion photography.** Photograph the entire installation at completion, including close-ups of post bases, concrete, gate hardware, and any areas that might be covered by landscaping later. Store these with your contract.

## The Takeaway

A fence quote is only useful if it contains enough detail to understand exactly what you are buying. Get at least three quotes against the same specification, compare them systematically, verify licenses and insurance, and never let price be the only factor. The contractor who builds your fence correctly the first time at a fair price is always cheaper than the one who charges less upfront but leaves you with a fence that needs rebuilding in five years.
