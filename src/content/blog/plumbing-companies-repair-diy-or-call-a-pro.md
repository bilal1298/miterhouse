Choosing between handling a plumbing repair yourself and hiring a plumbing company comes down to three things: the complexity of the problem, the tools required, and the potential downside if something goes wrong. This guide helps you evaluate both options honestly so you can make the right call for your specific situation.

A reputable plumbing repair company charges $150 to $350 for a standard service call, plus parts. For many jobs, that is money well spent. For others, you are paying $200 for someone to do $15 worth of work that takes 10 minutes.

## What Plumbing Companies Actually Charge

Understanding the pricing structure helps you evaluate quotes and spot overcharges.

**Service call fee:** $50 to $150 just to show up. This covers the plumber's travel time and truck. Some companies waive this if you proceed with the repair. Ask before scheduling.

**Hourly rate:** $75 to $200 per hour during business hours. After-hours and weekend rates add 50% to 100%. Emergency rates (nights and holidays) can hit $300 to $500 per hour.

**Flat-rate pricing:** Many companies have moved to flat-rate pricing for common repairs. This means a toilet repair is a fixed price regardless of how long it takes. Flat rate favors the company on simple jobs and favors you on complicated ones.

**Parts markup:** Plumbing companies mark up parts 50% to 200% over retail. A $15 faucet cartridge might appear on your invoice at $35 to $45. This is standard industry practice, but it is worth knowing when you compare DIY parts cost to a professional quote.

## Jobs Worth Hiring Out

Some plumbing work is genuinely better left to a professional company. Here is where the value justifies the cost.

### Sewer Camera Inspection

A sewer camera costs $10,000 to $15,000 to buy. No homeowner should own one. But a $150 to $300 camera inspection tells you exactly what is happening in your sewer line -- roots, bellied sections, offsets, or breaks. This information prevents you from spending $5,000 on excavation when a $400 hydro-jetting would have solved the problem.

### Water Heater Replacement

Gas water heater installation involves gas connections, flue venting, and building code compliance. Most jurisdictions require permits and licensed installers. A plumbing company handles the entire process -- permits, installation, inspection, and haul-away of the old unit -- for $400 to $1,000 in labor on top of the unit cost. Our [water heater installation guide](/blog/installing-water-tank-heater-what-to-know-first/) covers the full scope.

### Repipes and Major Line Work

Replacing the plumbing in a whole house or running new supply lines to a remodeled space is a multi-day project involving permits, wall penetrations, and connections to the main line. A plumbing company brings a crew, coordinates the work efficiently, and carries insurance if something goes wrong.

### Gas Line Work

Licensed, insured, permitted. No shortcuts. A plumbing company that handles gas work carries specific insurance and certifications for it. The consequences of improper gas work -- from [OSHA's perspective](https://www.osha.gov/) and your family's safety -- make this a firm no-DIY zone.

## Jobs You Should Handle Yourself

For these repairs, paying a plumbing company is paying a premium for simplicity.

**Toilet flapper and fill valve replacement.** A $12 universal kit and 20 minutes of time versus a $150 to $250 service call. The risk of getting this wrong is basically zero.

**Unclogging drains.** A plunger and drain snake handle 90% of household clogs. The other 10% (deep main line blockages) is where a plumber's tools make sense.

**Replacing faucets and showerheads.** These come with instructions, and the connections are standardized. A basin wrench makes faucet installation much easier, and the total DIY cost is typically 40% to 60% less than hiring out.

**Supply line and valve replacement.** Braided stainless supply lines and compression-fit shutoff valves install with basic wrenches. Replacing them preventively every 8 to 10 years is one of the cheapest insurance policies in your house.

**Garbage disposal reset and replacement.** A humming disposal just needs the Allen wrench socket on the bottom turned to free the jam. Even a full disposal replacement is a 30 to 60-minute DIY project if the electrical outlet already exists.

## How to Evaluate a Plumbing Company

When you do need professional help, here is how to find a company that will treat you fairly.

### Licensing

Every state licenses plumbers. Use your state's contractor license lookup to verify. An unlicensed plumber is illegal in most jurisdictions and cannot pull permits, which means the work is not inspected and your insurance may not cover failures.

### Insurance

Ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability and workers' compensation. If an uninsured plumber floods your bathroom during a repair, you are left holding the bag.

### Reviews -- But Read Between the Lines

Look at reviews on multiple platforms. A company with 4.5 stars across 200 reviews is generally reliable. More useful than star ratings: read the negative reviews. If complaints consistently mention hidden charges, no-show appointments, or pressure tactics, that tells you more than the positive reviews.

### The Estimate

A professional plumbing company provides a written estimate before starting work. That estimate should include:

- Specific description of the work to be performed
- Parts and materials with individual pricing
- Labor charges (hourly or flat rate)
- Permit costs, if applicable
- Warranty terms
- Timeline for completion

If a company will not commit to a written estimate, that is a red flag. Verbal quotes lead to invoice surprises.

### Warranty

Reputable companies warranty their work for a minimum of one year. Some offer two-year warranties on major installations. The warranty should cover both parts and labor -- a parts-only warranty means you pay the service call again if their installation fails.

## Red Flags to Watch For

Years of hearing from readers has taught me the warning signs of problematic plumbing companies:

- **Demanding full payment upfront.** A reasonable deposit (10% to 30%) for large jobs is normal. Full payment before work begins is not.
- **Quoting without seeing the problem.** A plumber who quotes you a price over the phone, sight-unseen, is either going to upsell you on arrival or lowball and add charges later.
- **Creating urgency.** "If we don't fix this today, your whole system could fail." Most plumbing problems develop over weeks or months. Manufactured urgency is a sales tactic.
- **Cash-only payment.** Legitimate companies accept multiple payment methods. Cash-only suggests they are avoiding a paper trail.
- **No license number provided.** If they hesitate or deflect when asked for their license number, end the conversation.

## The Hybrid Approach

The smartest homeowners use a hybrid strategy: DIY the simple stuff, build a relationship with a reliable plumbing company for the complex work.

Handle your own toilet repairs, drain clogs, faucet swaps, and supply line replacements. This keeps you familiar with your plumbing system and saves hundreds per year.

For [pipe repairs](/blog/plumbing-pipe-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/), [emergency situations](/blog/emergency-plumbing-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/), and [major plumbing work](/blog/home-plumbing-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/), have a trusted company you can call. Meet building code requirements set by the [IRC](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) and checked by [WaterSense standards](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) to ensure efficiency.

When your company knows you handle the small stuff yourself, they know you are calling about something that actually needs their expertise. That makes for a better working relationship and often faster response times.

The goal is not to never call a plumber. It is to call a plumber only when calling a plumber is the right answer.
