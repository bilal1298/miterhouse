Granite countertops cost $50 to $120 per square foot fully installed, with the average kitchen landing between $2,000 and $4,800 for 40 square feet of counter space. The range is wide because granite pricing depends heavily on the specific color you choose, the complexity of your kitchen layout, and your local fabricator's rates.

Understanding the pricing structure helps you avoid the two most common granite shopping mistakes: choosing a slab based on the starting-at price (which is always the least popular colors) and forgetting to budget for the extras that add 20 to 30 percent on top.

## Granite Slab Pricing: The Level System

Granite fabricators and distributors grade their inventory by color rarity and demand, not by quality or durability. All granite, regardless of grade, is equally hard and functionally identical. The grading is purely aesthetic and supply-driven.

### Level 1 (Entry): $50–$75 per SF Installed

The most abundant colors with steady supply and moderate demand. These are not lesser stones — they are simply common.

Popular Level 1 options:
- **Uba Tuba:** Dark green-black with subtle gold flecks. Hides everything, works with any cabinet color.
- **Caledonia:** Medium gray with black and white speckling. Clean, neutral, modern.
- **New Venetian Gold:** Warm tan with brown, gold, and cream veining. Pairs well with cherry or oak cabinets.
- **Santa Cecilia:** Gold base with burgundy and black movement. Warm and busy pattern.
- **Giallo Ornamental:** Light cream with brown and gray. Bright and airy.

Level 1 granites are the value play. A 40 SF kitchen in Caledonia granite runs $2,000 to $3,000 installed — competitive with mid-range quartz and far below marble or quartzite.

### Level 2 (Mid-Range): $75–$100 per SF Installed

Less common colors with more dramatic veining, unique mineral patterns, or trending color profiles.

Popular Level 2 options:
- **White Ice:** White base with gray veining. The affordable alternative to marble aesthetics.
- **Alaska White:** Creamy white with gray and garnet deposits.
- **Colonial White:** Light gray with burgundy and black movement.
- **Blue Pearl:** Dark blue-gray with iridescent labradorite crystals.

A 40 SF kitchen in Level 2 granite runs $3,000 to $4,000.

### Level 3+ (Premium/Exotic): $100–$120+ per SF Installed

Rare colors, dramatic movement, large-crystal structures, and limited availability.

Examples:
- **Calacatta marble-look granites** (White Fantasy, Thunder White)
- **Exotic blues** (Azul Macaubas, Blue Bahia)
- **Book-matched slabs** (consecutive slabs cut from the same block, installed to mirror the pattern)

A 40 SF kitchen in exotic granite runs $4,000 to $4,800+. At this price point, you are competing with premium quartz — and the decision comes down to whether you value natural uniqueness over engineered consistency.

## Beyond the Slab: The Full Cost Picture

The per-square-foot installed price covers the slab, basic fabrication, and installation. Here is everything else.

### Template ($0–$200)

Most fabricators include the template visit in the per-square-foot price. Some charge separately — $100 to $200 for the templater to come out with digital measuring equipment and map your counter layout. Confirm before signing.

### Edge Profiles

| Profile | Additional Cost per Linear Foot |
|---|---|
| Eased (straight with softened top edge) | Included |
| Flat polished | Included |
| Half bullnose | $8–$12 |
| Full bullnose | $10–$15 |
| Beveled | $8–$12 |
| Ogee | $15–$25 |
| Dupont | $15–$20 |
| Mitered (thick look) | $25–$40 |

A 40 SF L-shaped kitchen has roughly 16 linear feet of finished edge. An ogee upgrade at $20/LF adds $320 to the project.

### Sink Cutouts

- **Drop-in sink:** Cutout typically included.
- **Undermount sink:** Cutout plus polished edge adds $150 to $300 per sink.
- **Farmhouse/apron sink:** Requires cabinet modification ($200 to $400) plus the cutout.

### Backsplash

- **4-inch granite backsplash:** Often included or discounted — cut from the same slab. If priced separately: $10 to $25/LF.
- **Full-height granite backsplash:** $30 to $50/LF. Dramatic but expensive — a 12 LF kitchen run adds $360 to $600.

### Old Countertop Removal

$200 to $600 depending on the existing material. Some fabricators include removal; others quote it separately. Our [countertop removal cost guide](/blog/countertop-removal-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/) covers the specifics.

### Plumbing

Disconnecting and reconnecting the sink, faucet, garbage disposal, and dishwasher line: $150 to $350 if you hire a plumber. This is straightforward DIY if you are comfortable with basic plumbing connections.

## Ways to Lower Granite Costs

**Choose Level 1 colors.** The functional quality is identical. Uba Tuba and Caledonia are genuinely attractive stones used in high-end kitchens daily.

**Keep the eased edge.** The eased edge (a simple straight edge with the sharp corner softened) is clean, modern, and free. Decorative edges add hundreds to the project for purely aesthetic value.

**Choose drop-in over undermount sinks.** The rim covers the cutout edge, eliminating the need for polished edging. Savings: $150 to $300.

**Buy remnants for small projects.** Bathroom vanities, laundry rooms, and wet bars often fit remnant pieces that fabricators sell at 30 to 50 percent off standard slab pricing.

**Skip the full-height backsplash.** A 4-inch granite splash with tile above is significantly cheaper than full-height granite and still looks sharp.

**Get quotes from small fabricators.** Independent shops with lower overhead often beat big-box programs by 15 to 25 percent. They also tend to have wider slab selection and more fabrication flexibility.

## Granite Maintenance Costs

Annual granite maintenance is minimal in both cost and time.

- **Sealer:** $15 to $30 per bottle, covers the whole kitchen. Apply once per year. Takes 15 minutes.
- **Daily cleaning:** Mild dish soap and warm water. $0 incremental cost.
- **Chip repair kit:** $15 to $30 for epoxy kits that fill small chips. Rarely needed.
- **Professional resealing:** $100 to $200 every 3 to 5 years if you prefer not to DIY. Optional — it is easy to do yourself.

Over 15 years, maintenance adds roughly $300 to $600 to the total cost of ownership. That is less than 10 percent of the installation cost.

## Is Granite Worth It in 2026?

Granite offers genuine natural stone at a price point that competes with engineered alternatives. At Level 1 pricing ($50 to $75/SF installed), it is one of the best values in premium countertops — real stone durability and beauty for barely more than high-end laminate.

The maintenance question is the real differentiator. If annual sealing bothers you, quartz eliminates that task entirely — see our [quartz vs. granite comparison](/blog/quartz-countertop-vs-granite-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/) for a direct matchup. If 15 minutes of sealing per year is a non-issue, granite gives you a unique, heat-resistant natural surface that no engineered product fully replicates.
