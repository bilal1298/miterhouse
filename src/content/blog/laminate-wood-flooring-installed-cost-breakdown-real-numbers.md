Laminate wood flooring installed costs between $6 and $12 per square foot for the complete package — product, underlayment, labor, subfloor prep, and trim. A typical 350-square-foot living room runs $2,100 to $4,200 fully installed, with most projects in the $2,500 to $3,500 range when using a mid-grade product and a professional installer. These are real 2026 numbers that include everything from the first measurement to the final transition strip.

The "installed" price is the number that matters because the material cost alone tells you almost nothing. A $3 per square foot laminate plank turns into $8 to $10 per square foot once you account for the labor, prep, and materials that surround it. Here is exactly how those dollars break down.

## Line-by-Line Cost Breakdown

I am going to walk through a real project: replacing 350 square feet of carpet in a living room with mid-range laminate wood flooring, professional installation, plywood subfloor in good condition.

### The Flooring Material: $1,050

Mid-range laminate at $2.75 per square foot, buying 385 square feet (350 sq ft of room plus 10% for waste and cuts). That 10% waste factor is not padding the budget — it accounts for the end cuts on each row, cuts around obstacles, and the inevitable plank or two that cracks during installation. Running short means a second trip to the store and potentially a different dye lot.

The product in this example is a 10mm, AC4-rated, click-lock plank with an embossed-in-register texture. It has no pre-attached underlayment. Brand-name products at this price point include Pergo Outlast, Mohawk RevWood Select, and Shaw Repel.

### Underlayment: $77

Standard 3mm foam underlayment at $0.20 per square foot for 385 square feet. This room is over a plywood subfloor on the second floor, so a moisture barrier is not needed, but the foam provides cushion and sound dampening. If this were a concrete slab, the underlayment would be a moisture-barrier product at $0.50 to $0.75 per square foot, nearly tripling this line item.

### Old Carpet Removal: $385

At $1.10 per square foot, this covers pulling up the carpet, removing the pad, pulling out tack strips, and removing all the staples left in the subfloor from the old padding. The staple removal is the most time-consuming part — a 350-square-foot room can have 200 to 400 staples embedded in the subfloor. Every one has to come out cleanly or it creates a bump under the new laminate.

Disposal is included in this line — the installer hauls the old carpet away. If you are DIY-removing the carpet, budget $40 to $80 for dump fees.

### Subfloor Preparation: $175

This room has a plywood subfloor in generally good condition. The prep work involves:
- Walking every square foot looking for squeaks, soft spots, and protruding fasteners
- Driving screws into any squeaky spots (squeaks come from the subfloor moving against the joist — screws pull the plywood tight against the framing and eliminate the movement)
- Checking flatness with a 6-foot straightedge and sanding any high spots
- Filling any gaps wider than 1/8 inch between subfloor panels

On this project, the subfloor prep is light: about an hour of work. On a project with a more damaged subfloor — water damage near an exterior door, for example — prep costs can run $500 to $1,500 for the same room size.

### Installation Labor: $1,050

At $3.00 per square foot, the installer lays the underlayment, installs the laminate planks, makes all necessary cuts, undercuts the door jambs (so the laminate slides neatly underneath instead of butting against the trim), and installs transition strips at the doorways.

This is the number where DIY saves you the most. The same installation done yourself costs $0 in labor — but plan for 8 to 12 hours of your time as a first-timer versus 4 to 6 hours for a professional crew.

### Baseboards and Trim: $320

Removing the existing baseboards before installation ($0.50/linear foot), then reinstalling them after the floor is in ($1.50/linear foot). The room has 80 linear feet of baseboard. Some baseboards crack during removal — budget for replacing 10-15% of the trim with new material.

Two transition strips at the doorways add another $70.

### The Complete Installed Price

| Line Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Laminate material (385 sq ft) | $1,050 |
| Underlayment | $77 |
| Carpet removal and disposal | $385 |
| Subfloor preparation | $175 |
| Installation labor | $1,050 |
| Baseboards (remove/reinstall) | $250 |
| Transition strips (2) | $70 |
| **Total installed** | **$3,057** |
| **Per square foot (based on 350 sq ft room)** | **$8.73** |

That $8.73 per square foot is the real installed cost — not the $2.75 you see on the price tag at the store.

## How the Numbers Change by Scenario

The example above is close to a best-case scenario — good subfloor, simple room, carpet removal. Here is how the installed cost shifts with different conditions:

**Over concrete slab (adds $0.75 to $1.50/sq ft):** Moisture testing ($150-$300), moisture-barrier underlayment upgrade ($0.30-$0.50/sq ft more), and potentially leveling compound for an uneven slab ($1.50-$3.00/sq ft for affected areas).

**Removing tile instead of carpet (adds $1.00 to $2.50/sq ft):** Tile demo is louder, dustier, and slower than carpet removal. It also frequently damages the subfloor beneath, adding repair costs.

**Complex room layout (adds $0.50 to $1.50/sq ft in labor):** Rooms with many angles, alcoves, built-in features, or numerous doorways require more cuts, more precision, and more time.

**Premium laminate material (adds $2.00 to $5.00/sq ft):** Stepping up from mid-range ($2.75/sq ft) to premium ($5.00-$7.00/sq ft) increases the material line proportionally but does not affect labor costs.

**Stairs (adds $50 to $100 per step):** Stair installation is a completely different technique — glued and nailed rather than floating — and each step is labor-intensive.

## The Moisture Factor

Moisture is the single biggest threat to a laminate installation, and it affects installed cost in two ways: the prevention costs upfront and the replacement costs if you get it wrong.

**Prevention costs on concrete slabs:**
- Moisture testing: $150-$300
- Moisture-barrier underlayment: $0.30-$0.75/sq ft more than standard foam
- Total additional cost for a 350-sq-ft room: $275-$575

**Replacement cost if moisture is not addressed:**
- New laminate material: $1,050
- New underlayment: $77-$260
- Labor to remove damaged laminate and reinstall: $1,400-$2,100
- Total cost of a do-over: $2,500-$3,400

The math is clear: spending $275 to $575 on proper moisture management is vastly cheaper than a $2,500+ reinstallation. If you have dealt with [moisture-damaged laminate](/blog/moisture-damage-laminate-flooring-what-homeowners-should-know/) before, you already know this lesson.

For concrete slab installations, insist on a calcium chloride moisture test or in-situ relative humidity test before installation begins. The results should be documented in writing, and the installer should not proceed if the readings exceed the laminate manufacturer's specifications. This documentation also protects your warranty.

## Comparing Installed Costs: Laminate vs. Alternatives

When you include all installation costs, here is how laminate compares to other flooring options:

| Flooring Type | Material/sq ft | Installed Total/sq ft | 350 sq ft Room |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget laminate | $1.00-$2.00 | $5-$8 | $1,750-$2,800 |
| Mid-range laminate | $2.50-$4.00 | $7-$11 | $2,450-$3,850 |
| Premium laminate | $4.00-$7.00 | $9-$14 | $3,150-$4,900 |
| Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) | $2.50-$6.00 | $7-$13 | $2,450-$4,550 |
| Engineered hardwood | $4.00-$10.00 | $10-$18 | $3,500-$6,300 |
| Solid hardwood | $5.00-$14.00 | $12-$22 | $4,200-$7,700 |

Laminate and LVP occupy nearly the same price bracket. The installed cost difference between mid-range laminate and mid-range LVP is often less than $1 per square foot. The decision between them should be based on moisture exposure (LVP wins), sound and feel underfoot (premium laminate wins), and visual preference (subjective, but premium laminate tends to produce a more convincing wood look at close inspection).

For [vinyl sheet flooring](/blog/installation-sheet-vinyl-flooring-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/), the installed cost is typically lower ($5-$9/sq ft), but the aesthetic is different — sheet vinyl reads as vinyl, while good laminate reads as wood.

## Getting the Best Installed Price

Strategies that actually save money without compromising quality:

**Buy materials during sales.** Home improvement stores run flooring promotions regularly — spring and fall events, holiday weekends, and clearance of discontinued patterns. The material is the same; the timing is better.

**Do your own demo.** Removing carpet, old laminate, or vinyl yourself is straightforward and saves $1 to $3 per square foot. The installer arrives to a clean subfloor, which also speeds up their work.

**Shop independent installers, not just big-box installation services.** Big-box stores (Home Depot, Lowe's, Floor & Decor) offer convenient one-stop-shop installation, but their pricing includes their markup on the labor subcontractor. An independent installer hired directly often charges 15-25% less for the same work.

**Bundle rooms together.** Installers price per square foot, and their mobilization cost (driving to your home, setting up, cleaning up) is the same whether they are doing one room or three. Per-square-foot pricing drops on larger projects.

**Avoid the cheapest laminate.** This sounds counterintuitive for saving money, but budget laminate wears out faster, looks less convincing, and is harder to install cleanly (thin planks are more prone to chipping during cuts and cracking during assembly). Spending $1 more per square foot on material ($350 on a 350-sq-ft room) gets you a product that lasts 5-10 years longer — a far better value over the life of the floor.

The [NWFA installation guidelines](https://www.nwfa.org/) recommend that homeowners verify installer credentials, confirm proper acclimation of materials (48-72 hours at room temperature before installation), and retain a copy of the manufacturer's installation instructions. Following these steps ensures both a quality installation and valid warranty coverage.

Laminate wood flooring installed cost is not the cheapest flooring option, but it delivers the most convincing wood look per dollar spent. Understanding exactly where every dollar goes — material, underlayment, demo, prep, labor, and trim — puts you in a position to get accurate quotes, avoid surprises, and make informed tradeoffs between budget and quality.
