Labour represents 70 to 85 percent of what you pay a professional painter. For interior work, labour costs run $25 to $75 per hour depending on the market, the scope, and the complexity of the surfaces. For exterior work, that range climbs to $30 to $85 per hour due to ladder work, weather considerations, and surface prep demands.

Understanding how painting labour is priced helps you evaluate quotes, negotiate effectively, and decide whether DIY makes financial sense for your project.

## How Painters Price Labour

Professional painters use one of three pricing models, and the model they use affects your final cost significantly.

### Per Square Foot

The most common method for residential work. The painter measures the wall area (or exterior surface area) and quotes a per-square-foot rate that includes labour and paint.

**Interior rates:**
- Basic rooms (good condition walls, minimal prep): $2 to $4 per square foot
- Standard rooms (some patching, normal prep): $3 to $5 per square foot
- Complex rooms (heavy prep, wallpaper removal, multiple colors): $5 to $8 per square foot

**Exterior rates:**
- Single-story, good condition: $1.50 to $3 per square foot
- Two-story or multi-level: $2.50 to $5 per square foot
- Heavy prep (scraping, priming, repair): $4 to $7 per square foot

### Per Room

Some painters quote a flat rate per room for interior work. This simplifies the estimate but can obscure what is included.

**Typical per-room rates (labour only, paint separate):**
- Small bedroom (10x10): $200 to $400
- Standard bedroom (12x14): $300 to $500
- Master bedroom (14x18): $400 to $700
- Bathroom: $200 to $400
- Kitchen: $400 to $800
- Living room (15x20): $500 to $900

These rates assume standard 8-foot ceilings, two coats, walls only (not ceiling or trim), and normal prep. Add 20 to 30 percent for ceiling painting and 15 to 25 percent for trim, doors, and windows.

### Hourly Rate

Less common for residential work, but some painters use it for small jobs, complex prep, or when the scope is hard to define upfront.

**Typical hourly rates by market:**

| Market | Apprentice/Helper | Journeyman | Master Painter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-cost areas | $18 - $25 | $28 - $40 | $40 - $55 |
| Average markets | $22 - $30 | $35 - $50 | $50 - $65 |
| High-cost areas (NYC, SF, Boston) | $28 - $40 | $45 - $65 | $60 - $85 |

Hourly pricing benefits you if the job is straightforward and the painter works efficiently. It hurts you if the painter works slowly or if unexpected prep extends the timeline.

## What Drives Labour Costs Up

Labour pricing is not arbitrary. These factors directly affect how long the job takes and what skill level is required.

### Surface Preparation

Prep work consumes 50 to 75 percent of total painting time. A room with perfect walls takes half the labour of a room that needs extensive patching, sanding, and priming.

**Low prep:** Clean walls, minor touch-ups, good existing paint. Labour: 2 to 3 hours per average room.

**Medium prep:** Some nail holes, minor cracks, sanding, one coat of primer on patches. Labour: 4 to 6 hours per room.

**Heavy prep:** Wallpaper removal ($1 to $3 per square foot extra), multiple texture repairs, water stain treatment, or lead paint containment. Labour: 6 to 12 hours per room.

If you live in a home built before 1978, [EPA lead paint regulations](https://www.epa.gov/lead) require licensed contractors to follow specific containment and disposal procedures. This adds 20 to 40 percent to the labour cost.

### Ceiling Height

Standard 8-foot ceilings are the baseline for most pricing. Each additional foot of ceiling height adds cost due to ladder work and reduced efficiency.

- 8-foot ceilings: baseline pricing
- 9 to 10 foot ceilings: add 10 to 15 percent
- 12-foot ceilings: add 25 to 35 percent
- Vaulted or cathedral: add 40 to 60 percent (scaffolding may be required)

### Trim and Detail Work

Cutting in around trim, painting crown molding, window frames, doors, and baseboards is the most time-consuming part of painting. A room with extensive trim can double the labour compared to walls only.

- Walls only: baseline
- Walls plus baseboards: add 15 to 20 percent
- Walls, baseboards, crown, and window trim: add 30 to 40 percent
- Add doors ($50 to $100 per door, both sides)

### Number of Colors

Each additional color in a room adds 30 to 60 minutes of labour for cutting in, taping, and equipment cleaning. An accent wall is relatively quick. Four different colors in one room (walls, ceiling, trim, accent) is significantly more work.

## Interior vs. Exterior Labour Differences

Exterior painting labour costs more per hour because of the additional risk, equipment, and variables involved.

**Exterior-specific labour factors:**
- Ladder and scaffolding setup and breakdown (1 to 4 hours per project)
- Safety equipment and liability (reflected in higher hourly rates)
- Weather delays (dead time the painter absorbs or passes to you)
- Surface prep on weathered exteriors (power washing, scraping loose paint, caulking gaps)
- Working around landscaping, outdoor fixtures, and uneven terrain

## How to Reduce Labour Costs (Legitimately)

You can meaningfully reduce your painting labour bill without compromising quality.

**Do the prep yourself.** Move furniture, remove switch plates and outlet covers, fill nail holes with lightweight spackle, sand smooth, and apply painter's tape. This eliminates 1 to 3 hours of labour per room. Most painters welcome this if it is done competently.

**Consolidate rooms.** Painting three rooms at once is cheaper per room than painting one at a time. The setup and cleanup time is amortized across more surface area.

**Choose simpler color schemes.** One color for all walls across multiple rooms is faster than unique colors for each room.

**Be flexible on scheduling.** Painters charge less during their slow season (late fall through early spring for exterior, winter for interior). A painter who can fit your job into a gap in their schedule may offer a better rate.

**Paint ceilings the same color throughout.** Standard white ceilings across the entire house can be rolled quickly without cutting in against a different wall color.

**Skip heavy texture changes.** Painting over a smooth wall is straightforward. Painting over heavy texture (knockdown, orange peel) uses more paint and more time.

## VOCs and Indoor Air Quality

The [EPA's guide to volatile organic compounds](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) is worth reviewing before your project. Low-VOC and zero-VOC paints have become the standard for interior work. They cost slightly more per gallon ($5 to $10 premium) but eliminate the headaches, odor, and air quality concerns of traditional paints.

Professional painters following [OSHA guidelines](https://www.osha.gov/) may charge a small premium for proper ventilation equipment on jobs where VOCs or lead paint are a concern.

## DIY Labour: What Your Time Is Really Worth

The DIY calculation is straightforward: you save the labour cost but invest your time.

**A professional painter** completes an average bedroom (walls, two coats) in 4 to 6 hours. That includes prep, painting, and cleanup. At $40 per hour average, that is $160 to $240 in labour.

**A competent DIYer** takes 8 to 12 hours for the same room. Inexperienced painters take even longer.

If you value your time at $20 per hour, DIYing a bedroom saves you $0 to $80 over hiring a pro. At $30 per hour, you are losing money. The math shifts dramatically for larger projects: a whole-house interior that costs $3,000 to $5,000 in professional labour might save you $2,000 or more as a DIY project, and the total time investment (40 to 80 hours) is spread across many weekends.

## The Bottom Line on Painting Labour

Labour drives the cost of any painting project, and the biggest variable is surface prep. Get quotes that break out prep work separately, ask what the painter considers "standard" prep versus extra, and do whatever prep you can handle yourself. For interior work in good-condition homes, expect to pay $25 to $50 per hour for competent labour. For exteriors or complex interiors, budget $35 to $65 per hour. Know your numbers, and the quotes will make sense.
