Painting the exterior of a house costs $3,000 to $8,000 for a single-story home and $5,000 to $15,000 for a two-story home when hiring a professional crew. DIY cuts those numbers by 60 to 70 percent but demands significantly more time, physical effort, and risk management. Here is how those costs actually break down.

## The Full Cost Picture

### Professional Exterior Painting

| Home Size | Stories | Professional Cost | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 1,500 sq ft | 1 | $2,500 - $5,000 | 2 - 4 days |
| 1,500 - 2,500 sq ft | 1 | $3,500 - $7,000 | 3 - 5 days |
| 1,500 - 2,500 sq ft | 2 | $5,000 - $10,000 | 4 - 7 days |
| 2,500 - 3,500 sq ft | 2 | $7,000 - $13,000 | 5 - 8 days |
| 3,500+ sq ft | 2-3 | $10,000 - $18,000 | 7 - 12 days |

These ranges include labour, paint, prep work, and standard cleanup. They do not include major repairs (rotten wood replacement, structural work) or specialty coatings.

### DIY Exterior Painting

| Paint (premium exterior, 10-15 gallons) | $400 - $750 |
| Primer (if needed, 5-10 gallons) | $150 - $350 |
| Brushes, rollers, and extension poles | $50 - $100 |
| Painter's tape and drop cloths | $30 - $60 |

The savings are real: $2,000 to $5,000 on a typical project. But the time investment is significant --- expect 60 to 100 hours of work for a two-story exterior, spread over several weekends.

## Cost Breakdown by Surface Type

Different exterior surfaces have different paint requirements, prep needs, and costs per square foot.

### Wood Siding (Clapboard, Shingles, Board-and-Batten)

Wood is the most labour-intensive exterior surface to paint. It requires scraping loose paint, sanding rough areas, priming bare wood, caulking gaps, and applying two coats of finish paint.

**Professional cost:** $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot of surface area
**Paint type:** 100% acrylic latex exterior (Sherwin-Williams Duration, Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior, or equivalent)
**Prep time:** 40 to 60 percent of total project time
**Special concern:** Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. The [EPA's Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting program](https://www.epa.gov/lead) requires certified contractors for lead paint disturbance. Testing costs $25 to $50 per area. Certified lead-safe work practices add 20 to 40 percent to labour costs.

### Vinyl Siding

Vinyl can be painted, but it requires specific preparation and products. The paint must be the same shade or lighter than the original color, because dark paints absorb heat and can cause vinyl to warp.

**Professional cost:** $1.50 to $3.00 per square foot
**Paint type:** Vinyl-safe exterior latex (specifically formulated to flex with the vinyl)
**Prep:** Power wash, let dry completely. No scraping needed.
**Lifespan:** 5 to 7 years before repainting (shorter than paint on wood)

### Stucco

Stucco surfaces are porous and consume more paint than smooth surfaces. They also develop hairline cracks that need filling before painting.

**Professional cost:** $2.00 to $4.50 per square foot
**Paint type:** Elastomeric coating (bridges hairline cracks) or 100% acrylic latex
**Prep:** Power wash, fill cracks with elastomeric caulk, prime if bare stucco
**Special concern:** Elastomeric coatings cost 2 to 3 times more per gallon than standard paint but last significantly longer (10 to 15 years vs. 5 to 7 years)

### Brick

Painting brick is a one-way decision. Once painted, brick requires repainting every 5 to 10 years because removing paint from brick is extremely difficult and expensive.

**Professional cost:** $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot
**Paint type:** Masonry-specific acrylic latex with alkalinity resistance
**Prep:** Power wash, repair mortar joints (tuckpointing), apply masonry primer
**Alternative:** Lime washing or German schmear for a lighter look without full paint commitment

### Metal Siding (Aluminum)

**Professional cost:** $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot
**Paint type:** DTM (direct-to-metal) acrylic or alkyd
**Prep:** Remove chalking (that powdery residue on old aluminum) with TSP and water, prime with metal primer

## The Prep Work That Determines Everything

For exterior painting, prep quality is the single biggest predictor of how long the paint job lasts. Rushing prep to save time or money costs you a repaint in 3 to 5 years instead of 7 to 10.

### Power Washing

Every exterior paint job starts here. Power washing removes dirt, mildew, chalking, and loose paint. Use 2,000 to 2,500 PSI for wood siding, 1,500 to 2,000 PSI for vinyl. Keep the nozzle 12 to 18 inches from the surface and work at a downward angle to avoid driving water behind the siding.

**Cost:** $100 to $300 for a professional wash, or $50 to $100 for a day rental on your own.

**Critical detail:** Let the surface dry completely before painting. Two to three dry, sunny days minimum. Painting over damp wood traps moisture and causes peeling.

### Scraping and Sanding

Any loose, peeling, or flaking paint must be removed. You are not stripping to bare wood everywhere --- just removing anything that is not firmly adhered.

**Tools:** 5-in-1 tool, carbide scraper, or oscillating multi-tool with a scraper blade. For large areas, a heat gun speeds up removal but requires care to avoid scorching the wood.

**Sanding:** After scraping, feather the edges where old paint meets bare wood with 80 to 100 grit sandpaper. This prevents a visible paint line.

### Caulking

Seal every gap where two surfaces meet: window and door frames, corner boards, butt joints in siding, and around penetrations (vents, hose bibs, light fixtures). Use a high-quality paintable exterior caulk rated for your climate.

**Budget:** $30 to $80 in caulk for a typical home. This step takes 2 to 4 hours and is the most overlooked part of DIY exterior painting.

### Priming

Prime all bare wood, patched areas, stain bleed-through (from tannin-rich woods like cedar and redwood), and previously unpainted surfaces. Spot priming is fine if most of the existing paint is sound. Whole-surface priming is needed if you are changing colors dramatically or the existing paint is heavily weathered.

## Weather and Timing

Outdoor painting has a narrower window than indoor work. The paint and the weather must cooperate.

**Temperature:** Most exterior paints require application between 50 and 90 degrees F. Some premium formulations work down to 35 degrees F, but curing is slower and results are less reliable.

**Humidity:** Below 85 percent relative humidity. High humidity slows drying and can cause the paint to sag or not adhere properly.

**Rain:** No rain for 4 to 8 hours after application (check the specific paint's spec sheet). If rain is forecast within 24 hours, postpone.

**Sun exposure:** Avoid painting in direct sunlight when the surface temperature exceeds 90 degrees F. Paint dries too fast, causing brush marks and poor leveling. Paint on the shaded side of the house and work with the sun's movement.

**Best painting seasons:** Spring (after the last frost) through early fall (before consistent temperatures drop below 50 degrees). Exact timing depends on your climate zone.

## Making the DIY Decision

DIY exterior painting makes financial sense when:
- Your home is single-story (minimal ladder work)
- The existing paint is in fair to good condition (moderate prep)
- You have 4 to 8 weekends available
- You are comfortable working on a ladder

Hire a professional when:
- Your home is two or more stories (safety risk)
- Lead paint is present or suspected
- The existing paint is in poor condition (extensive prep)
- You need the job done in 1 to 2 weeks, not 2 to 3 months

Either way, do not cut corners on prep. The difference between a 5-year paint job and a 10-year paint job is the 10 to 20 hours spent scraping, sanding, caulking, and priming before the first coat of finish goes on. That prep time --- whether you pay a pro for it or invest your own weekends --- is the best money spent on the entire project.
