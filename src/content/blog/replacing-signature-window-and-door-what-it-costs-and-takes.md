When your home's signature windows and entry doors reach the end of their service life, replacing them together creates an opportunity to improve energy efficiency, curb appeal, and security in a single coordinated project. Tackling windows and doors at the same time also reduces the total cost compared to two separate projects, since you only mobilize one crew and do the finishing work once.

A combined window and door replacement project typically runs $5,000 to $25,000 for a mid-size home, depending on how many openings you are replacing and the quality of materials you choose. A single entry door averages $1,500 to $4,000 installed, while each window runs $300 to $1,500.

## Why Replace Windows and Doors Together

There are practical reasons beyond convenience for bundling these projects.

**Aesthetic consistency.** When you replace windows but keep a 20-year-old front door (or vice versa), the age difference is obvious. Matching frame colors, hardware finishes, and glass packages across all openings creates a unified look that improves curb appeal and, by extension, resale value.

**Energy efficiency gains compound.** Windows and doors are the two biggest sources of air infiltration in most homes. Replacing all of them with modern, properly sealed units can reduce heating and cooling costs by 15% to 30%. Doing only half the openings means you are still losing conditioned air through the remaining old units.

**One project disruption.** Each replacement project involves scheduling, permits, temporary weather exposure, debris, and noise. Combining them means you deal with the disruption once instead of twice.

**Better contractor pricing.** Contractors give better per-unit rates on larger projects. A 15-window-plus-two-doors package will price out 10% to 20% lower per opening than doing each separately.

## Cost Breakdown: Windows

Window costs depend heavily on the frame material and glass package:

| Frame Material | Cost Per Window (Installed) | Maintenance Level |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl | $300 - $800 | Minimal |
| Fiberglass | $500 - $1,200 | Low |
| Wood | $700 - $2,000+ | High (paint/stain every 3-7 years) |
| Aluminum | $350 - $900 | Low |

Glass upgrades add to the base cost. Low-E coating adds $30 to $60 per window. Argon gas fill adds $25 to $50. Triple-pane configurations add $100 to $250. For most climates, double-pane with Low-E and argon provides the best value.

Signature-sized windows — large picture windows, arched units, or specialty shapes — cost 50% to 200% more than standard-sized units of the same material due to custom fabrication.

## Cost Breakdown: Doors

Entry doors vary widely based on material and design:

**Steel entry doors** cost $400 to $1,200 for the door and $300 to $800 for installation. They offer the best security, good insulation (when foam-filled), and low maintenance. The downside: they dent and can rust if the finish is compromised.

**Fiberglass entry doors** run $600 to $2,500 for the door and $300 to $800 for installation. Many come with convincing wood-grain textures that you can stain. They resist warping, rotting, and denting. This is my recommendation for most homeowners — it is the best all-around performer.

**Wood entry doors** cost $800 to $4,000+ for the door and $400 to $1,000 for installation. Nothing matches the look and feel of solid wood, but they demand regular finishing and are prone to warping in humid climates.

**Sliding patio doors** typically cost $800 to $3,000 for the unit and $300 to $700 for installation. French patio doors cost slightly more, ranging from $1,200 to $4,000 installed.

## Planning the Project: Step by Step

### 1. Assess Every Opening

Walk through your home and catalog every window and door. Note the size, condition, and any specific issues. Not every opening needs replacement — some may only need new weatherstripping, hardware, or a glass unit swap. Prioritize the worst performers.

### 2. Set a Realistic Budget

For a typical 3-bedroom home with 12 to 18 windows and 2 to 3 exterior doors, budget:
- **Economy (vinyl windows, steel door):** $6,000 to $12,000
- **Mid-range (vinyl/fiberglass windows, fiberglass door):** $10,000 to $20,000
- **Premium (wood-clad windows, solid wood door):** $18,000 to $35,000+

These ranges include professional installation. DIY installation can save 25% to 40%, but windows and doors must be installed correctly to perform as designed — poor installation voids warranties and causes air and water leaks.

### 3. Choose a Contractor

Get three bids minimum. When comparing, normalize the scope — make sure each bid includes the same materials, trim, flashing, disposal, and permits. Check that the [contractor's agreement covers all the details](/blog/window-company-replacement-full-planning-guide/) before signing.

Verify licensing, insurance, and references. For projects involving doors, confirm the contractor has experience with entry door installation specifically — it requires different skills than window work, particularly around threshold details, weatherstripping, and lockset installation.

### 4. Order Materials and Schedule

Custom windows and doors typically have 3 to 8 week lead times. Stock sizes may be available in 1 to 2 weeks. Your contractor should take final measurements after you sign the contract, not before — measurements taken during the estimate phase are preliminary.

Schedule the work for mild weather. Spring and fall are ideal. Winter installations are possible but cost more due to heating requirements for sealants and the need to minimize the time openings are exposed.

## Installation Day: What to Expect

A typical whole-house project (15 windows, 2 doors) takes 3 to 5 days with a crew of 2 to 3 installers. Here is the general sequence:

**Day 1: Interior prep and door removal.** Furniture moves away from windows. Drop cloths go down. The old entry door comes out, and the new one goes in first — you need a lockable entry point at the end of each work day.

**Days 2-4: Window replacement.** The crew works through the house systematically, typically completing 4 to 6 windows per day. Each window involves removing the old unit, inspecting the rough opening, making repairs if needed, setting the new window, shimming, insulating, and sealing.

**Day 4-5: Exterior trim and finishing.** New trim is installed, all joints are caulked, and exterior surfaces get touch-up paint. Interior trim and casing go on. The crew does a final inspection with you, testing every window and door for proper operation.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**Choosing different contractors for windows and doors.** If one installer does the windows and another does the doors, warranty claims fall into a gray area. The window company blames the door installer and vice versa. Use one contractor for the entire project.

**Ignoring the rough opening.** When old windows and doors come out, the rough opening often reveals hidden water damage, insect damage, or insulation gaps. Budget an extra $1,000 to $3,000 for the total project for these repairs — they are nearly universal in homes over 20 years old.

**Skipping Low-E glass.** On south and west-facing windows, Low-E glass reduces heat gain by 40% to 70%. The $30 to $60 per window upcharge pays for itself in reduced cooling costs within a few years. The [American Wood Council](https://www.awc.org/) and other building standards increasingly recommend or require Low-E in new construction and major renovations.

**Forgetting about door hardware.** Your new entry door may not accommodate your existing deadbolt and lockset without modification. Discuss hardware with your contractor during the planning phase, not on installation day.

## Energy Efficiency and ROI

According to [AARP's livable communities data](https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/housing/), window and door replacement is among the top five home improvements for aging-in-place value. New windows and doors reduce drafts, improve security, and can lower energy bills by $200 to $600 per year for a full-house replacement.

The return on investment at resale is typically 60% to 80% of the project cost for mid-range materials. Premium materials do not proportionally increase resale value — you will recover a larger percentage of a $15,000 mid-range project than a $35,000 premium one.

If budget forces you to choose, [replace windows first](/blog/when-to-replace-signature-window-signs-and-costs/) — there are more of them, and they have a larger combined impact on energy performance. A new entry door improves security and curb appeal but affects only one opening.

## After Installation

Register all manufacturer warranties immediately. Take dated photos of every installed window and door. Keep your contract, warranty cards, and contractor contact information in one file. Schedule a check at the 6-month mark to inspect caulking and weatherstripping, and at the 1-year mark to verify everything is still operating properly.
