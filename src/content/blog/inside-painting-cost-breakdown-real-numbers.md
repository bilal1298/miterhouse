The average inside painting project costs between $1,000 and $5,500 for a professional job on 3 to 5 rooms, with the final number depending on room sizes, wall conditions, paint quality, and your local labor market. I am going to break that down line by line so you can see exactly where the money goes — no vague ranges without context.

## The Full Cost Breakdown for a Typical Project

Let's use a real-world example: painting the main living areas of a 1,800-square-foot home — living room, dining room, kitchen, hallway, and master bedroom. That is roughly 1,600 square feet of paintable wall area after subtracting windows and doors.

| Cost Category | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Paint (walls)** | $280–$480 | 8–10 gallons at $35–$48/gal |
| **Paint (trim/doors)** | $70–$150 | 2–3 gallons semi-gloss |
| **Primer** | $40–$80 | 1–2 gallons for spot priming |
| **Supplies** | $60–$120 | Tape, drop cloths, rollers, brushes, caulk, spackle |
| **Labor (pro)** | $2,400–$4,800 | 2-person crew, 3–5 days |
| **Misc/contingency** | $100–$300 | Unexpected repairs, extra paint |
| **Total (with pro)** | **$2,950–$5,930** | |
| **Total (DIY materials only)** | **$450–$830** | |

The gap between DIY and professional is significant — you save $2,500 to $5,000 by doing the work yourself. But you also invest 60 to 100 hours of your own time, which is the honest trade-off.

## Material Costs: Line by Line

**Paint.** The biggest variable. A 5-gallon bucket of mid-range interior paint (Benjamin Moore Regal, Sherwin-Williams SuperPaint, Behr Ultra) runs $160 to $240. That covers roughly 1,750 to 2,000 square feet in one coat on smooth walls. For two coats on 1,600 square feet, you need approximately 8 to 10 gallons, which is two 5-gallon buckets — $320 to $480.

Going premium (Benjamin Moore Aura, Sherwin-Williams Emerald) raises paint costs by 40 to 60 percent but gets you better coverage, richer colors, and a finish that holds up years longer. For five rooms, that means $450 to $700 in paint alone.

**Primer.** Not every wall needs dedicated primer. You need it for: bare drywall or patches, stains (water marks, crayon, marker, smoke), dramatic color changes (dark to light or vice versa), and surfaces previously painted with oil-based paint. Budget 1 to 2 gallons at $25 to $40 each.

**Trim paint.** Semi-gloss or satin for baseboards, door frames, window casings, and crown molding. You use less volume than wall paint but at a higher cost per gallon (trim paint tends to be premium grade). Two to three gallons covers the trim in a five-room project.

**Consumables.** These are the costs people forget to budget:
- Painter's tape: $6–$8 per roll, you need 4–8 rolls for five rooms ($24–$64)
- Drop cloths: $10–$25 for canvas, $5–$15 for plastic
- Roller covers: $5–$8 each, plan on 3–5 for the whole project
- Sandpaper/sanding sponges: $8–$15
- Spackle/joint compound: $5–$12
- Caulk: $4–$7 per tube, 2–4 tubes ($8–$28)

Total consumables: $60 to $130. These costs are the same whether you hire a pro or DIY — professionals include them in their bid.

## Labor Costs: What You Are Actually Paying For

Professional painters charge $25 to $75 per hour per painter, with most experienced painters in the $40 to $55 range. A two-person crew working 8-hour days at $45 per hour totals $720 per day. A five-room interior project typically takes 3 to 5 days, putting labor at $2,160 to $3,600.

What that labor includes:
- Moving and covering furniture
- Removing and reinstalling outlet covers, switch plates, and hardware
- Cleaning and prepping all surfaces
- Patching small holes and nail pops
- Caulking gaps between trim and walls
- Taping edges
- Priming where necessary
- Two coats of paint on all specified surfaces
- Cleanup and a final walkthrough

What it does not typically include: moving heavy furniture to another room (you are expected to do this before they arrive), extensive drywall repair, wallpaper removal, or painting surfaces not specified in the scope.

## Where Quotes Diverge: Hidden Variables

Two painters quoting the same five rooms can be $2,000 apart, and both quotes might be honest. Here is why:

**Wall condition.** A home with well-maintained walls that just needs a color refresh requires minimal prep. A home with 30 years of nail holes, drywall cracks, and peeling patches needs hours of repair work before any paint goes on. The painter has to account for this in the bid.

**Ceiling inclusion.** Some quotes include ceilings, others do not. Painting ceilings adds 30 to 50 percent to a room's cost because ceiling work is physically harder and slower (gravity is not your friend).

**Trim scope.** Painting all the trim in a room — baseboards, window casings, door frames, crown molding — can double the time compared to walls only. A room with ornate trim molding takes significantly longer than one with flat stock baseboards.

**Number of colors.** One wall color for all five rooms is the simplest and cheapest scenario. Five different colors means five different setup/cleanup cycles, five times the cutting-in precision, and more leftover paint waste.

**Paint quality specified.** A contractor using premium paint builds that cost into the bid. Some cheaper quotes use lower-grade paint without explicitly saying so. Always ask what product they plan to use.

## Real Project Timelines

Understanding the timeline helps you plan for disruption and evaluate whether a contractor's bid is reasonable.

**DIY timeline for 5 rooms (working evenings/weekends):**
- Prep (all rooms): 8–12 hours across 2–3 evenings
- Priming: 3–5 hours
- First coat (all rooms): 10–15 hours across 2 weekends
- Second coat (all rooms): 8–12 hours across 1–2 weekends
- Trim and detail work: 8–12 hours
- Cleanup and touch-ups: 3–5 hours
- **Total: 40–61 hours over 3–5 weeks**

**Professional timeline for 5 rooms (full-time 2-person crew):**
- Day 1: Prep, repairs, priming
- Day 2: First coat on all rooms
- Day 3: Second coat, begin trim
- Day 4: Finish trim, touch-ups, cleanup
- **Total: 3–4 working days**

The professional crew finishes in 4 days what takes a DIYer 4 to 5 weekends. That speed difference is the real product you are buying.

## Cost-Saving Strategies That Work

**Buy during sales.** Major paint brands run sales 4 to 6 times per year, typically around holiday weekends. Savings of $8 to $15 per gallon are common. On a 10-gallon project, that is $80 to $150 saved.

**Choose one color.** A whole-house neutral — warm white, greige, soft gray — that works in every room eliminates waste, allows bulk purchasing, and simplifies both the painting process and future touch-ups.

**Do your own prep.** If hiring a pro, offer to handle all prep work yourself: patching, sanding, cleaning, taping, moving furniture. Some contractors will reduce their bid by 15 to 25 percent if they can skip straight to priming and painting.

**Skip the ceiling if it is in good shape.** If your ceilings are not stained, cracked, or visibly yellowed, you can skip them and save 30 to 50 percent of the project cost. Fresh wall paint next to a clean ceiling looks fine.

**Batch your painting.** Painting five rooms in one project is cheaper per room than painting them one at a time over five years. Contractors give volume discounts, you buy paint in bulk, and you reuse supplies.

## The Bottom Line

The [EPA's RRP rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program) applies to any home built before 1978 — verify your home's age before disturbing any painted surface, whether you are DIYing or hiring a pro. And the [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov/) recommends proper ventilation and safety equipment for all painting projects.

Inside painting is one of the highest-impact, most cost-effective home improvements you can make. A $3,000 professional paint job — or a $500 DIY project — transforms the feel of your home immediately. Unlike a kitchen remodel that takes months and costs five figures, painting delivers visible results in days. Budget carefully, choose quality paint, do not skip prep, and the results will last 7 to 10 years before you need to think about it again.
