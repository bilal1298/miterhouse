When a pipe bursts at 2 a.m. or your toilet overflows onto the bathroom floor, the first 15 minutes determine whether you are dealing with a $300 repair or a $15,000 water damage restoration. Emergency plumbing situations happen to every homeowner eventually, and the single most important thing you can do right now -- before an emergency hits -- is know where your main water shutoff is and how to operate it.

This guide covers the most common plumbing emergencies, what to do immediately, and how to figure out whether you need a middle-of-the-night emergency call or can wait until morning.

## Step One: Shut Off the Water

Every plumbing emergency starts the same way. Stop the water.

**Fixture shutoff valves** are located behind toilets and under sinks. Turn them clockwise to close. If the emergency is isolated to one fixture, this is all you need.

**Main water shutoff** is typically located where the water supply enters your house. In cold climates, it is usually in the basement near the front wall. In warm climates, it may be near the water heater or in a utility closet. Turn the valve clockwise or perpendicular to the pipe to close it.

**Street-side shutoff** is a last resort. This valve is at the meter, usually in a box near the curb. You may need a meter key (a T-shaped tool, $10 at any hardware store) to operate it. Some municipalities consider this their property, but in a genuine emergency, shut it off first and sort out the bureaucracy later.

**Do this right now:** walk to your main shutoff and turn it. Make sure it actually works. Old gate valves can seize, and discovering that during an emergency is a nightmare. If it is stuck, call a plumber to replace it on your schedule, not in a panic.

## The Big Five Emergency Scenarios

### 1. Burst Pipe

**What is happening:** A pipe has split or a joint has failed, and water is spraying or flooding.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Shut off the main water supply
2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain remaining water from the system
3. Turn off electricity to affected areas if water is near outlets or electrical panels
4. Start removing water with towels, a wet/dry vacuum, or a mop

**Can this wait until morning?** Once the water is off, yes. The pipe is not going anywhere. But start drying the area immediately to prevent mold. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours.

**Cost for repair:** $150 to $500 for an accessible burst pipe. Add $500 to $2,000 if the pipe is behind a wall or under a slab.

### 2. Sewage Backup

**What is happening:** Wastewater is coming up through floor drains, toilets, or tub drains, usually starting in the lowest fixtures.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Stop using all water in the house immediately -- every flush and every drain adds to the backup
2. Do not attempt to clean up raw sewage without proper protection (rubber boots, gloves, N95 mask)
3. Open windows for ventilation
4. Keep children and pets away from affected areas

**Can this wait until morning?** If it is a minor backup and you can stop using water, yes. If sewage is actively flooding your home, call an emergency plumber immediately. This is a health hazard.

**Cost for repair:** $200 to $500 for a main line cleaning. $2,000 to $10,000 if the sewer line itself needs repair or replacement.

### 3. Water Heater Failure

**What is happening:** The tank is leaking from the bottom, the relief valve is discharging, or there is no hot water.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Turn off the fuel source (gas valve to "off" or electrical breaker)
2. Shut off the cold water inlet valve at the top of the heater
3. If the tank is actively flooding, connect a hose to the drain valve at the bottom and route it outside or to a floor drain

**Can this wait until morning?** If you have stopped the leak and turned off the fuel, yes. You will just be without hot water. See our detailed guide on [water tank heater installation](/blog/installing-water-tank-heater-what-to-know-first/) for replacement options.

**Cost for repair:** $150 to $400 for valve or element repairs. $800 to $2,500 for full replacement including installation.

### 4. Overflowing Toilet

**What is happening:** The toilet bowl is filling and water is spilling onto the floor.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Remove the tank lid and push the flapper valve down to stop water from entering the bowl
2. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to close it
3. If there is no shutoff valve (older homes), lift the float in the tank to stop the fill valve

**Can this wait until morning?** Once the water stops, absolutely. Use a different bathroom and deal with the clog when you are not in panic mode. A plunger solves most toilet clogs. For stubborn ones, a [toilet auger](/blog/when-to-call-a-plumber-vs-diy/) ($15 to $30) reaches further than a plunger.

**Cost for repair:** Usually $0 to $20 for a DIY fix. $100 to $250 for a plumber.

### 5. Gas Leak (Plumbing-Adjacent)

**What is happening:** You smell rotten eggs near a gas appliance or gas line.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Do NOT flip any electrical switches, light matches, or use your phone inside the house
2. Open doors and windows as you leave
3. Get everyone (including pets) out of the house
4. Call your gas utility's emergency line from outside the house
5. Call 911 if the smell is strong

**Can this wait until morning?** Absolutely not. Gas leaks are an immediate danger. The utility company will respond 24/7 at no charge for the initial safety check.

## When Emergency Rates Are Worth It

Emergency plumber rates run $200 to $500 per hour, roughly double or triple the standard rate. That is a lot of money, and not every situation justifies it.

**Worth the emergency call:**
- Active flooding you cannot stop
- Sewage backing up into living spaces
- Gas leak smell
- No heat in freezing weather (if you have hydronic heating)

**Can wait until regular business hours:**
- Water heater failure (once you have shut off the supply and fuel)
- Single clogged toilet when another bathroom is available
- Dripping faucet or slow leak you have contained with a bucket
- Frozen pipe that has not burst (apply heat gradually with a hair dryer)

The difference between an 11 p.m. emergency call and an 8 a.m. appointment can be $300 to $800 in after-hours charges. If you can safely contain the situation, waiting is the financially smart move.

## Building Your Emergency Plumbing Kit

Keep these items accessible (not buried in the garage):

- **Pipe repair clamps** (two or three sizes): $5 to $15 each
- **Plumber's epoxy putty**: $8
- **Teflon tape**: $3
- **Adjustable wrench** (10-inch): $15
- **Channel-lock pliers**: $15
- **Plunger** (flange-style for toilets, cup-style for sinks): $10 each
- **Wet/dry vacuum**: $60 to $100 (doubles as a shop vac for other projects)
- **Bucket and old towels**: free
- **Flashlight with fresh batteries**: $10
- **Main shutoff valve key** (if yours is a curb-side meter): $10

For around $150, you are prepared to contain most plumbing emergencies until professional help arrives.

## After the Emergency: Next Steps

Once the immediate crisis is handled, document everything for insurance purposes. Take photos and videos of the damage, the source of the problem, and any repairs made. Keep receipts for everything.

Contact your homeowner's insurance company within 24 hours if there is significant water damage. Most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from burst pipes but do not cover gradual leaks or [sewer backups](/blog/plumbing-pipe-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/) unless you have specific endorsements.

For plumbing emergencies that involve your home's [water supply lines](/blog/plumbing-pipes-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/) or that comply with [building codes](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/), always verify the permanent repair is done to code. Emergency patches are temporary. Schedule a proper repair and, if applicable, get the required permits and inspections.

The best emergency plan is one you hope you never need. But when that pipe does burst at 2 a.m., you will be glad you knew exactly where the shutoff was and had the right supplies on hand.
