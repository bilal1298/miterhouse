Most homeowners facing a burst pipe at 2 a.m. do the worst possible thing: they panic, grab towels, and spend 10 minutes mopping while hundreds of gallons continue pouring in. The difference between a $300 repair and a $15,000 water damage restoration comes down to how quickly you stop the water — not how fast you clean it up. And the single most important thing you can do right now, before any emergency, is walk to your main water shutoff and test it.

## The Problem: You're Fighting Water You Should Be Stopping

Every minute a burst pipe runs, it dumps 4–8 gallons of water into your home. In 15 minutes of panicked towel-grabbing, that's 60–120 gallons soaking into subfloors, drywall, and insulation. Mold starts growing in 24–48 hours. But if you shut the water off in the first 2 minutes, you're dealing with a puddle and a plumber — not a restoration crew.

The solution is always the same first step, regardless of the emergency type.

## The Fix: Shut Off the Water First, Every Time

**Fixture shutoff valves** sit behind toilets and under sinks. Turn clockwise to close. If the emergency is isolated to one fixture, this is all you need.

**Main water shutoff** is where the supply enters your house. In cold climates, it's usually in the basement near the front wall. In warm climates, look near the water heater or in a utility closet. Turn the valve clockwise or perpendicular to the pipe.

**Street-side shutoff** is the last resort — at the meter, in a box near the curb. You may need a meter key ($10 at any hardware store). Some municipalities consider this their property, but in a real emergency, shut it off and sort out the paperwork later.

**Do this right now.** Walk to your main shutoff valve and turn it. Make sure it actually works. Old gate valves seize from years of non-use, and discovering that during a flood is a nightmare. If it's stuck, call a plumber to replace it this week — on your schedule, not in a panic.

## The Five Emergencies and How to Handle Each

### Burst Pipe

**What's happening:** A pipe has split or a joint has failed. Water is spraying or flooding.

**Your response:**
1. Shut off the main water supply
2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain remaining water from the pipes
3. Turn off electricity to affected areas if water is near outlets or the electrical panel
4. Start removing standing water with towels, a wet/dry vacuum, or a mop

**Can it wait until morning?** Once the water is off, yes. The pipe isn't going anywhere. But start drying the area immediately — mold begins growing in 24–48 hours.

**Repair cost:** $150–$500 for an accessible burst pipe. Add $500–$2,000 if it's behind a wall or under a slab.

### Sewage Backup

**What's happening:** Wastewater is coming up through floor drains, toilets, or tub drains, starting at the lowest fixtures.

**Your response:**
1. Stop using all water in the house — every flush and every drain adds to the backup
2. Don't attempt cleanup without rubber boots, gloves, and an N95 mask
3. Open windows for ventilation
4. Keep children and pets away from affected areas

**Can it wait until morning?** If it's a minor backup and you can stop using water, yes. If sewage is actively flooding living spaces, call an emergency plumber now. Raw sewage is a health hazard.

**Repair cost:** $200–$500 for a main line cleaning. $2,000–$10,000 if the [sewer line needs repair or replacement](/blog/plumbing-pipe-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/).

### Water Heater Failure

**What's happening:** The tank is leaking from the bottom, the relief valve is discharging, or there's no hot water.

**Your response:**
1. Turn off the fuel source — gas valve to "off" or flip the electrical breaker
2. Shut off the cold water inlet valve at the top of the heater
3. If the tank is actively flooding, connect a hose to the drain valve at the bottom and route it outside or to a floor drain

**Can it wait until morning?** Once you've stopped the leak and killed the fuel, yes. You'll just be without hot water. See our guide on [water heater installation](/blog/installing-tank-water-heater-what-to-know-first/) for replacement options.

**Repair cost:** $150–$400 for valve or element repairs. $800–$2,500 for full replacement with installation.

### Overflowing Toilet

**What's happening:** The bowl is filling and water is spilling onto the floor.

**Your response:**
1. Remove the tank lid and push the flapper valve down to stop water entering the bowl
2. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise
3. If there's no shutoff valve (common in older homes), lift the float in the tank to stop the fill valve

**Can it wait until morning?** Once the water stops, absolutely. Use another bathroom and deal with the clog when you're calm. A [plunger](/blog/when-to-call-a-plumber-vs-diy/) solves most toilet clogs. For stubborn ones, a toilet auger ($15–$30) reaches further.

**Repair cost:** $0–$20 for a DIY fix. $100–$250 for a plumber.

### Gas Leak (Plumbing-Adjacent)

**What's happening:** You smell rotten eggs near a gas appliance or gas line.

**Your response:**
1. Do NOT flip any electrical switches, light matches, or use your phone inside the house
2. Open doors and windows as you leave
3. Get everyone — including pets — out of the house
4. Call your gas utility's emergency line from outside
5. Call 911 if the smell is strong

**Can it wait until morning?** Absolutely not. Gas leaks are immediate danger. The utility company responds 24/7 at no charge for the initial safety check.

## When Emergency Rates Are Worth the Money

Emergency plumber rates run $200–$500 per hour — double or triple the standard rate. Not every situation justifies that premium.

**Worth the emergency call:**
- Active flooding you can't stop
- Sewage backing into living spaces
- Gas leak smell
- No heat in freezing weather (if you have hydronic heating)

**Can wait for business hours:**
- Water heater failure (once supply and fuel are shut off)
- Single clogged toilet when another bathroom is available
- Dripping faucet or slow leak contained with a bucket
- Frozen pipe that hasn't burst (apply heat gradually with a hair dryer — never a torch)

The difference between an 11 p.m. emergency call and an 8 a.m. appointment saves $300–$800 in after-hours charges. If you can safely contain the situation, waiting is the smart move.

## Build Your Emergency Kit Before You Need It

Keep these accessible — not buried in the back of the garage:

- **Pipe repair clamps** (2–3 sizes): $5–$15 each
- **Plumber's epoxy putty**: $8
- **Teflon tape**: $3
- **Adjustable wrench** (10-inch): $15
- **Channel-lock pliers**: $15
- **Plunger** — flange-style for toilets, cup-style for sinks: $10 each
- **Wet/dry vacuum**: $60–$100 (doubles as a shop vac for other projects)
- **Bucket and old towels**: free
- **Flashlight with fresh batteries**: $10
- **Main shutoff valve key** (if yours is curb-side): $10

Total: about $150 for a kit that handles most emergencies until a pro arrives.

## After the Emergency

Once the immediate crisis is handled, document everything for insurance. Photos and video of the damage, the source of the problem, and any repairs you made. Keep every receipt.

Contact your homeowner's insurance within 24 hours if there's significant water damage. Most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from burst pipes but don't cover gradual leaks or sewer backups unless you have specific endorsements.

For any emergency involving your home's [water supply lines](/blog/plumbing-pipe-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/) or work that must comply with [building codes](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/), verify the permanent repair is done to code. Emergency patches are temporary. Schedule a proper repair and, if needed, get the permits and inspections.

The best emergency plan is one you never use. But when that pipe does burst at 2 a.m., you'll be glad you knew exactly where the shutoff was and had the right supplies within reach.
