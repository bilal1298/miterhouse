Moisture-damaged laminate flooring cannot be repaired — it must be replaced. Once water reaches the fiberboard core of laminate planks, they swell, warp, and delaminate permanently. Replacing moisture-damaged laminate costs $3 to $10 per square foot for materials and installation, but the real expense is often the detective work: finding and fixing the moisture source before you lay down new flooring that will suffer the same fate.

If your laminate is buckling, swelling at the joints, or showing soft spots, here is what is actually happening and what to do about it.

## How Moisture Destroys Laminate

Laminate flooring is built in layers: a decorative photograph layer on top, a clear wear layer above that, and a fiberboard core (HDF or MDF) that gives the plank its structure. That fiberboard core is essentially compressed wood fiber — and wood fiber absorbs water aggressively.

When moisture reaches the core:

**Stage 1 — Edge swelling.** Water enters through the click-lock joints, and the edges of adjacent planks swell upward, creating visible ridges at every seam. This happens with as little as a persistent spill that sits for a few hours.

**Stage 2 — Buckling.** Extended moisture exposure causes planks to expand beyond the room's expansion gap, and the floor buckles upward — lifting off the subfloor in visible humps. This happens with sustained humidity, a slow leak, or a subfloor moisture problem.

**Stage 3 — Delamination.** The decorative surface layer separates from the core. The plank feels soft, spongy, or crumbly when pressed. This is the point of no return — the plank is structurally failed.

**Stage 4 — Mold growth.** Moisture trapped between the laminate and the subfloor creates ideal conditions for mold, especially if the problem goes undetected for weeks or months. You may notice a musty smell before you see visible damage.

## Identifying the Moisture Source

Before replacing a single plank, identify and fix the water source. The most common culprits:

### Subfloor Moisture (Concrete Slabs)

Concrete is porous. Moisture vapor from the ground can migrate up through a concrete slab and accumulate beneath the laminate. This is especially common in basements, ground-floor slabs without a vapor barrier, and homes in areas with high water tables.

**How to test:** Tape a 2x2-foot piece of clear plastic to the concrete. Check after 48 hours. If condensation forms under the plastic, the slab is emitting excessive moisture. A more precise test uses a calcium chloride moisture test kit ($20 to $30 per test).

**The fix:** A vapor barrier (6-mil polyethylene sheeting) laid over the concrete before installing flooring. Most laminate manufacturers require this — installing without it voids the warranty.

### Plumbing Leaks

A slow leak under a sink, dishwasher, or water heater can saturate laminate from below without any visible surface water. Check for:
- Discoloration or warping localized near plumbing fixtures
- Musty odor near kitchen or bathroom cabinets
- Unexplained increases in water bills

### Appliance Failures

Dishwashers, washing machines, refrigerators with ice makers, and water heaters are all common sources of sudden or gradual water release onto laminate floors.

### High Humidity

In humid climates or poorly ventilated spaces, ambient moisture alone can cause laminate damage over time. Relative humidity consistently above 60% will gradually swell laminate edges and promote mold growth beneath the flooring.

### Surface Water

The most obvious source: spills, pet accidents, mop water, and tracked-in rain that sit on the surface long enough to seep through the joints. Laminate's click-lock joints are not watertight — any water that stays on the surface for more than 20 to 30 minutes can penetrate.

## Assessing the Damage

Pull up a damaged plank and inspect both the plank underside and the subfloor beneath it.

**Plank condition:**
- If the core is swollen but intact, the plank is damaged but the problem may be recent and contained
- If the core is soft, crumbly, or has visible mold, the damage is advanced
- Check adjacent planks that look fine on top — they may be damaged underneath

**Subfloor condition:**
- Dry, clean subfloor: The moisture event was brief or surface-only
- Damp subfloor with no mold: The moisture event was moderate — dry the subfloor completely before reinstalling
- Moldy or rotted subfloor: The moisture event was prolonged or ongoing — subfloor repair is needed before any new flooring goes down

If you find mold on the subfloor, check the [EPA's mold cleanup guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/lead) for proper handling. Small areas (under 10 square feet) can be cleaned with detergent solution. Larger areas may need professional remediation.

## Repair Options

### Replacing Individual Planks

If the damage is limited to a few planks in one area, you can replace just those planks — assuming you have matching material. This is one of laminate's advantages over [sheet vinyl](/blog/install-vinyl-flooring-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/): individual pieces can be swapped.

The process:
1. Remove baseboards along the nearest wall
2. Disassemble planks row by row from that wall to the damaged area
3. Replace damaged planks with new ones
4. Reassemble the floor back to the wall
5. Reinstall baseboards

This works well for damage near a wall. For damage in the middle of a large room, disassembly from the wall means removing dozens of planks to reach the affected area — tedious but doable.

**Alternative for mid-room repairs:** Cut out the damaged plank(s) using a circular saw set to the laminate thickness. Remove the pieces, clean the subfloor, and install new planks by cutting the locking tongue off the long side and gluing in place. This is a permanent repair that avoids disassembling the entire floor but loses the click-lock connection.

### Full Room Replacement

If damage extends across a large area, or if the subfloor requires extensive work, replacing the entire room's flooring is often more practical and produces a better result.

**Cost to replace a full room:**
- Materials (laminate + underlayment): $1.50 to $4/sf
- Old flooring removal: $0.50 to $1.50/sf
- Subfloor prep: $0.50 to $3/sf (depending on condition)
- Installation labor: $1.50 to $3/sf
- Total: $4 to $11.50/sf

For a 200-square-foot room, that is $800 to $2,300.

### Switching to a Water-Resistant Product

If moisture was the problem, consider whether laminate is the right replacement. Waterproof luxury vinyl plank (LVP) costs similarly to mid-range laminate and handles moisture infinitely better.

For rooms with known moisture risks — basements, kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms — LVP is the smarter choice. The [installation costs for vinyl flooring](/blog/install-vinyl-flooring-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/) are comparable, and you eliminate the risk of repeating the same moisture damage cycle.

Some manufacturers now sell "waterproof laminate" with plastic or stone composite cores instead of fiberboard. These products cost $3 to $6 per square foot and offer genuine moisture resistance while maintaining the look and feel of traditional laminate. They are worth the premium for any room with moisture exposure.

## Prevention Going Forward

### During Installation

- Always install a vapor barrier over concrete subfloors (6-mil poly or the underlayment product specified by the manufacturer)
- Leave the required expansion gap (typically 1/4 to 3/8 inch) around all walls and fixed objects — a constrained floor buckles under even minor moisture expansion
- Acclimate the laminate in the room for 48 hours before installation
- Do not install laminate in rooms with floor drains, sump pits, or active moisture problems

### During Daily Use

- Wipe up spills immediately — 30 seconds of attention now prevents $2,000 in replacement later
- Use damp mops, never wet mops. Ring the mop until it is barely damp. Never pour water on laminate to clean it.
- Place mats at exterior doors to catch tracked-in moisture
- Run exhaust fans in kitchens and bathrooms during and after water use
- Check under sinks, behind refrigerators, and around dishwashers monthly for signs of slow leaks

### Environmental Controls

- Maintain indoor relative humidity between 35% and 55% year-round
- Use a dehumidifier in basements and poorly ventilated spaces
- Ensure bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans vent to the outside, not into the attic or crawl space
- Address any standing water or grading issues around the foundation that could create [basement moisture problems](/blog/install-vinyl-flooring-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/)

The [TCNA (Tile Council of North America)](https://www.tcnatile.com/) and various flooring industry groups publish moisture testing standards that apply to all flooring types. Following these standards — particularly moisture testing before installation — prevents the vast majority of moisture-related flooring failures.

Laminate flooring and moisture are fundamentally incompatible. Understanding this limitation, preventing exposure, and acting quickly when water events occur are the keys to getting a full lifespan from your laminate floor. And when moisture damage does occur, treat it as a signal to fix the underlying water problem — not just a flooring replacement project.
