Sewer plumbing repair is one of the most expensive and disruptive home repairs you can face, ranging from a $200 drain cleaning to a $15,000 full line replacement. The good news is that a $150 to $300 camera inspection will tell you exactly what you are dealing with before anyone starts digging. Never authorize major sewer work without one.

This guide covers the common types of sewer problems, what each repair costs, and how to avoid paying more than you should.

## How Your Sewer System Works

Your home's drain-waste-vent (DWV) system collects wastewater from every fixture and routes it through progressively larger pipes to a main sewer line. That main line exits your home through the foundation and connects to either the municipal sewer or a private septic system.

The main sewer line is typically 4 inches in diameter, made of PVC (homes built after 1980), cast iron (1950s to 1980s), or clay tile (pre-1960s). The line runs underground from your foundation to the street connection or septic tank, usually at a slope of 1/4 inch per foot.

Everything upstream of the connection to the municipal line (or to your septic system) is your responsibility as the homeowner. This includes the lateral line from your house to the street.

## Common Sewer Problems

### Tree Root Intrusion

This is the single most common sewer line problem. Roots seek moisture and nutrients, and your sewer line provides both. Roots enter through joints, small cracks, and even the porous walls of older clay pipes. Once inside, they grow into a dense mat that catches debris and eventually blocks the pipe.

**Symptoms:** Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling sounds from drains, sewage odor in the yard.

**Repair cost:** $200 to $600 for mechanical root cutting (a temporary fix that needs repeating every 1 to 3 years). $4,000 to $8,000 for pipe lining that permanently seals the pipe. $5,000 to $15,000 for pipe replacement.

### Bellied or Sagging Pipe

Ground movement, settling, or improper initial installation can cause a section of pipe to sag. The low point collects waste and water, creating a recurring blockage.

**Symptoms:** Frequent backups in the same location, slow drains that repeatedly clog despite cleaning.

**Repair cost:** $1,500 to $5,000 depending on depth and location. This requires excavation to the affected section.

### Offset Joints

As the ground settles over decades, pipe sections can shift out of alignment at the joints. Even a small offset catches debris and eventually causes blockages.

**Symptoms:** Similar to a bellied pipe -- recurring clogs and slow drainage.

**Repair cost:** $1,000 to $4,000 for spot repair. Pipe lining can sometimes bridge small offsets without excavation.

### Corroded or Collapsed Pipe

Cast iron pipes eventually corrode from the inside out. Clay pipes crack under ground pressure or root forces. When the pipe structure fails, soil enters the line and creates a blockage that no amount of snaking will fix.

**Symptoms:** Complete drain failure, sewage in the yard, sinkholes near the sewer line path.

**Repair cost:** $3,000 to $15,000 for partial or full replacement.

## The Camera Inspection: Your First Step

Before authorizing any sewer repair beyond a basic drain cleaning, insist on a camera inspection. A plumber feeds a waterproof camera through the sewer line, recording video of the pipe's interior condition. This reveals:

- The exact location of the problem (measured from the cleanout)
- The type of problem (roots, belly, offset, crack, collapse)
- The pipe material and general condition
- Whether the problem is isolated or systemic

A camera inspection costs $150 to $300. It is the single best investment you can make when facing sewer problems, because it prevents you from guessing about the scope and lets you get accurate quotes based on a known condition.

**Tip:** Ask for a copy of the video. Any reputable plumber will provide one. If they will not, that is suspicious.

## Repair Methods

### Hydro-Jetting

A high-pressure water nozzle (3,000 to 4,000 psi) scours the inside of the pipe, cutting through roots and flushing debris. This is the most effective cleaning method and is far superior to a mechanical snake for sewer lines.

**Cost:** $300 to $600. Some plumbers charge more for first-time cleaning when heavy buildup is present.

**Best for:** Root intrusion (as maintenance), grease buildup, general cleaning.

**Limitations:** Does not fix structural problems. If the pipe is cracked, collapsed, or has significant offsets, jetting will not help and may make things worse.

### Pipe Lining (CIPP - Cured-in-Place Pipe)

A flexible liner coated with epoxy resin is inserted into the existing pipe and inflated. The resin cures, creating a new pipe inside the old one. This eliminates root intrusion, seals cracks, and bridges minor offsets without excavation.

**Cost:** $4,000 to $8,000 for a typical residential lateral (40 to 80 feet).

**Best for:** Root intrusion, cracked pipes, minor offsets, corroded cast iron.

**Limitations:** Cannot fix bellied pipes (the liner follows the existing pipe shape). The pipe diameter is reduced by about 1/4 inch, which is insignificant for 4-inch sewer lines but may matter for smaller branch lines.

### Pipe Bursting

A bursting head is pulled through the old pipe, shattering it outward while simultaneously pulling a new HDPE pipe into place. This replaces the pipe without excavating the full trench.

**Cost:** $4,000 to $10,000.

**Best for:** Severely damaged pipes where lining is not feasible. Upgrading from smaller to larger pipe diameter.

**Limitations:** Requires access pits at each end. May not work if the pipe has severe offsets or if large trees or structures are directly above the line.

### Traditional Excavation and Replacement

Dig up the old pipe, remove it, and install new PVC. This is the most invasive but also the most complete solution.

**Cost:** $5,000 to $15,000 depending on depth (3 to 10 feet is typical), length, soil conditions, and what is above the line (driveway, landscaping, sidewalk).

**Best for:** Bellied pipes, collapsed sections, and situations where the line needs to be re-graded for proper slope.

**Recovery time:** Expect 1 to 3 days for the repair itself, plus landscaping restoration that can take weeks.

## Permits and Code Compliance

Sewer line repair almost always requires a permit. The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) and your local building department govern sewer work. Typical requirements include:

- Minimum pipe slope (1/8 inch per foot minimum, 1/4 inch per foot preferred)
- Approved pipe materials (PVC Schedule 40 for most residential applications)
- Proper backfill materials and compaction
- Cleanout access within 5 feet of the building
- Inspection before backfill (the inspector needs to see the pipe before you cover it)

A permit costs $75 to $250 and includes one or two inspections. Skipping the permit creates problems at resale and may void your insurance coverage.

## How to Avoid Getting Overcharged

Sewer repair is a high-ticket item, and unfortunately, some companies take advantage of panicking homeowners. Protect yourself:

1. **Never authorize major work based on a snake operator's diagnosis.** A plumber running a snake cannot see inside the pipe. Camera inspection first.
2. **Get three quotes** from different companies. Provide each with the camera inspection video so they are quoting on the same information.
3. **Ask specifically about lining and pipe bursting** as alternatives to excavation. Not all companies offer trenchless methods, and some will default to excavation because that is what they do.
4. **Verify the company's license** with your state licensing board.
5. **Ask about warranty.** Pipe lining typically carries a 10 to 50-year warranty. Traditional replacement should be warranted for at least 5 years on labor.

Health and safety are serious concerns during sewer work. The [EPA's WaterSense program](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) addresses water quality standards, and [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/) sets safety standards for excavation work. Confirm your contractor follows these requirements, especially for deep trenches.

## Maintenance to Prevent Future Problems

After a sewer repair, maintenance prevents repeat issues:

- **Camera inspection every 2 to 3 years** for older pipes or properties with trees near the line ($150 to $300)
- **Hydro-jetting every 2 to 3 years** if root intrusion is a recurring issue ($300 to $600)
- **Root barrier treatment** with copper sulfate or foaming root killer annually ($15 to $30 per application)
- **Do not plant trees within 10 feet of the sewer line.** If existing trees are near the line, root barriers ($500 to $1,000 installed) can redirect growth.
- **Do not flush anything but toilet paper.** So-called "flushable" wipes do not break down and are a leading cause of sewer blockages.
