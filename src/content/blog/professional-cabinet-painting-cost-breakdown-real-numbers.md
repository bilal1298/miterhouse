Professional cabinet painting costs $3,500 to $8,000 for a full kitchen, with most homeowners landing around $4,500 to $6,000 for a 25-door kitchen. That is a fraction of the $15,000 to $35,000 you would spend on new cabinet refacing or $25,000+ for replacement — and when done right, the results look nearly indistinguishable from a factory finish.

The price gap between a good professional job and a great one comes down to process: where the doors are painted, what products are used, and how many coats go on. This breakdown covers exactly what you should expect at each price point.

## Price Tiers Explained

### Budget Professional ($2,000–$3,500)

At this level, you are typically hiring a general painter who does cabinet work as part of a broader painting business. The work happens on-site in your kitchen.

What you get:
- Doors removed, sanded, and primed in your garage or driveway
- Boxes sanded and primed in the kitchen
- Brushed and rolled finish (sometimes light spraying on doors)
- Two coats of paint
- Basic cleanup and reinstallation

What you do not get:
- Dust-free finish environment
- Factory-smooth spray finish
- Extended warranty on the finish

This tier works fine for rental properties, guest spaces, or rooms where a perfect finish is less critical. The results are clearly better than most DIY jobs but will show brush strokes on close inspection.

### Mid-Range Professional ($3,500–$6,500)

The most common tier. You are hiring a dedicated cabinet painter or a painting company with a cabinet division. Doors are usually sprayed off-site or in a portable spray setup.

What you get:
- Full removal, labeling, and documentation of all doors and hardware
- Professional degreasing and sanding
- Shop-quality primer and two coats of high-performance paint (typically sprayed)
- Cabinet boxes brushed and rolled or lightly sprayed on-site
- New hardware installation if you provide it
- Touch-up visit 2 to 4 weeks after completion
- 2- to 5-year warranty on peeling and chipping

This is the sweet spot for most homeowners. The finish is smooth and durable, the process is managed for you, and the warranty provides peace of mind.

### Premium Professional ($6,500–$12,000+)

At the high end, you are paying for shop-quality work on every surface. Dedicated cabinet refinishing shops handle this work.

What you get:
- All pieces transported to a professional spray booth
- Chemical stripping if needed (for previously painted or damaged surfaces)
- Catalyzed lacquer or conversion varnish finish — the hardest, most durable coating available
- Factory-smooth finish with zero texture
- Color matching to any sample you provide
- 5- to 10-year warranty
- Full reinstallation including hinge adjustment and soft-close conversions

This tier makes sense for high-end kitchens with quality wood cabinets worth preserving, or when the alternative is $25,000+ in new cabinets and the homeowner wants a premium look.

## What Professional Painters Actually Charge For

Understanding the cost components helps you evaluate quotes:

**Labor (60%–65% of total):** The single biggest cost. A professional crew spends 40 to 80 hours on a typical kitchen including prep, painting, and installation. At shop rates of $50 to $75 per hour, that is $2,000 to $6,000 in labor alone.

**Materials (15%–20%):** Primer, paint, sandpaper, tape, caulk, and cleaning supplies. Professional-grade products cost more than consumer versions — a gallon of conversion varnish runs $120 compared to $50 for consumer cabinet paint. Material cost for a full kitchen: $300 to $800.

**Overhead and profit (15%–25%):** Business expenses, insurance, equipment maintenance (spray equipment alone costs $2,000 to $10,000), vehicle costs for door transport, and profit margin.

## The Professional Process in Detail

### Consultation and Quote

A reputable cabinet painter will visit your home to assess the cabinets before quoting. They are looking at:

- Cabinet material (solid wood, MDF, plywood, thermofoil, laminate)
- Current finish condition (intact, peeling, damaged)
- Number of pieces
- Complexity (glass inserts, decorative molding, specialty hardware)
- Kitchen layout and access

Be wary of quotes given over the phone or based on photos alone. Cabinet painting requires hands-on assessment of the surface condition.

### Day 1: Removal and Prep

The crew arrives, photographs the kitchen for reference, numbers every door and drawer with a labeling system, and removes all pieces along with hinges and hardware. Doors leave your kitchen and go to the shop.

On-site, the crew begins prepping the cabinet boxes — degreasing, sanding, and masking off countertops, walls, appliances, and flooring.

### Days 2–3: Shop Work (Doors and Drawers)

In the shop, doors and drawer fronts go through a thorough prep sequence:

1. Cleaning with a commercial degreaser
2. Sanding with 150-grit, then 220-grit
3. Any repairs (filling dings, smoothing edges)
4. Primer coat, sprayed
5. Light sand between primer and first coat
6. First finish coat, sprayed
7. Light sand or scuff between coats
8. Final finish coat, sprayed
9. Inspection and touch-up as needed

Each step requires drying time, so the shop work typically spans 3 to 5 business days.

### Days 2–3: On-Site Work (Boxes)

While doors are at the shop, the on-site crew primes and paints the cabinet boxes. Boxes get sprayed if conditions allow (good ventilation, ability to mask thoroughly) or brushed and rolled.

Interior cabinet surfaces are usually painted at the front edge only — the first 2 to 3 inches visible when the door is open. Full interior painting is available but costs an additional 30% to 40%.

### Day 4–5: Reinstallation

Finished doors return from the shop. The crew reinstalls each piece using their labeling system, adjusts hinges for proper alignment, and installs new hardware if provided. A final walkthrough with the homeowner identifies any touch-ups needed.

### Touch-Up Visit (2–4 Weeks Later)

Paint continues to cure for 2 to 4 weeks after application. A good cabinet painter schedules a return visit to address any issues that appear during the cure period — minor chips from reinstallation, spots where the paint did not bond properly, or areas that need an additional coat.

## Red Flags When Hiring

**No in-home consultation.** If they quote without seeing the cabinets, they will either surprise you with upcharges or cut corners on prep.

**Vague product descriptions.** "We use professional-grade paint" means nothing. You should know the specific primer, the specific paint product, and how many coats of each.

**No warranty.** Any painter confident in their work offers at least a 2-year warranty against peeling, chipping, and adhesion failure. No warranty suggests they know the work will not hold up.

**Very fast timelines.** If someone promises your kitchen in 2 days, they are skipping critical cure time between coats. Proper cabinet painting takes 5 to 10 business days from removal to reinstallation.

**Much lower price than competitors.** If three quotes come in at $4,000–$5,500 and one comes in at $2,000, that painter is cutting corners somewhere — cheaper paint, fewer coats, skipping primer, or not properly prepping the surface.

## Professional Painting vs. Other Cabinet Updates

| Option | Cost Range | Timeline | Result Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professional painting | $3,500–$8,000 | 1–2 weeks | Excellent |
| DIY painting | $200–$500 | 3–5 weeks | Variable |
| Refacing (new doors + veneer) | $8,000–$15,000 | 1–2 weeks | Excellent |
| New doors only (painting boxes) | $3,000–$6,000 | 2–3 weeks | Good |
| Full replacement | $12,000–$35,000+ | 3–6 weeks | New |

Professional painting offers the best value when your cabinet boxes are in good shape and you want a dramatic visual change without structural work. If the boxes themselves are damaged, warped, or poorly configured, [refacing or replacement](/blog/federal-way-kitchen-remodel-what-homeowners-should-know/) becomes the better investment.

## Getting and Comparing Quotes

Request quotes from at least three cabinet painting specialists (not general painters). For each quote, confirm:

1. **Product names and coats** — exactly what primer and paint, how many coats of each
2. **Spray vs. brush** — for both doors and boxes
3. **Shop vs. on-site** — where doors are painted
4. **What is included** — hardware removal/reinstall, touch-up visit, new hardware holes
5. **What is excluded** — interior painting, hardware cost, repairs to damaged surfaces
6. **Warranty** — length and what it covers
7. **Timeline** — start to reinstallation, including cure time
8. **Payment schedule** — avoid paying more than 30% upfront

Visit their shop if possible. Look at doors in progress. A clean, organized spray booth with good lighting produces better results than a cluttered garage.

The investment in professional cabinet painting typically recoupes well. Updated cabinets are one of the first things buyers notice, and a quality paint job on solid cabinets is often more appealing than cheap new cabinets — because buyers can tell the difference.
