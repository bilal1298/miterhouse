Installing a pet fence in 2026 ranges from $200 for a basic DIY underground system to $15,000 or more for professional physical fencing around a large yard. The right option for your situation depends on your dog's size, temperament, your property layout, and local regulations. Here is an honest comparison of every major pet fence type with current pricing.

## Underground Pet Fences

Underground systems remain the most cost-effective option for pet containment per square foot of coverage. The technology has matured significantly, with better collar reliability, adjustable correction levels, and improved wire durability.

**What is new in 2026:** GPS-based wireless fences have improved enough to be viable alternatives to buried wire. Systems like SpotOn and Halo create virtual boundaries using GPS and cellular connectivity. They cost $400 to $700 for the collar (no installation needed) but have monthly subscription fees of $5 to $10. Accuracy is within 3 to 5 feet, which is good enough for most properties but not as precise as a buried wire system.

**Traditional underground systems** still offer the best precision and lowest long-term cost. ## Physical Fence Options for Pets

Physical fences provide containment plus security --- they keep your pets in and other animals out. For dogs that are climbers, jumpers, or diggers, a physical fence may be the only reliable option.

### Chain Link

The workhorse of pet fencing. Affordable, durable, and effective. A 4-foot chain link fence is adequate for most small to medium dogs. Large dogs or jumpers need 6 feet.

**2026 pricing:** $8 to $15 per linear foot installed (4 ft height), $12 to $22 per linear foot for 6 ft. Material costs for DIY: $4 to $8 per linear foot.

**Pros:** Cheapest physical fence, lasts 20+ years with no maintenance, see-through design does not make the yard feel small.

**Cons:** Not the best looking, dogs can see through it (which triggers some dogs to bark at passersby), determined dogs can climb it.

**Pet-specific modifications:** Add a lean-in extension at the top for climbers ($2 to $4 per foot). Add a concrete footer or bury the bottom 6 inches for diggers ($3 to $6 per foot extra).

### Wood Fencing

Wood provides a solid visual barrier, which calms dogs that are triggered by visual stimuli. A 6-foot wood privacy fence is the gold standard for pet containment because dogs cannot see through it, climb it easily, or squeeze through gaps.

**2026 pricing:** $15 to $30 per linear foot installed for pressure-treated pine privacy fence. [Cedar fencing](/blog/cedar-fence-installation-guide-diy-or-hire-out/) runs $20 to $40 per linear foot.

**Pros:** Complete visual barrier, good for reactive dogs, adds privacy for the homeowner, wide style variety.

**Cons:** Requires regular staining or sealing (every 2 to 3 years), can rot at ground contact, gaps develop as boards shrink.

**Pet-specific tip:** Dogs chew pressure-treated lumber, and the chemicals in it are not something you want in your dog. If your dog chews wood, go with cedar (naturally rot-resistant and non-toxic) or add a wire mesh on the interior side at dog height.

### Vinyl Fencing

Vinyl privacy fencing offers the look of wood with far less maintenance. No painting, no staining, no rot.

**2026 pricing:** $20 to $40 per linear foot installed. Premium brands with wind-rated engineering run higher.

**Pros:** Zero maintenance, long lifespan (20-30 years), consistent appearance, no splinters or toxic treatments.

**Cons:** Expensive upfront, limited color options, can crack in extreme cold, difficult to repair (whole panels must be replaced).

### Aluminum and Wrought Iron

Ornamental metal fencing looks sharp but has gaps between pickets. Good for non-climbing dogs, but smaller dogs may squeeze through standard spacing.

**2026 pricing:** $25 to $50 per linear foot installed. [Wrought iron options](/blog/wrought-iron-fence-installation-costs-and-tips/) are at the top of this range.

**Pet-specific consideration:** Standard picket spacing is 3.5 to 4 inches. Small dogs or puppies can fit through this. Specify closer spacing (2.5 to 3 inches) if you have small breeds, which adds 15 to 25 percent to the cost.

## Permit Requirements in 2026

Permit rules for fencing vary significantly by municipality, and they are getting more specific:

- **Physical fences over 4 feet** typically require a permit ($50 to $200)
- **Fences near property lines** must meet setback requirements (usually 2 to 6 inches from the property line)
- **Underground electronic fences** generally do not require a permit in most jurisdictions
- **HOA restrictions** may limit fence height, style, material, and color regardless of municipal code

Check the [International Residential Code](https://www.strongtie.com/) for baseline requirements and your local building department for specific regulations.

Always verify the property line before building a physical fence. A survey costs $300 to $600 but prevents the far more expensive problem of building on a neighbor's property.

## Choosing the Right Pet Fence

The decision matrix is simpler than it seems.

**Choose an underground fence if:** Your lot is large (savings increase with property size), your HOA prohibits or restricts physical fences, your dog is well-trainable, and you do not need to keep wildlife or other animals out.

**Choose a physical fence if:** Your dog is a climber, jumper, or escape artist, you have wildlife concerns (coyotes, stray dogs), you want dual-purpose containment and privacy, or your dog does not respond well to collar corrections.

**Choose a combination if:** You want maximum security. Running an underground fence 3 to 5 feet inside a physical fence creates a buffer zone that trains the dog to stay away from the fence itself, reducing jumping attempts and fence-line aggression.

## Money-Saving Strategies

**Get multiple quotes.** For professional installation of any type, get at least three written quotes. Price variation of 30 to 50 percent between contractors is common.

**Consider hybrid approaches.** A full physical fence on the front and sides (where appearances matter) with an underground fence across the back (where the lot is widest and physical fencing is most expensive) can cut total costs by 30 to 40 percent.

**Install in the off-season.** Late fall and winter quotes are 10 to 20 percent lower for physical fencing. The ground may be harder to trench for underground systems, but fence installers are hungry for work.

**DIY the easy parts.** If you hire a contractor for a physical fence, consider staining or sealing it yourself. That saves $1 to $3 per linear foot on the initial installation.

## The Bottom Line for 2026

Pet fence costs have increased 5 to 10 percent since 2024, driven primarily by lumber and metal prices. Electronic systems have held steady or decreased slightly as competition increases. The best value remains a DIY underground fence for large properties ($0.50 to $1.00 per linear foot) and a professionally installed wood or chain link fence for smaller properties where physical containment is needed ($8 to $30 per linear foot). Budget an extra 10 to 15 percent above the estimates above for permit fees, property surveys, and the inevitable materials you did not account for in the initial plan.
