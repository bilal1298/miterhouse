Insulating an attic costs between $1,500 and $3,500 for a typical 1,000-square-foot attic when you hire a professional, or $500 to $1,500 if you do the work yourself. The exact number depends almost entirely on two factors: what type of insulation you choose and how much R-value you need to reach.

Those are not arbitrary choices. Your climate zone dictates the target R-value, and your attic's layout determines which insulation type works. Here is how to estimate your specific cost with real numbers.

## How Much Insulation Do You Actually Need?

The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/insulation) recommends attic insulation levels by climate zone:

- **Zones 1-3 (Southern US):** R-30 to R-60
- **Zone 4 (Mid-Atlantic, lower Midwest):** R-38 to R-60
- **Zones 5-8 (Northern US):** R-49 to R-60

Most existing homes have R-19 to R-30 in the attic — below current recommendations. If you are insulating from scratch (no existing insulation), you are installing 10 to 20 inches of material depending on type. If you are adding to existing insulation, you may only need 4 to 8 inches on top to reach the target.

**Measuring what you have:** Pull back a section of existing insulation and measure from the top of the ceiling drywall to the top of the insulation. Fiberglass batts provide roughly R-3.2 per inch, cellulose about R-3.5 per inch, and spray foam about R-6 per inch. Multiply your depth by the per-inch R-value to estimate your current level.

## Cost by Insulation Type

### Blown-In Cellulose

**Cost:** $0.80 to $1.50 per square foot installed professionally; $0.40 to $0.70 per square foot DIY (machine rental is typically free with material purchase of 20+ bags).

**What it is:** Ground-up recycled newspaper treated with borate fire retardant. It is blown into the attic through a hose connected to a machine, filling around joists, wiring, and obstacles.

**Best for:** Open attic floors over flat ceilings, adding insulation over existing batts, and irregular joist spacing. It conforms to any shape, fills gaps, and provides excellent coverage.

**R-value per inch:** 3.2 to 3.8 (settled). Plan on 12 to 14 inches for R-49.

**For a 1,000 sq ft attic to R-49:**
- DIY: $400 to $700 (25 to 30 bags at $12 to $18 each, machine rental free)
- Professional: $800 to $1,500

Cellulose is the cost-performance sweet spot for most attics.

### Blown-In Fiberglass

**Cost:** $1.00 to $1.80 per square foot installed; $0.50 to $0.90 DIY.

**What it is:** Loose fiberglass blown in the same way as cellulose. It does not settle as much over time and does not absorb moisture.

**Best for:** Same applications as cellulose. Slightly better moisture resistance, slightly lower R-value per inch (2.5 to 3.0), so you need more depth.

**For a 1,000 sq ft attic to R-49:**
- DIY: $500 to $900
- Professional: $1,000 to $1,800

### Fiberglass Batts

**Cost:** $0.50 to $1.20 per square foot for materials; professional installation adds $0.50 to $1.00 per square foot.

**What it is:** Pre-cut rolls or batts of fiberglass blanket insulation. Comes in standard widths (15 and 23 inches) to fit between joists spaced 16 or 24 inches on center.

**Best for:** New construction or completely empty attic floors with standard joist spacing. Not ideal for retrofitting over existing insulation or in attics with many obstructions (pipes, wires, HVAC ducts).

**Limitation:** Batts leave gaps. Every wire, pipe, junction box, and irregular framing member creates a gap that reduces effective R-value. In practice, batt installations test at 15 to 25 percent below their rated R-value because of imperfect fit. Blown-in products avoid this problem.

**For a 1,000 sq ft attic to R-49:**
- DIY: $500 to $1,200
- Professional: $1,000 to $2,200

### Spray Foam

**Cost:** $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot for open-cell; $2.50 to $6.00 per square foot for closed-cell. Professional installation only — DIY spray foam kits exist but are not practical for attic-scale projects.

**What it is:** Two-part chemical mixture sprayed as a liquid that expands into a solid foam. Open-cell is soft and spongy; closed-cell is dense and rigid.

**Best for:** Cathedral ceilings, finished attic spaces, and situations where you need both insulation and an air barrier. Spray foam is the only insulation that also serves as a vapor barrier (closed-cell) and completely seals air leaks.

**For a 1,000 sq ft attic (roof deck application):**
- Open-cell to R-38: $1,500 to $3,500
- Closed-cell to R-38: $2,500 to $6,000

Spray foam is 2 to 4 times the cost of blown-in products but may be the only option for certain attic configurations.

## The Costs Most People Forget

### Air Sealing

Before adding any insulation, seal air leaks. Every penetration in the attic floor — around wiring, plumbing vents, recessed lights, the attic hatch, and top plates of interior walls — leaks conditioned air into the attic. Air leaks can reduce insulation effectiveness by 25 to 40 percent.

Professional air sealing costs $350 to $1,000 for a typical attic. DIY air sealing with caulk and expanding foam costs $30 to $80 in materials and takes 3 to 5 hours. This is the single highest-ROI step in any attic insulation project.

### Old Insulation Removal

If existing insulation is damaged, contaminated (mouse droppings, mold), or you need to air-seal before re-insulating, removal comes first. Professional removal costs $1 to $3 per square foot — see our [insulation removal cost guide](/blog/insulation-removal-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/) for detailed pricing. You can also check our [attic insulation removal guide](/blog/attic-insulation-removal-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/) for the full process.

### Attic Ventilation

Insulation and ventilation work as a system. After insulating, ensure soffit vents are not blocked by the new insulation (install baffles at $1 to $2 each to maintain airflow) and that ridge or gable vents provide adequate exhaust. Inadequate ventilation after insulating can cause moisture buildup, ice dams, and shortened roof life.

Baffles for a 1,000-square-foot attic cost $50 to $100 in materials.

## DIY vs. Professional: Which Makes Sense?

**DIY works well for:**
- Blown-in cellulose or fiberglass over an open attic floor. The equipment is straightforward, the materials are available at any home center, and the machine is often free with purchase.
- Adding insulation over existing insulation (assuming it is in decent condition).
- Air sealing before insulating.

**Hire a professional for:**
- Spray foam (requires specialized equipment and training).
- Attics with significant hazards: vermiculite insulation (may contain asbestos), knob-and-tube wiring, or extensive rodent contamination.
- If you need old insulation removed first — professional vacuum equipment handles this 10 times faster than manual removal.
- Cathedral ceilings and finished attic spaces where access is limited.

## Return on Investment

Attic insulation is one of the highest-ROI home improvements available. The [EPA estimates](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that proper attic insulation and air sealing saves 15 to 20 percent on heating and cooling costs. For a home spending $2,400 per year on energy, that is $360 to $480 annually.

A $1,500 blown-in cellulose installation pays for itself in 3 to 4 years and continues saving money for decades. Insulation does not wear out, break, or need replacement under normal conditions — it just sits there saving energy for the life of the house.
