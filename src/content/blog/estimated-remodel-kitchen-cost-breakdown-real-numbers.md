A kitchen remodel costs $15,000–$75,000 for most homeowners, with the national average landing at $35,000–$40,000 for a mid-range renovation of a standard-sized kitchen. That range is enormous because "kitchen remodel" covers everything from a cosmetic refresh — paint, hardware, countertops — to a gut renovation with new cabinets, appliances, flooring, plumbing, and electrical. Here's how to figure out where your project falls, with every line item broken down.

## Three Tiers, Three Budgets

### Cosmetic Refresh: $5,000–$15,000

You keep the existing layout, cabinets, and infrastructure. The work is surface-level:

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Cabinet painting or refacing | $3,000–$7,000 |
| New countertops (laminate or butcher block) | $1,000–$3,000 |
| Updated hardware (pulls, knobs, hinges) | $200–$600 |
| New backsplash | $500–$2,000 |
| Updated lighting fixtures | $200–$800 |
| Fresh paint | $200–$500 DIY / $500–$1,200 pro |

**The editorial take:** This tier delivers the highest return per dollar spent because you're changing the look without touching infrastructure. If your cabinet boxes are structurally sound, your layout works, and your plumbing and electrical are up to code — stop here. Spend $8,000 on paint, refacing, new hardware, and a quartz countertop, and your kitchen looks like a different room.

### Mid-Range Renovation: $25,000–$50,000

New cabinets, countertops, and appliances within the existing footprint. Minor plumbing or electrical updates but no wall removal or major layout changes.

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Cabinets (stock or semi-custom) | $8,000–$18,000 |
| Countertops (quartz or granite) | $3,000–$8,000 |
| Appliances (mid-range package) | $3,000–$8,000 |
| Flooring | $1,500–$4,000 |
| Backsplash | $800–$2,500 |
| Plumbing fixtures (sink, faucet) | $400–$1,200 |
| Lighting | $500–$2,000 |
| Labor (installation) | $5,000–$12,000 |
| Permits and inspections | $200–$500 |

**The editorial take:** This is where most homeowners land, and the biggest trap is blowing 40% of the budget on cabinets. Stock cabinets from Home Depot or Lowe's at $60–$200 per linear foot cover 90% of standard kitchen layouts. Semi-custom at $100–$400/ft looks nicer but eats your appliance and countertop budget fast.

### Major Renovation: $50,000–$100,000+

Layout changes, wall removal, new plumbing and electrical runs, custom cabinetry, premium everything.

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Custom cabinets | $15,000–$40,000 |
| Premium countertops (quartz, marble) | $5,000–$15,000 |
| Appliance package (premium) | $8,000–$20,000 |
| Structural work (wall removal, beam) | $3,000–$10,000 |
| Plumbing relocation | $2,000–$6,000 |
| Electrical upgrade (new circuits, panel work) | $2,000–$5,000 |
| Flooring | $2,000–$6,000 |
| Backsplash (custom tile) | $2,000–$5,000 |
| Design fees | $2,000–$5,000 |
| General contractor (15–25% of total) | Varies |

**The editorial take:** Unless you're fixing a genuinely broken layout — a galley kitchen you can't work in, a closed-off space that kills the home's flow — keep the walls where they are. Removing a load-bearing wall with an engineered beam costs $3,000–$10,000 and triggers a chain reaction of drywall patching, flooring transitions, ceiling repair, and structural engineering fees. That money often does more for your kitchen as premium appliances or custom countertops.

## Where the Budget Actually Goes

Across all tiers, here's how the budget breaks down:

| Category | % of Budget | On a $35,000 Project |
|----------|------------|---------------------|
| Cabinets and hardware | 30–35% | $10,500–$12,250 |
| Countertops | 10–15% | $3,500–$5,250 |
| Appliances | 15–20% | $5,250–$7,000 |
| Labor/installation | 20–25% | $7,000–$8,750 |
| Flooring | 5–8% | $1,750–$2,800 |
| Lighting and electrical | 3–5% | $1,050–$1,750 |
| Plumbing | 3–5% | $1,050–$1,750 |
| Backsplash | 2–4% | $700–$1,400 |
| Permits, design, misc | 3–5% | $1,050–$1,750 |

Cabinets dominate because they define the kitchen's look and function more than any other single element. If you're tight on budget, keeping existing cabinets — paint them, reface them, or swap out doors and drawer fronts — frees up 30%+ for everything else.

## What Pushes Costs Higher

**Layout changes are the big multiplier.** Moving the sink requires plumbing relocation ($1,000–$3,000). Moving the range means gas line rerouting ($500–$2,000) and potentially a new hood vent run ($500–$1,500). The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) governs relocated plumbing and electrical — expect $200–$500 in permit costs for any layout change.

**Old electrical can't keep up.** Homes built before 1990 rarely have the dedicated circuits modern kitchens need. Adding a 20-amp circuit for a microwave or upgrading the panel for an induction range costs $200–$500 per circuit.

**Structural work compounds fast.** A non-load-bearing wall removal runs $1,000–$3,000 but still requires drywall patching, [flooring transitions](/blog/finishes-in-interior-design-honest-advice-for-your-home/), and ceiling repair. Load-bearing walls with an engineered beam hit $3,000–$10,000 including the structural engineer's stamp.

**Geography moves the needle.** Kitchen remodels in San Francisco, New York, or Boston run 30–50% above the national average. Mid-size cities in the Midwest and South fall 10–20% below. Same cabinets, same countertops, very different labor rates.

## Where to Save Real Money

**Keep the layout.** Eliminating plumbing relocation, electrical rerouting, and structural work saves $3,000–$10,000 in one decision.

**Choose stock cabinets.** $60–$200 per linear foot versus $300–$1,000+ for full custom. Unless your space has genuinely unusual dimensions, stock cabinets in standard sizes handle the job.

**Go direct to a countertop fabricator.** Big-box retailers mark up countertop materials and installation heavily. Going directly to a local fabricator for quartz or granite saves 20–30% for the same stone.

**DIY the demolition.** Ripping out old cabinets, countertops, backsplash, and flooring is physical but simple. A weekend of demo saves $1,000–$2,500 in labor. Rent a 10-yard dumpster ($300–$500) and go to town. Leave the plumbing and electrical disconnects to a licensed pro.

**Phase the project.** Cabinets and countertops now, flooring and backsplash in three months, appliances when the holiday sales hit. Phasing stretches the timeline but lets you cash-flow the renovation without financing costs.

## Realistic Timelines

| Project Scope | Contractor-Led | DIY (Weekends) |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Cosmetic refresh | 1–2 weeks | 3–6 weekends |
| Mid-range renovation | 4–8 weeks | 3–6 months |
| Major renovation | 8–16 weeks | Not recommended for full DIY |

Add 2–4 weeks for cabinet ordering. Stock ships in 1–2 weeks. Semi-custom takes 4–8 weeks. Custom takes 8–16 weeks. Countertop fabrication and installation adds another 2–3 weeks after template measurement — and the template can't happen until cabinets are fully installed. Order cabinets first, or your entire timeline slides.

## Getting Accurate Estimates

Three quotes is the minimum. Five is better for projects over $25,000. When comparing quotes, make sure each one includes:

- Detailed line items for every material and labor category
- Specific product names and model numbers — not "quartz countertop" but the brand, color, and edge profile
- All permit fees
- Debris removal and dumpster costs
- A 10–15% contingency (reputable [contractors](/blog/contractor-to-remodel-kitchen-what-to-know/) build this in)
- Payment schedule tied to milestones, not calendar dates
- Start date and estimated completion date
- Warranty terms for labor

If a quote comes in well below the others for the same scope, ask what's excluded. Common omissions in low bids: electrical work, plumbing modifications, drywall repair, painting, and debris hauling. A low bid with hidden exclusions isn't a deal — it's a change-order machine.
