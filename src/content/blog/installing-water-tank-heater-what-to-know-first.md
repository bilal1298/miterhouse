A water tank heater installation typically runs between $800 and $2,500 for the unit and labor combined, depending on whether you choose gas or electric and whether the existing hookups need modification. If your current water heater is over 10 years old, leaking from the tank itself, or just not keeping up with demand, replacement is almost always a better investment than repair.

Before you start shopping for a new unit or calling contractors, there are several things worth understanding about how water heaters work, what the installation involves, and where DIY ends and professional work begins.

## Gas vs. Electric: Choosing the Right Type

The first decision is fuel source, and for most homeowners, the answer is whatever you already have. Switching from electric to gas (or vice versa) adds significant cost because you need new supply lines, venting, or electrical circuits.

**Gas water heaters** heat faster and cost less to operate in most markets. A standard 40-gallon gas unit runs roughly $400 to $800 for the unit itself. They require a dedicated gas line, a flue or power vent for exhaust, and adequate combustion air supply. The installation is more complex and almost always requires a licensed plumber.

**Electric water heaters** are simpler to install and have lower upfront costs, typically $300 to $700 for the unit. They need a dedicated 240-volt circuit (usually 30 amps for a standard unit) and don't require venting. The trade-off is higher operating costs in most regions, since electricity tends to cost more per BTU than natural gas.

**Tank size matters.** For a household of two to three people, a 40-gallon tank usually suffices. Four or more people generally need 50 gallons. Going bigger than you need wastes energy keeping water hot that nobody uses.

## What the Installation Actually Involves

Whether you hire a pro or tackle this yourself, here is what happens during a typical water tank heater installation:

### Removing the Old Unit

1. Shut off the fuel supply (gas valve or breaker) and the cold water inlet valve.
2. Connect a garden hose to the drain valve and drain the tank completely. This takes 20 to 40 minutes for a full tank.
3. Disconnect the water supply lines, the gas line or electrical connections, and the flue pipe (gas units).
4. Move the old unit out. A full-size water heater weighs 100 to 150 pounds empty, so have a helper or a dolly.

### Setting the New Unit

1. Position the new heater on the existing platform or stand. In earthquake-prone areas, seismic straps are required by [building code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/).
2. Connect the cold water inlet and hot water outlet. Use dielectric unions if you are connecting copper to galvanized steel to prevent corrosion.
3. Install a new temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve if the unit does not come with one. The discharge pipe must terminate within 6 inches of the floor or outside.
4. For gas units, reconnect the gas supply line, check every joint with soapy water for leaks, and reconnect the flue. For electric units, wire the 240-volt connection.
5. Open the cold water supply to fill the tank completely before turning on the power or gas. Heating an empty tank destroys the element (electric) or damages the tank (gas).

## Where DIY Ends

Some tasks are well within a capable homeowner's reach. Others are not.

**Reasonable DIY tasks:**
- Draining and disconnecting the old unit
- Hauling out the old tank and positioning the new one
- Connecting water supply lines with flexible connectors
- Installing seismic straps

**Leave these to a licensed professional:**
- Any gas line connections or modifications
- Running or modifying a 240-volt electrical circuit
- Installing new venting or modifying existing flue runs
- Pulling permits and scheduling inspections

In most jurisdictions, gas and electrical work requires permits and licensed tradespeople. This is not just a legal formality. Improper gas connections can cause carbon monoxide poisoning or explosions, and faulty electrical work creates fire hazards. [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/) standards exist for a reason.

## Permits and Code Requirements

Nearly every municipality requires a permit for water heater replacement, even when it is a like-for-like swap. The permit fee is usually $50 to $150 and includes an inspection. Common code requirements include:

- An expansion tank on closed-loop systems (most municipal water systems now)
- A drip pan under the unit if it is installed above living space
- Proper clearances from combustible materials (gas units)
- An accessible shut-off valve on the cold water supply
- Adequate combustion air supply (gas units in enclosed spaces)
- A sediment trap (drip leg) on the gas supply line

Skipping the permit creates problems at resale and may void your homeowner's insurance if something goes wrong.

## Cost Breakdown: What to Budget

Here is a realistic breakdown of what you might spend:

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Tank water heater (40-50 gal) | $300 - $800 |
| Professional installation labor | $300 - $1,000 |
| Expansion tank | $40 - $80 |
| New water supply connectors | $15 - $40 |
| Permit and inspection | $50 - $150 |
| Haul-away of old unit | $25 - $75 |
| **Total (typical)** | **$730 - $2,145** |

If your installation requires new gas lines, electrical work, or relocating the water heater, add $500 to $1,500 for those modifications. Bringing an older installation up to current code sometimes adds unexpected costs, so budget a 15% contingency.

## Efficiency and Long-Term Savings

Modern [WaterSense-labeled](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) fixtures and Energy Star-rated water heaters use significantly less energy than units from even 10 years ago. Look for the Energy Factor (UEF) rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label. For gas units, a UEF of 0.64 or higher is decent. For electric, look for 0.93 or above.

A few upgrades that pay for themselves quickly:

- **Insulation blanket** on the tank: $20 to $30, saves roughly $20 to $45 per year
- **Pipe insulation** on the first 6 feet of hot water pipe: under $10 in materials
- **Setting the thermostat to 120 degrees** instead of the factory default of 140: reduces scalding risk and cuts standby losses

If your household uses less than 40 gallons per day, a tankless unit might save money long-term, but the upfront cost is two to three times higher and the installation is more involved.

## Timing and Planning

Water heaters rarely fail at convenient times, but if yours is approaching 10 years old, plan ahead rather than waiting for an emergency. A planned replacement lets you compare quotes, choose the right unit, and schedule the work on your timeline.

If you are dealing with a leaking tank, there is no repair that makes sense. A leaking tank means the steel has corroded through, and it will only get worse. Shut off the water supply to the unit, shut off the fuel source, and get a replacement scheduled.

## Final Thought

A water tank heater installation is one of those jobs where spending a few hundred dollars on professional labor buys you genuine peace of mind. The unit itself is straightforward, but the gas and electrical connections carry real risk if done incorrectly. Get quotes from at least three licensed plumbers, confirm they will pull the permit, and make sure the price includes hauling away the old unit. A good plumber can complete a standard swap in two to four hours, and you will have reliable hot water for the next decade.
