A granite countertop installation for a typical U-shaped kitchen (roughly 40 to 50 square feet of counter space) runs between $3,500 and $7,000 when you factor in materials, fabrication, and professional installation. That range covers Level 1 through Level 2 granite with a standard edge profile — bump up to exotic slabs or complex layouts and you will push past $10,000 quickly.

I have been tracking real project costs from my own kitchen renovation and several neighbors' projects over the past few years, and the numbers below reflect what homeowners actually pay in 2026 — not contractor list prices or manufacturer MSRPs.

## Line-by-Line Cost Breakdown

Here is where every dollar goes on a granite countertop project. I am using a 45-square-foot kitchen as the baseline, which covers a fairly standard L-shape with an island.

### Material Costs

| Component | Price Range | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Granite slab (Level 1) | $1,800–$2,250 | $40–$50/sq ft |
| Granite slab (Level 2) | $2,250–$3,150 | $50–$70/sq ft |
| Granite slab (Level 3+) | $3,150–$4,500+ | $70–$100+/sq ft |
| Plywood subtop (if needed) | $75–$150 | 3/4" plywood for support |
| Adhesive and caulk | $30–$60 | Silicone-based for stone |
| Sealer | $15–$30 | First application often included |

### Labor and Fabrication

| Service | Price Range | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Templating | $0–$300 | Often included in fabrication |
| Fabrication | $10–$30/sq ft | Cutting, polishing, cutouts |
| Edge profiling upgrade | $10–$30/linear ft | Beyond standard eased edge |
| Installation labor | $10–$20/sq ft | Setting, seaming, sealing |
| Sink cutout | $100–$300 | Per cutout |
| Cooktop cutout | $150–$350 | Larger and more complex |
| Faucet hole drilling | $30–$75 | Per hole |

### Related Work

| Task | Price Range | Notes |
|------|------------|-------|

| Cabinet leveling/repair | $100–$400 | Critical for granite |

## Real Project Example: My Kitchen

When I replaced the laminate in my kitchen, here is exactly what I paid:

- **45 sq ft of Level 2 Giallo Ornamental granite:** $2,925 ($65/sq ft)
- **Fabrication with bullnose edge:** $675
- **Two sink cutouts (kitchen sink + prep sink):** $400
- **Installation labor:** $675
- **Old laminate removal (I did this myself):** $0
- **Plumber disconnect/reconnect:** $250
- **Sealing:** included
- **Total: $4,925**

That total would have been about $700 higher if I had not removed the old countertops myself and hauled them to the dump. Demolition is the easiest way to trim costs without compromising quality.

## Hidden Costs That Catch People

After going through this process and helping friends plan theirs, I have identified the expenses that consistently surprise homeowners.

**Seam placement fees.** If your layout requires seams in unusual locations — like mid-island or near a sink — expect the fabricator to charge more. Seams near moisture areas are harder to execute well and require more precision. A good fabricator will explain seam placement during templating and let you weigh in on where they fall.

**Backsplash removal damage.** Pulling off an old tile backsplash often damages the drywall behind it. If you are replacing the backsplash anyway, this is not a big deal. But if you planned to reuse the existing backsplash and it gets damaged during countertop removal, that is an unplanned $300 to $800 repair.

**Support brackets for overhangs.** Any granite overhang beyond 10 to 12 inches (common on islands for bar seating) needs steel support brackets. These run $30 to $80 each, and you typically need one every 24 inches along the overhang. For a 6-foot island with a 14-inch overhang, that is three to four brackets plus installation.

**Appliance clearance issues.** Granite is thicker than laminate (3cm vs. about 1.5cm including substrate). After installation, your dishwasher might not fit under the counter anymore. Check clearances before the templating visit — adjusting dishwasher legs or trimming cabinet openings post-installation is a hassle.

## How to Save Without Sacrificing Quality

You do not need to go cheap on granite to keep costs reasonable. These strategies trim the total without affecting what guests see.

**Choose remnants for small projects.** If you are only covering a bathroom vanity or a small prep area, ask fabricators about remnant pieces. These are leftover sections from larger jobs, and they typically cost 30 to 50% less per square foot than buying a full slab. The trade-off is limited color selection.

**Pick a Level 1 color confidently.** Uba Tuba, Caledonia, and New Venetian Gold are "builder grade" only because they are popular — they look great in most kitchens. The per-square-foot savings of $15 to $30 compared to Level 2 adds up to $600 to $1,200 on a typical kitchen.

**Handle demo yourself.** Removing old laminate or tile countertops is straightforward. Unscrew from below, pry up, carry out. You need a helper for the weight but no special skills. This saves $150 to $500 in labor.

**Consolidate cutouts.** Every hole and cutout costs extra. If you can use a single-hole faucet instead of a three-hole, that saves $60 to $150. Similarly, consider whether you really need that soap dispenser hole — a countertop-mounted pump works fine.

**Time the purchase right.** Fabricators are often slower in January through March. Scheduling your project in that window can sometimes get you a better rate or faster turnaround, and faster turnaround means less time eating takeout because your [kitchen is torn apart](/blog/the-real-deal-on-kitchen-renovation-services/).

## Getting Quotes: What to Compare

When I collected quotes, I made a spreadsheet comparing them apples-to-apples. Here is the framework I used:

1. **Slab grade and specific color** — make sure every quote covers the same stone
2. **Edge profile** — standard eased vs. upgraded profiles
3. **Number of cutouts** — sinks, cooktop, faucet holes
4. **Removal of old counters** — included or not
5. **Plumbing scope** — disconnect/reconnect or just disconnect
6. **Warranty terms** — fabrication defect coverage and duration
7. **Timeline** — from templating to completed installation
8. **Payment terms** — deposit percentage and final payment timing

The lowest quote I received was $1,400 less than the highest for nearly identical scope. That gap was entirely in labor pricing — the stone cost was almost the same across all three fabricators because they source from the same regional distributors.

## When Granite Is Not the Right Call

Granite is excellent for most kitchens, but there are situations where another material makes more sense.

If your cabinets are in poor condition and you cannot afford to replace or reinforce them, the weight of granite (roughly 20 lbs per square foot for 3cm thickness) could accelerate cabinet failure. Lighter materials like quartz composite or even high-quality laminate may serve you better until you can address the cabinet situation.

If you are doing a [full kitchen renovation](/blog/home-additions-and-renovations-what-homeowners-should-know/) and plan to resell within two years, mid-range quartz actually edges out granite on ROI in most markets — buyers perceive quartz as more modern and maintenance-free.

And if your primary concern is a seamless, uniform appearance without natural variation, quartz or solid surface will give you more predictable results. Granite's beauty is in its natural variation, but that same variation means your installed countertop will never look exactly like the showroom sample.

## Bottom Line

For a typical kitchen, budget $3,500 to $6,000 for a quality granite countertop installation using Level 1 or Level 2 stone with professional fabrication and installation. Add 15% as a contingency for the inevitable surprises — plumbing complications, cabinet repairs, or that one cutout that costs more than quoted.

Granite remains one of the most durable and visually appealing countertop materials available, and when you shop the process correctly, it delivers strong value for decades of daily use. The key is understanding every line item upfront so nothing catches you off guard on installation day.
