The total installed cost for granite countertops runs $50 to $130 per square foot in 2026, which translates to $2,500 to $6,500 for a typical 50-square-foot kitchen layout. That all-in number includes the slab, fabrication, edge profiling, cutouts, delivery, and professional installation — but it does not include plumbing work, old countertop removal, or backsplash changes, which can add another $500 to $1,500.

Those ranges are wide because granite pricing depends on several factors that interact with each other. Here is how to figure out where your specific project will land.

## The Four Factors That Determine Your Price

### 1. Granite Grade

This is the single biggest cost driver. Granite gets graded by rarity, quarry origin, and visual complexity — not by quality or durability. A Level 1 slab is just as structurally sound as a Level 3.

- **Level 1:** $40–$50/sq ft. Common colors quarried in large volumes. Uba Tuba, Baltic Brown, New Venetian Gold.
- **Level 2:** $50–$70/sq ft. More distinct patterns. Colonial White, Bianco Antico, Typhoon Bordeaux.
- **Level 3+:** $70–$100+/sq ft. Rare colors, dramatic veining. Blue Bahia, Van Gogh, Azul Macaubas.

A practical tip: walk into the slab yard without a specific color in mind and look at what they have in stock. Fabricators often keep a rotating inventory of Level 1 and Level 2 slabs they bought at volume pricing. You get better per-square-foot rates on in-stock material, and you can see the exact slab that will end up in your kitchen rather than guessing from a sample chip.

### 2. Layout Complexity

A straight 8-foot run along one wall is the cheapest layout to fabricate and install. Every turn, corner, and seam adds cost.

- **Straight run:** Base fabrication rate
- **L-shape:** One additional seam, roughly $200–$400 extra
- **U-shape:** Two additional seams, $400–$800 extra
- **Island (separate piece):** Additional template, delivery, and installation — $500–$1,000 extra

Seam placement matters aesthetically too. A skilled fabricator positions seams at natural transition points (like where the counter meets a wall or changes direction) and uses color-matched epoxy that becomes nearly invisible.

### 3. Edge Profile

The edge profile is the cross-section shape of the countertop's front edge. Standard profiles are included in most quotes; upgraded profiles add $10 to $30 per linear foot.

**Included in most quotes:** Eased edge (slightly rounded square edge) or flat polished.

**Common upgrades and approximate added cost per linear foot:**
- Bullnose (half-round): $10–$15
- Beveled: $10–$15
- Ogee (S-curve): $20–$30
- Dupont (stepped): $25–$35
- Laminated edge (doubled thickness): $30–$50

For a kitchen with 25 linear feet of exposed edge, an ogee upgrade costs $500 to $750 more than the standard eased edge. It looks great but that money might deliver better returns elsewhere in the kitchen if you are on a tight budget.

### 4. Geographic Location

Labor rates and stone availability vary significantly by region. Metros with lots of granite fabricators (like Houston, Atlanta, or the DC suburbs) tend to have more competitive pricing. Rural areas or markets with fewer fabricators may charge 15 to 25% more simply because of limited competition and higher delivery costs.

## What the Quote Should Include

A complete, transparent quote covers all of these items. If any are listed separately or omitted, ask — it does not mean they are free, it means they will show up as change orders later.

- Slab material (specific color and grade)
- Template visit
- Fabrication (all cuts, polishing)
- Edge profile (specify which)
- Sink cutout(s)
- Cooktop cutout (if applicable)
- Faucet hole(s)
- Delivery and installation
- Sealing (first application)
- Cleanup and debris removal

Items that are commonly excluded and charged separately:
- Old countertop removal and disposal
- Plumbing disconnect and reconnect
- Electrical work (moving outlets)
- Backsplash removal or installation
- Cabinet repairs or leveling

When comparing multiple quotes, [make sure the scope is identical](/blog/sustainable-home-renovation-what-homeowners-should-know/). The lowest bid often excludes items the other quotes include.

## Real Cost Scenarios

Here are three realistic scenarios based on actual project costs I have tracked:

### Scenario 1: Budget Kitchen Refresh — $2,500 to $3,500

- 30 sq ft of Level 1 Uba Tuba granite
- Eased edge profile
- Single sink cutout, single faucet hole
- Homeowner removes old laminate countertops
- Homeowner disconnects/reconnects plumbing (basic P-trap swap)
- No backsplash changes

### Scenario 2: Standard Kitchen Upgrade — $4,500 to $6,500

- 45 sq ft of Level 2 Bianco Antico granite
- Bullnose edge profile
- Undermount sink cutout with new sink
- Cooktop cutout
- Professional removal of old countertops
- Plumber handles disconnect/reconnect
- Basic drywall patching where old backsplash was disturbed

### Scenario 3: Full Kitchen Overhaul — $7,000 to $12,000

- 60+ sq ft of Level 2-3 granite including island
- Ogee edge with laminated edge on island
- Two sink cutouts (main + prep)
- Cooktop cutout
- Full granite backsplash (4-inch)
- Professional removal, plumbing, and electrical work
- Part of a [larger renovation project](/blog/home-additions-and-renovations-what-homeowners-should-know/)

## Mistakes That Inflate the Bill

**Not measuring before the slab yard visit.** Know your approximate square footage before shopping. Measure the length and depth of each counter section, multiply, and add 10% for waste. This prevents you from falling in love with a slab that is too small for your layout, which would require ordering a second slab at premium cost.

**Forgetting to check for level.** Granite fabricators assume your cabinets are level. If they are not, and the installer discovers this on installation day, you are paying for shimming and leveling at emergency rates. Check yourself with a 4-foot level beforehand and address any problems early.

**Choosing too many cutouts.** Every hole in granite costs money and creates a potential stress point. Do you really need a soap dispenser hole, a hot water dispenser hole, and a separate sprayer hole? Consolidate where possible. A single-handle pull-down faucet eliminates the need for a separate sprayer, saving both the cutout cost and one more thing to maintain.

**Ignoring existing plumbing layout.** Moving a sink from one location to another is not just a countertop decision — it involves rerouting supply lines and drain piping, which can cost $500 to $2,000 depending on distance and accessibility. Keep the sink where it is unless the [plumbing rework](/blog/bathroom-remodeling-salem-what-homeowners-should-know/) is already part of a bigger renovation budget.

## The Installation Day Experience

Most homeowners have never seen granite installation up close, and the process can be nerve-wracking when a crew is maneuvering 300-pound slabs through your kitchen doorway.

The crew arrives with the slabs on an A-frame cart, usually carried by two to four people. They do a dry fit first, checking alignment and seam locations. Then they apply silicone adhesive to the subtop, carefully lower the granite into position, and level everything using shims as needed.

Seams get filled with color-matched epoxy and cured. Undermount sinks get clipped and sealed from below. The whole process takes 2 to 5 hours depending on complexity.

You should not walk on, lean on, or put heavy items on the countertops for 24 hours after installation while the adhesive cures. The sealer needs 24 hours before the surface can handle food preparation.

Per the [National Fire Protection Association](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) and local building codes, any electrical outlets near the new countertop must be GFCI-protected if they are within 6 feet of a water source. Countertop replacement is a good time to verify this and upgrade non-compliant outlets.

## Final Numbers

For a straight answer: most homeowners pay $3,500 to $6,000 total for granite countertops installed in a standard kitchen. That assumes Level 1 or Level 2 granite, a moderately complex layout, professional installation, and basic supporting work like plumbing.

Add 15% contingency for surprises, get three quotes with identical scope, and visit the slab yard in person before committing. Granite is a 20-plus-year surface when properly installed and maintained — the upfront investment pays off in durability, daily satisfaction, and [resale value](/blog/the-real-deal-on-kitchen-renovation-services/) that far outlasts trendy alternatives.
