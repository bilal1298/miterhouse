A dog fence installation runs anywhere from $250 for a DIY invisible fence on a small lot to $20,000 or more for a professional privacy fence around a large property. That is an enormous range, so let us break it into actual numbers you can use for planning.

The cost depends on four things: fence type, property size (measured in linear feet of perimeter), who does the work, and your region. Here is what each combination looks like in practice.

## Cost by Fence Type and Property Size

These numbers represent installed costs for professional work, including materials, labor, and standard site prep. DIY material-only costs are in parentheses.

The price per linear foot drops slightly on larger jobs because setup and mobilization costs are spread across more footage. Expect roughly 5 to 10 percent savings on jobs over 400 linear feet.

## Labor Costs: What Installers Actually Charge

Labor typically represents 40 to 60 percent of the total installed cost. Here is how that breaks down.

**Fence contractors** generally charge one of two ways:

1. **Per linear foot** (most common): $5 to $15 per foot for chain link, $8 to $20 for wood, $12 to $25 for vinyl. This includes post holes, setting posts in concrete, attaching rails and pickets, and cleanup.

2. **Per day** (for complex jobs): $400 to $800 per day per crew. A two-person crew can install 80 to 120 linear feet of wood fence per day on flat terrain.

**Gate installation** adds $200 to $600 per gate, depending on size and type. A standard 4-foot walk-through gate costs $200 to $350. A 10 to 12-foot double-drive gate for vehicle access costs $400 to $800.

**Post hole digging** is the most labor-intensive part. Manual post holes take 15 to 30 minutes each in easy soil. Rocky or clay soil can triple that time. Power auger rental ($50 to $100 per day) is worth every penny if you are doing more than 10 posts.

## The Hidden Costs Nobody Mentions

These are the line items that push your actual spending 15 to 25 percent beyond the base quote.

**Property survey.** If you do not know exactly where your property line is, get a survey before building. Cost: $300 to $600. Building even a few inches on your neighbor's property can result in forced removal.

**Utility locating.** Call 811 (free) to mark buried utilities. If the fence line runs near a utility, the installer may need to hand-dig in that area, adding labor time.

**Grading and clearing.** If the fence line runs through brush, stumps, or uneven terrain, expect $500 to $2,000 for clearing and grading depending on the length and severity.

**Old fence removal.** Removing an existing fence costs $3 to $5 per linear foot for chain link and $5 to $8 for wood. Disposal adds $100 to $300 depending on volume and local dump fees.

**Permit fees.** Most municipalities charge $50 to $200 for a fence permit. Processing takes 1 to 4 weeks.

**Concrete for posts.** Each post needs 1 to 2 bags of concrete ($4 to $8 per bag). For a 300-foot fence with posts every 8 feet, that is 38 posts and $150 to $300 in concrete alone.

**Gates.** Budget quotes often assume no gates. A fence is useless without access, and gates cost disproportionately more per foot than the fence line.

## Dog-Specific Fence Considerations

Not all fences are equal when the goal is pet containment. Dogs test fences in ways that other fence uses (privacy, property line) do not account for.

### For Diggers

Dogs that dig need a barrier below the fence line. Options include:

- **Buried wire mesh (hardware cloth):** Bend 12-inch wide galvanized hardware cloth in an L-shape and bury it along the fence base with the horizontal section extending into the yard. Material: $0.50 to $1.50 per foot.
- **Concrete footer:** Pour a 4 to 6 inch deep concrete strip along the fence base. Effective but expensive: $5 to $12 per linear foot.
- **Buried fence extension:** Bury the bottom 6 to 12 inches of the fence panel below grade. Adds $2 to $5 per foot in materials and labor.

### For Jumpers and Climbers

Dogs that go over or up the fence need height and anti-climb features:

- **6-foot minimum height** for any jumping breed (labs, huskies, shepherds, pit bulls)
- **Lean-in extensions** at the top, angled inward at 45 degrees, adding 18 to 24 inches of barrier. Cost: $3 to $6 per foot.
- **Roller bars** on top that spin when a dog grabs them. Cost: $5 to $10 per foot.
- **Solid fence panels** (privacy fencing) are harder to climb than chain link. Upgrade from chain link to privacy if climbing is the issue.

### For Fence Fighters

Dogs that run the fence line barking at people, dogs, or wildlife create a different set of problems. The solution is reducing visual stimulation.

- **Privacy slats in chain link** ($3 to $5 per foot) block the view
- **Solid privacy panels** eliminate the trigger entirely
- **An underground fence set 3 to 5 feet inside the physical fence** creates a buffer zone that keeps the dog away from the fence line

## DIY vs. Professional: The Honest Assessment

DIY fence installation saves 40 to 60 percent on labor but demands significant time and physical effort.

**A realistic DIY timeline for 200 linear feet of wood privacy fence:**
- Layout and marking: 2 to 3 hours
- Post hole digging (manual): 6 to 10 hours
- Setting posts in concrete: 4 to 6 hours
- Wait for concrete to cure: 24 to 48 hours
- Attaching rails and pickets: 8 to 12 hours
- **Total: 20 to 31 hours of labor, spread over 2 to 3 weekends**

A professional crew handles the same job in 1 to 2 days.

**DIY makes sense if:** You have the time, enjoy physical work, have a helper for the heavy parts (posts and concrete), and the terrain is manageable.

**Hire a pro if:** Your property has slopes, rocky soil, or extensive hard-surface crossings. If the fence needs to be precisely on the property line for legal reasons, professional installation with a survey is money well spent.

For finding and evaluating [fence installation companies](/blog/pet-fence-installation-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/), get at least three quotes and check references. The [Simpson Strong-Tie](https://www.strongtie.com/) website has specifications for proper post brackets and hardware if you want to verify a contractor's methods. And for [water-efficient landscaping](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) around your new fence, plan plantings that do not interfere with the fence structure.

## Regional Price Variation

Where you live affects the price more than most people realize.

| Region | Price Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Northeast (NY, NJ, CT, MA) | +15 to 25% above national average |
| West Coast (CA, WA, OR) | +10 to 20% |
| Southeast (FL, GA, NC, SC) | -5 to 10% |
| Midwest (OH, IN, MI, IL) | -5 to 15% |
| Southwest (TX, AZ, NM) | National average |

These variations reflect differences in labor rates, material availability, and permit costs. A 300-foot wood privacy fence that costs $6,000 in Texas might cost $7,500 in New Jersey and $5,400 in Georgia.

## The Bottom Line

For most dog owners with a quarter to half-acre lot, a professional wood privacy fence runs $4,000 to $12,000, and a DIY invisible fence runs $200 to $500. The right choice depends on your dog's behavior, your budget, and whether you need physical security in addition to containment. Get at least three quotes, add 15 to 20 percent to any estimate for hidden costs, and always verify your property line before the first post hole goes in the ground.
