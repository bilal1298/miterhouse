Carpet flooring installation typically runs between $3.50 and $11 per square foot fully installed, with most homeowners spending $700 to $2,500 for a standard bedroom or living room. The final number swings dramatically based on carpet grade, padding quality, and how much subfloor work your room needs before anything gets tacked down.

I've installed carpet in three rooms of my own house and helped friends with two more. Every single project taught me something the last one didn't — usually the hard way. Here's what I wish someone had laid out for me before I started.

## What Goes Into the Total Cost

Carpet pricing breaks down into three buckets that are roughly equal in importance: the carpet itself, the padding underneath, and the labor to install everything properly.

**Carpet material** ranges from $1 to $5 per square foot at retail. Builder-grade polyester sits at the low end. Mid-range nylon with stain treatment occupies the $2 to $3.50 range, and wool or premium branded nylon pushes past $4. For most living spaces, that mid-range nylon hits the sweet spot between durability and cost.

**Padding** costs $0.50 to $1.50 per square foot. This is where many homeowners make the mistake of going cheap. Thin, low-density padding wears out faster, makes the carpet feel flat within a year, and actually voids some carpet warranties. A good 7/16-inch, 6-pound density pad costs maybe $0.30 more per square foot than the bottom-tier option — over a 200-square-foot room, that's $60 for a noticeably better result that lasts years longer.

**Installation labor** typically runs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot. This covers measuring, cutting, seaming, stretching with a power stretcher, and trimming around doors and transitions. Stairs add cost — expect $15 to $25 per step because each one requires individual cutting, tucking, and securing.

## The Hidden Costs Most Guides Skip

The per-square-foot price you see advertised rarely includes everything. Here's what usually gets added:

**Old flooring removal**: $0.50 to $1.50 per square foot. Pulling up old carpet and pad is straightforward. Removing old tile or hardwood before carpeting costs more and creates debris that needs hauling.

**Subfloor repair**: If your plywood subfloor has water damage, soft spots, or squeaks, fixing those runs $1 to $3 per square foot for the affected areas. Ignoring subfloor problems is a mistake that shows up within months — lumps, dips, and squeaks that telegraph through even thick carpet.

**Furniture moving**: Some installers include basic furniture moving. Others charge $25 to $75 per room, or they expect rooms cleared before they arrive. Ask upfront.

**Transitions and thresholds**: Metal or wood transition strips where carpet meets tile, hardwood, or another carpet run $5 to $15 each, plus installation time.

**Disposal fees**: Hauling away old carpet and padding typically costs $25 to $50 per room if the installer handles it.

## Room-by-Room Cost Examples

For a **standard 12x12 bedroom** (144 square feet), expect:
- Budget carpet with basic pad: $500–$750 installed
- Mid-range nylon with quality pad: $850–$1,300 installed
- Premium carpet with upgraded pad: $1,400–$1,900 installed

A **living room at 300 square feet** scales up:
- Budget: $1,050–$1,500
- Mid-range: $1,800–$2,700
- Premium: $2,900–$3,800

**Whole-house carpet** (1,000–1,500 square feet) often gets volume discounts of 10–15% from installers, bringing mid-range jobs to $5,500–$9,000 total.

## Choosing the Right Carpet for Your Space

Not every carpet works everywhere. Here's what actually matters:

**High-traffic areas** (hallways, stairs, family rooms) need dense, low-pile carpet. Look for face weight of 35 ounces or higher and a density rating above 2,000. Nylon fiber outperforms polyester here — it bounces back from compression better and handles abrasion without matting.

**Bedrooms** have lighter traffic, so you can prioritize comfort over durability. Plush or frieze styles in the 25- to 35-ounce range feel great underfoot without the premium price of high-density options.

**Basements** require moisture-resistant materials. Synthetic fibers (polyester or olefin) resist mold better than nylon. If your basement has any history of dampness, consider a moisture barrier pad and make sure [your subfloor is in good shape](/blog/cost-to-finish-a-basement/) before installation.

## Subfloor Prep: The Step That Makes or Breaks the Job

Before any carpet goes down, your subfloor needs to be clean, dry, level, and structurally sound.

**Plywood subfloors** should be checked for:
- Delamination (layers separating, feels spongy)
- Water stains indicating past or current moisture issues
- Protruding nail or screw heads that need setting
- Squeaks from loose fasteners (drive screws into joists to fix)

**Concrete subfloors** need:
- Moisture testing (tape a 2x2 piece of plastic to the slab for 24 hours — if condensation forms underneath, you have a moisture issue)
- Crack filling with appropriate patching compound
- Leveling if any dips exceed 3/16 inch over 10 feet

The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) has specific requirements for subfloor materials and fastening schedules. If you're adding new plywood over an existing subfloor, make sure it meets code thickness requirements.

## DIY vs. Professional Installation

I've done it both ways. Here's my honest take.

**DIY makes sense when:**
- You're doing one or two standard rectangular rooms
- You have access to a carpet stretcher (rentable for $30–$50/day)
- Your subfloor is already in good condition
- You're comfortable with utility knife work on hands and knees

**Hire a pro when:**
- Rooms have complex shapes, multiple doorways, or stairs
- You need seams (seaming carpet invisibly is genuinely difficult)
- The old flooring removal involves [hazardous materials like asbestos](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos) (common in older homes' floor tiles and adhesives)
- You want the carpet warranty honored (many manufacturers require professional installation)

The labor savings from DIY on a 200-square-foot room is roughly $300 to $700. Whether that's worth the 8 to 12 hours of physical work, tool rental, and risk of visible seams is a personal call.

## The Installation Process Step by Step

Whether you're doing this yourself or watching a pro, here's the sequence:

**1. Clear and prep the room.** All furniture out. Remove existing flooring and baseboards (or plan to trim carpet at baseboards). Clean the subfloor thoroughly.

**2. Install tack strips.** Nail tack strips around the room's perimeter, leaving a gap of about 2/3 the carpet thickness between the strip and the wall. The angled tacks point toward the wall.

**3. Lay the padding.** Roll out padding to cover the floor, keeping it inside the tack strips. Staple to plywood subfloors or tape to concrete. Butt the seams tightly without overlapping and tape them together.

**4. Cut and position the carpet.** Cut carpet 4–6 inches longer than the room dimensions on each side. Lay it out and let it acclimate (especially in cold weather). Position seams away from high-traffic paths and doorways.

**5. Seam if needed.** For rooms wider than your carpet roll, use seam tape and a seam iron. This is the step where most DIY jobs get visually obvious. The iron temperature, tape quality, and hand speed all matter.

**6. Stretch and attach.** Use a power stretcher (not just a knee kicker) to stretch carpet taut across the room and hook it onto the tack strips. Under-stretched carpet develops wrinkles and ripples within months. This is the single most important step.

**7. Trim and tuck.** Cut excess carpet at walls with a wall trimmer or sharp utility knife, then tuck edges between the tack strip and wall. Reinstall or replace baseboards.

## When Carpet Makes Financial Sense

If you're weighing carpet against other flooring for resale value, know that carpet has the lowest ROI of any major flooring type in high-visibility rooms. In bedrooms and bonus rooms, though, buyers often prefer it — especially families with young children.

For a [bathroom remodel](/blog/bathroom-remodel-cost-estimate/), carpet is never appropriate due to moisture. But for bedrooms adjacent to a remodeled bathroom, fresh carpet can complement the overall improvement. A [small bathroom remodel](/blog/small-bathroom-remodel-cost/) paired with new bedroom carpet creates a cohesive upgrade that buyers notice.

## Money-Saving Tips That Actually Work

**Buy carpet and padding as a package.** Many retailers discount padding when you buy carpet at the same time — sometimes including free basic pad.

**Shop remnants.** Large enough for a single room? Remnants from major rolls sell at 30–50% discounts. Check flooring stores' remnant sections regularly.

**Time your purchase.** Holiday weekends (Memorial Day, Labor Day, Black Friday) consistently offer the best carpet deals. January and February are also slow months when installers may negotiate labor rates.

**Skip the upgraded padding in closets.** Nobody needs premium pad inside a closet. Use standard 6-pound pad there and put the savings toward better pad in areas your feet actually touch.

**Get at least three quotes.** The spread between the highest and lowest bid on the same job commonly exceeds 30%. Just make sure each bid covers the same scope — including old flooring removal, furniture moving, and transitions.

## Red Flags When Hiring an Installer

Watch out for these:

- **No written estimate** — verbal-only quotes leave room for surprise charges
- **Insisting on starting immediately** — pressure tactics usually signal desperation or inexperience
- **No references or photos of past work** — any experienced installer has a portfolio
- **Suspiciously low price** — usually means they're cutting corners on stretching, padding, or seaming
- **Won't discuss warranty** — installation workmanship should be guaranteed for at least one year

Good carpet installed properly lasts 10 to 15 years in moderate-traffic areas. Bad installation on great carpet creates problems within the first year. The installer matters as much as the material.
