Carpet installation runs $3.50 to $11 per square foot fully installed, with most homeowners spending $700–$2,500 for a standard bedroom or living room. The number swings based on carpet grade, padding quality, and how much subfloor work the room needs before anything gets tacked down.

Here's the part most people get wrong: the padding underneath matters as much as the carpet on top.

## What Goes Into the Price

**Carpet material** runs $1–$5 per square foot at retail. Builder-grade polyester sits at the low end. Mid-range nylon with stain treatment occupies $2–$3.50, and wool or premium branded nylon pushes past $4. For most living spaces, mid-range nylon hits the sweet spot between durability and cost.

**Padding** costs $0.50–$1.50 per square foot. This is where going cheap backfires. Thin, low-density padding wears out faster, makes carpet feel flat within a year, and actually voids some carpet warranties. A 7/16-inch, 6-pound density pad costs maybe $0.30 more per square foot than bottom-tier — that's $60 extra on a 200-square-foot room for a noticeably better result that lasts years longer. Spend it.

**Installation labor** runs $1.50–$3.50 per square foot covering measuring, cutting, seaming, power stretching, and trimming. Stairs add $15–$25 per step because each requires individual cutting, tucking, and securing.

## Hidden Costs the Quote Doesn't Mention

**Old flooring removal:** $0.50–$1.50/sq ft. Old carpet and pad are straightforward. Old tile or hardwood before carpeting costs more and creates hauling-worthy debris.

**Subfloor repair:** $1–$3/sq ft for affected areas. Water damage, soft spots, or squeaks need fixing first. Ignoring subfloor problems shows up within months as lumps, dips, and squeaks that telegraph through even thick carpet.

**Furniture moving:** Some installers include basic moves. Others charge $25–$75/room or expect cleared rooms. Ask upfront.

**Transitions:** Metal or wood strips where carpet meets tile, hardwood, or another carpet run $5–$15 each plus install time.

**Disposal:** $25–$50/room for hauling away old carpet and pad.

## Room-by-Room Costs

**Standard 12×12 bedroom (144 sq ft):**
- Budget carpet + basic pad: $500–$750
- Mid-range nylon + quality pad: $850–$1,300
- Premium carpet + upgraded pad: $1,400–$1,900

**Living room (300 sq ft):**
- Budget: $1,050–$1,500
- Mid-range: $1,800–$2,700
- Premium: $2,900–$3,800

**Whole-house (1,000–1,500 sq ft):** Volume discounts of 10–15% are standard. Mid-range jobs land at $5,500–$9,000 total.

## Picking the Right Carpet

**High-traffic areas** (hallways, stairs, family rooms): Dense, low-pile carpet. Face weight of 35 ounces or higher, density above 2,000. Nylon fiber outperforms polyester — it bounces back from compression and handles abrasion without matting.

**Bedrooms:** Lighter traffic means you can prioritize comfort. Plush or frieze in the 25–35 ounce range feels great underfoot without the premium price.

**Basements:** Synthetic fibers (polyester or olefin) resist mold better than nylon. If the basement has any dampness history, use a moisture barrier pad and make sure the [subfloor is solid](/blog/cost-to-finish-a-basement/) first.

## Subfloor Prep: The Step That Makes or Breaks It

**Plywood subfloors** — check for:
- Delamination (layers separating, feels spongy underfoot)
- Water stains from past or current moisture issues
- Protruding nail heads that need setting flush
- Squeaks from loose fasteners (drive screws into joists to fix)

**Concrete subfloors** — check for:
- Moisture (tape a 2×2 piece of plastic to the slab for 24 hours — condensation underneath means a moisture problem)
- Cracks needing patching compound
- Dips exceeding 3/16 inch over 10 feet

The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) specifies subfloor material and fastening requirements.

## DIY vs. Professional

**DIY makes sense when:**
- Standard rectangular rooms, one or two at a time
- You have access to a power stretcher ($30–$50/day rental)
- Subfloor is already in good shape
- You're comfortable working on hands and knees with a utility knife

**Hire a pro when:**
- Complex room shapes, multiple doorways, or stairs
- Seams are required (invisible seaming is genuinely difficult)
- Old flooring involves [hazardous materials like asbestos](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos)
- You want the carpet warranty honored (many manufacturers require professional install)

The labor savings from DIY on a 200-square-foot room: roughly $300–$700. Whether that's worth 8–12 hours of physical work and the risk of visible seams is a personal call.

## The Installation Sequence

**1. Clear and prep.** All furniture out. Remove existing flooring and baseboards (or plan to trim at baseboards). Clean subfloor thoroughly.

**2. Install tack strips.** Nail around the room perimeter, leaving a gap of about 2/3 the carpet thickness between strip and wall. Angled tacks point toward the wall.

**3. Lay padding.** Roll out to cover the floor inside the tack strips. Staple to plywood or tape to concrete. Butt seams tight without overlapping, tape together.

**4. Cut and position carpet.** Cut 4–6 inches longer than room dimensions on each side. Position seams away from high-traffic paths and doorways.

**5. Seam if needed.** Seam tape and iron. This is the step where most DIY jobs become visible. Iron temperature, tape quality, and hand speed all matter.

**6. Stretch and attach.** Use a power stretcher — not just a knee kicker — to pull carpet taut and hook it onto tack strips. Under-stretched carpet develops ripples and wrinkles within months. This is the single most important step.

**7. Trim and tuck.** Cut excess at walls with a wall trimmer or sharp knife, tuck edges between tack strip and wall. Reinstall baseboards.

## Money-Saving Moves

**Buy carpet and pad as a package.** Many retailers discount or include padding with carpet purchases.

**Shop remnants.** Large enough for one room? Remnants sell at 30–50% off. Check flooring stores' remnant sections regularly.

**Time your purchase.** Memorial Day, Labor Day, and Black Friday consistently offer the best carpet deals. January and February are slow months when installers negotiate on labor.

**Skip upgraded pad in closets.** Standard 6-pound pad is fine inside a closet. Put the savings toward better pad in rooms your feet actually touch.

**Get at least three quotes.** The spread between high and low bids on the same job commonly exceeds 30%. Make sure each covers identical scope — removal, furniture moving, and transitions included.

## Red Flags When Hiring

- **No written estimate** — verbal quotes leave room for surprise charges
- **Pressure to start immediately** — usually signals desperation or inexperience
- **No references or past work photos** — experienced installers have portfolios
- **Suspiciously low price** — corners being cut on stretching, padding, or seaming
- **Won't discuss warranty** — installation workmanship should be guaranteed at least one year

Good carpet properly installed lasts 10–15 years in moderate traffic. Bad installation on great carpet creates problems within the first year. The installer matters as much as the material — sometimes more.
