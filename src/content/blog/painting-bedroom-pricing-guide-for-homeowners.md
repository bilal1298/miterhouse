Painting a bedroom costs between $300 and $800 for a professional job on a standard-sized room, or $50 to $150 in materials if you handle it yourself. The actual number depends on room size, ceiling height, surface condition, paint quality, and whether you include the ceiling and trim.

A bedroom is one of the most manageable painting projects for a homeowner -- limited square footage, usually straightforward geometry, and minimal moisture concerns. Here is what the costs look like broken down, and where the money actually goes.

## Professional Bedroom Painting Costs

When you hire a painter for a bedroom, here is how the numbers typically break down:

| Room Size | Walls Only | Walls + Ceiling | Walls + Ceiling + Trim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (10x10) | $250 -- $450 | $350 -- $550 | $450 -- $700 |
| Average (12x14) | $350 -- $600 | $450 -- $750 | $550 -- $900 |
| Large (14x18) | $450 -- $800 | $600 -- $1,000 | $750 -- $1,200 |
| Primary suite (16x20+) | $600 -- $1,100 | $800 -- $1,400 | $1,000 -- $1,700 |

These ranges assume 8 to 9-foot ceilings in average condition. Vaulted ceilings, extensive wall damage, or dark-to-light color changes push costs toward the higher end.

**What drives the price up:**

- Rooms with multiple windows and doors require more cutting-in time, which is slow, skilled work
- Dark existing colors need primer plus two finish coats instead of the standard two coats
- Textured walls (knockdown, orange peel) use 15-20% more paint and take longer to roll
- Closet interiors add $75 to $200 depending on size
- Accent walls in a different color add $50 to $150 for the extra setup and paint

Labor accounts for about 70-75% of a professional bedroom paint job. The actual paint and materials are a relatively small portion of the bill.

## DIY Bedroom Painting Cost Breakdown

If you have a free weekend, painting your own bedroom is one of the best return-on-effort home improvement projects. Here is what you will spend:

**Materials for one average bedroom (12x14, 8-foot ceiling, walls only):**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Paint (1.5 gallons of quality interior paint) | $40 -- $75 |
| Primer (if needed, 1 gallon) | $20 -- $35 |
| Roller frame + covers (2) | $10 -- $18 |
| Extension pole | $12 -- $20 |
| Angled sash brush (2.5") | $8 -- $15 |
| Painter's tape (2-3 rolls) | $10 -- $18 |
| Drop cloths | $8 -- $20 |
| Sandpaper + spackle for minor repairs | $5 -- $10 |
| **Total** | **$113 -- $211** |

If you already own basic painting supplies from a previous project, your cost drops to just the paint -- typically $30 to $55 per gallon for mid-range interior paint. Plan on roughly one gallon of paint per 350 to 400 square feet of wall space. A 12x14 bedroom with 8-foot ceilings has about 400 square feet of wall area (minus windows and doors), so one gallon usually covers the first coat but you will need to start a second gallon for the second coat.

## Where Paint Quality Actually Matters

The difference between a $25 gallon and a $50 gallon is not marketing. Cheap paint has less pigment, so it covers poorly and requires more coats. It also has lower-quality binders, meaning it does not hold up to cleaning and yellows faster over time.

For bedrooms, here is the practical breakdown:

- **Budget paint ($20-30/gallon):** Fine for rental properties or rooms you plan to repaint soon. Expect three coats for decent coverage.
- **Mid-range paint ($35-55/gallon):** The sweet spot for most homeowners. Good coverage in two coats, decent durability, wide color selection.
- **Premium paint ($55-80/gallon):** Superior coverage (sometimes one thick coat works), excellent washability, and longer color retention. Worth it for high-traffic bedrooms or if you want the finish to last 7-10 years.

**Finish matters too.** For bedroom walls, eggshell or satin is the standard recommendation. Flat hides imperfections better but marks easily and is harder to clean. Semi-gloss is too reflective for bedroom walls but works well on trim and doors. If you have kids or pets, lean toward satin -- it holds up better to wiping.

## The Prep Work Nobody Wants to Do

Prep takes longer than actual painting in most bedrooms, and it determines whether the result looks professional or amateur. The [EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program) are worth reviewing, especially in older homes where [lead paint](https://www.epa.gov/lead) may be present.

**Minimum prep checklist:**

1. **Move furniture out or to the center.** Do not try to paint around a bed pushed against the wall. You will drip on it, and you will miss spots behind it.
2. **Remove outlet and light switch covers.** Takes two minutes, eliminates hours of careful taping and cutting in.
3. **Fill holes and dents.** Lightweight spackle, a putty knife, and 10 minutes of drying time. Sand smooth once dry.
4. **Sand glossy surfaces lightly.** If the existing paint has a sheen, a quick scuff-sand gives the new paint something to grip.
5. **Clean the walls.** Dust and cobwebs at the top, fingerprints and scuffs at kid-height. A damp cloth works for most rooms.
6. **Tape edges.** Ceiling line, trim, window frames, door frames. Press the tape edge firmly -- loose tape lets paint bleed underneath.
7. **Lay drop cloths.** Canvas on the floor, plastic on furniture. Canvas stays put and absorbs drips. Plastic is slippery and pools paint.

## Painting Technique for Clean Results

The actual painting follows a specific order that minimizes mess and maximizes coverage:

**Step 1: Cut in edges.** Use an angled brush to paint a 2-3 inch border around all edges -- ceiling line, corners, trim, outlets. Do one wall at a time so the cut-in edge is still wet when you roll.

**Step 2: Roll the wall.** Load the roller evenly (roll it in the tray until it is covered but not dripping). Start with a W or M pattern to distribute paint, then smooth it out with long, overlapping vertical strokes. Work top to bottom.

**Step 3: Maintain a wet edge.** The key to avoiding lap marks is overlapping each section while the previous one is still wet. In a bedroom, this means doing one wall from corner to corner without stopping.

**Step 4: Second coat after full drying.** Do not rush this. Most interior paints need 2-4 hours between coats. Recoating too early traps moisture and causes peeling or bubbling.

For your first room, expect the whole process to take 6 to 8 hours spread over a day (including drying time). An experienced DIYer can paint a bedroom in 4 to 5 hours.

## How to Evaluate Professional Painting Quotes

If you decide to hire out, get three quotes and compare them carefully. A good quote should specify:

- Number of coats included
- Paint brand and product line
- Whether primer is included or extra
- What prep work is covered (patching, sanding, caulking)
- Whether the ceiling and trim are included
- Timeline (a single bedroom should take a pro team one day or less)
- Touch-up policy

Red flags in quotes: vague descriptions ("paint room"), no mention of prep work, quoting without seeing the room, or prices dramatically below the market range (they are cutting corners on paint quality or prep).

## Accent Walls and Two-Tone Bedrooms

Accent walls add visual interest without painting the entire room a bold color. From a cost perspective:

- **DIY accent wall:** One quart of accent paint ($12-20) is usually enough for one wall in a standard bedroom
- **Professional accent wall addition:** $50-150 extra on top of the base room price
- **Two-tone (wainscoting line or horizontal divide):** Adds $100-250 professionally due to the extra taping and precision required

The key with accent walls is keeping the tape line crisp. Apply tape, then brush a thin line of the base color over the tape edge first. This seals the tape so the accent color cannot bleed under it. Once dry, apply the accent color. Remove tape while the final coat is still slightly wet for the cleanest line.

## Timing and Scheduling Tips

The best conditions for bedroom painting are 50-85 degrees with moderate humidity. Air conditioning or heating running during painting helps maintain consistent drying conditions.

**Seasonal pricing:** Professional painters are busiest from late spring through early fall (exterior season). Booking interior bedroom painting during the off-season (November through February) can save 10-20% and get you faster scheduling.

**Planning around your schedule:** If you are doing it yourself, start early on a Saturday morning. You can get the first coat done by lunch, let it dry through the afternoon, and apply the second coat by late afternoon. Sleep in a different room that night and the room is usable by Sunday evening.

Bedrooms connect to the rest of the house -- if you plan to repaint hallways, [ceiling work](/blog/ceiling-painting-services-a-practical-guide/), or other rooms, doing them as one project saves setup and cleanup time.

## Bottom Line

A bedroom repaint is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost improvements you can make. A professional job on an average room runs $350 to $750. DIY brings that down to under $150 in materials, plus a weekend of your time. Either way, the prep work is what separates a job that looks fresh for years from one that starts showing problems within months. Do not skip it.
