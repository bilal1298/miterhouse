A drywall repair specialist is the person you call when a handyman's patch job is not going to cut it — when the damage is extensive, the texture needs matching, or the repair has to be genuinely invisible. Specialists in this trade typically charge $250 to $750 for residential repairs, and the premium over general handyman rates buys you finishing quality that most generalists cannot match.

Here is how to know when you need a specialist, what they bring to the table, and how to make sure you are hiring the right one.

## When a Specialist Makes Sense Over a Handyman

Most small drywall repairs do not require a specialist. A competent handyman or even a confident DIYer can handle nail holes, small anchor holes, and simple patches on smooth walls. The specialist becomes worth the money in specific situations:

**Textured walls and ceilings.** If your home has knockdown, orange peel, skip trowel, or any applied texture, matching it after a repair is genuinely difficult. Specialists have spray rigs, hopper guns, and enough practice to replicate patterns that amateurs and generalists struggle with.

**Visible, high-traffic areas.** A living room wall at eye level is not the same as a closet interior. If the repair will be seen daily in good lighting, the finishing quality matters more.

**Water damage requiring section replacement.** Cutting out damaged drywall, checking for mold and framing issues, properly installing new sheets, and finishing the seams so they disappear — this is a multi-step process where each step depends on the one before it.

**Ceiling repairs.** Working overhead magnifies every difficulty. Compound is heavier to work against gravity, finishing marks are more visible (light hits ceilings differently), and texture matching on ceilings is harder than on walls.

**Plaster-over-drywall situations.** Some homes have a thin plaster skim coat over drywall. Repairing these surfaces requires understanding both materials and how they interact.

## What a Drywall Specialist Does Differently

The visible difference between amateur and specialist work comes down to a few critical techniques.

### Compound Application

A specialist applies joint compound in controlled, consistent strokes. Each pass lays down a uniform thickness, and each coat extends beyond the previous one to create a gradual transition (called feathering). The final skim coat may extend 10 to 14 inches beyond the repair on each side.

An amateur tends to apply too much compound, leave tool marks, and not feather wide enough. The result is a visible hump or ridge at the repair boundary.

### Sanding Technique

Specialists sand methodically with the right grits — usually 120-grit for initial coats and 150 to 220-grit for the final coat. They use sanding lights (bright lights held at low angles) to check their work in real-time, catching imperfections before priming reveals them.

Many specialists now use dustless or near-dustless sanding systems that connect to vacuums, reducing the mess significantly. This alone can be worth the premium over a handyman who sands by hand and leaves your home coated in white dust.

### Texture Matching

This is where specialists truly earn their fees. Matching an existing wall texture requires:

1. Identifying the original texture type and application method
2. Mixing compound to the right consistency for the pattern
3. Applying with the right equipment (spray rig, hopper gun, roller, or hand tools)
4. Timing the knockdown correctly (for knockdown textures)
5. Adjusting for the fact that new texture dries slightly differently than aged texture

A specialist who has done this hundreds of times will get it right in one pass. A generalist may take multiple attempts and still not match perfectly.

## Cost Breakdown: What Specialists Charge

Drywall repair specialists typically price their work one of three ways:

**Per-patch pricing** is most common for individual repairs:

| Repair Type | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Small patch (under 4 in) | $100–$175 |
| Medium patch (4–12 in) | $175–$325 |
| Large section (1–4 sq ft) | $300–$550 |
| Full sheet replacement | $400–$750 |
| Ceiling patch with texture | $250–$500 |

**Hourly pricing** is used for multi-repair jobs or exploratory work: $65 to $100 per hour.

**Project-based pricing** applies to larger scopes like full room repairs after water damage: $800 to $2,500+ depending on the extent.

Most specialists have a minimum service call of $150 to $250, which covers one to two patches. Bundling multiple repairs into one visit gives you better per-repair economics.

## Finding the Right Specialist

Drywall finishing is a niche skill. Here is how to find someone genuinely qualified rather than a general contractor who dabbles.

**Ask to see recent work.** A real specialist will have photos or can point you to recent jobs. Ideally, ask to see completed repairs in person — photos do not show whether a repair is truly invisible.

**Request texture samples.** For textured wall repairs, a good specialist will offer to do a test patch on a piece of scrap drywall or in an inconspicuous area to confirm the match before proceeding.

**Check for VOC awareness.** Modern joint compounds and primers come in low-VOC formulations. The [EPA provides guidance on volatile organic compounds and indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) that any indoor construction professional should be familiar with.

**Verify insurance and licensing.** At minimum, general liability insurance. In states that require contractor licensing, verify the license is current. [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/) compliance matters too — a specialist working in your home should follow safety standards for dust control and scaffolding.

**Lead paint certification.** For homes built before 1978, the [EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Program](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program) requires contractors to be RRP-certified. A specialist working in older homes should have this certification.

## The Repair Appointment: What to Expect

A typical specialist visit follows this pattern:

**Initial walkthrough (15 to 30 minutes).** The specialist examines all damage areas, asks about the cause, checks for moisture or structural concerns, and discusses the scope. This is when they determine pricing.

**Prep (30 to 60 minutes).** Drop cloths go down, furniture moves back from walls, and plastic sheeting goes up if sanding will be involved. The damaged areas get cut back and cleaned.

**First coat (30 to 60 minutes).** Depending on the repair type, the first coat of compound goes on. For large patches, new drywall sections are installed first.

**Dry time (12 to 24 hours).** This is the unavoidable waiting period. Some specialists schedule the first coat on day one and return the next day for the second coat and finishing. Others use setting-type compound for the first coat (which dries in 20 to 90 minutes) and regular compound for the finish coats, completing the job in a single long visit.

**Finish coats and sanding (1 to 3 hours).** The second and possibly third coats go on, each sanded smooth. Texture is applied if needed.

**Priming (30 minutes).** The repaired area gets primed. The specialist typically leaves painting to you or your painter.

## After the Repair: Painting Tips

Once the specialist finishes, you will have a primed patch ready for paint. A few tips for a seamless final result:

Paint the entire wall, not just the patch. Even with primer, spot-painting almost always shows — the new paint will not match the aged, faded surrounding paint exactly.

Use the same sheen as the existing paint. Flat paint is the most forgiving for hiding imperfections. Eggshell and satin show more. If you are not sure what sheen is on your walls, a paint store can help you match it.

Apply two coats of finish paint over the primer. One coat rarely provides complete coverage, and the patch area may absorb slightly more paint than the surrounding surface.
