A water heater making noise is trying to tell you something. The specific sound --- popping, rumbling, screeching, hammering, or hissing --- points directly to the cause. Most of these noises are fixable without calling a plumber, but a few signal problems that need professional attention before they become expensive or dangerous.

Here is a sound-by-sound breakdown of what your water heater is actually saying.

## Popping and Crackling Sounds

This is the most common water heater noise, and it almost always means sediment buildup.

Minerals in your water supply (calcium, magnesium, and lime) settle to the bottom of the tank over time. As the burner heats the water beneath this sediment layer, steam bubbles form and pop through the mineral crust. The result is that distinctive popping or crackling you hear during heating cycles.

**How serious is it?** Moderately. The sediment itself is not dangerous, but it insulates the bottom of the tank from the burner, forcing the system to work harder. This raises energy bills by 10 to 25 percent and accelerates tank corrosion. Left untreated for years, it can shorten the heater's lifespan significantly.

**The fix:** Flush the tank. Connect a garden hose to the drain valve at the bottom, route it to a floor drain or outside, and open the valve. Let it run until the water comes out clear. This takes 15 to 30 minutes and costs nothing.

For detailed flushing instructions, see our guide on [how often to flush a hot water heater](/blog/how-often-to-flush-hot-water-heater-a-diyers-honest-take/). The short version: do this once a year, and you will likely never hear popping again.

If the sediment has been building for many years, a simple flush may not clear it all. In severe cases, you may need to remove the drain valve entirely and use a long brush or shop vac to break up the hardened mineral deposits. That is still a DIY job, but it is messier and takes 1 to 2 hours.

## Rumbling or Low Groaning

A deep rumble during heating cycles is closely related to popping --- it is sediment at a more advanced stage. When enough mineral buildup coats the tank bottom, the entire heated water mass churns and rumbles as steam works through the deposits.

**How serious is it?** More concerning than popping. Heavy sediment buildup means the tank has been working overtime for a while. Check the age of the unit. If it is over 10 years old with significant rumbling, start budgeting for replacement even if a flush temporarily quiets it.

**The fix:** Same as above --- flush the tank. If rumbling persists after flushing, the sediment may be too hardened to remove without physical agitation. At that point, a plumber can assess whether the tank interior is still in serviceable condition or whether replacement makes more sense.

## Screeching, Screaming, or High-Pitched Whining

A high-pitched noise, especially one that occurs when a hot water tap is opened somewhere in the house, typically points to a restricted valve.

The most common culprit is the inlet valve on top of the water heater not being fully open. When a valve is partially closed, water forces through the narrow gap at high velocity, creating that screech. Think of it like partially covering a garden hose nozzle with your thumb.

**How serious is it?** Not very, if it is just a valve position issue. Potentially more serious if the noise is coming from inside the tank, which could indicate a failing check valve or a buildup restricting the dip tube.

**The fix:** First, check every valve between the main water supply and the heater. Make sure the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater is fully open (handle parallel to the pipe). Also check any shut-off valves upstream.

If all valves are fully open and the noise persists, the issue may be the [water heater pipe connections](/blog/water-heater-pipe-size-what-homeowners-should-know/) themselves. Undersized pipes or corroded galvanized fittings can create similar noises. A plumber can diagnose this with a pressure test.

## Hammering or Banging (Water Hammer)

A loud bang or series of bangs when a hot water faucet shuts off is water hammer. It is not actually a water heater problem --- it is a plumbing system problem that manifests at the heater because that is where the pressure wave terminates.

Water hammer occurs when fast-closing valves (like single-lever faucets, dishwashers, or washing machines) abruptly stop water flow. The momentum of the moving water creates a pressure spike that reverberates through the pipes.

**How serious is it?** Annoying and potentially damaging over time. Repeated water hammer can loosen pipe joints, damage fittings, and stress the water heater connections. The pressure spikes can also trigger the T&P (temperature and pressure) relief valve to weep or discharge.

**The fix:** Install water hammer arrestors at the fixtures causing the problem. These are small devices with an air-filled chamber that absorbs the pressure spike. They cost $10 to $25 each and screw onto the supply line behind the offending fixture.

For whole-house solutions, a thermal expansion tank on the cold water inlet (if your system does not already have one) helps absorb pressure fluctuations. These cost $30 to $50 and are code-required in many jurisdictions for closed-loop systems.

If your [pop-off valve is leaking](/blog/pop-off-valve-water-heater-leaking-honest-advice-for-your-home/) in conjunction with water hammer, the two issues are likely connected. Address the water hammer first.

## Hissing or Sizzling

A hissing sound from a gas water heater during heating is often normal --- it is condensation dripping onto the hot burner. When cold water enters the tank and the burner fires, moisture from combustion gases can condense on the tank bottom and sizzle on the burner.

**When it is normal:** Brief hissing during the first few minutes of a heating cycle, especially in colder weather or after heavy hot water use.

**When it is not normal:** Persistent hissing, hissing accompanied by a wet spot or puddle, or hissing from electric water heaters (which should never hiss). These can indicate:

- A small leak at a fitting, dripping onto the burner
- A crack in the tank itself
- A failing [T&P valve](/blog/water-heater-blow-off-valve-leaking-a-practical-guide/) slowly releasing steam

**The fix:** For condensation hissing, nothing needs to be done. For persistent hissing, inspect every connection and the tank surface for moisture. If you find a leak at a fitting, tightening or replacing the fitting may solve it. If the tank itself is leaking, replacement is the only option.

## Ticking or Tapping

Rhythmic ticking, especially when the heater is warming up or cooling down, is almost always thermal expansion. As the pipes and tank heat up, they expand slightly. As they cool, they contract. If a pipe is passing through a tight hole in a joist or bracket, the movement creates an audible tick.

**How serious is it?** Not at all. This is a cosmetic noise, not a functional problem.

**The fix:** If it bothers you, locate where the pipe passes through framing and add a foam pipe insulator sleeve at that point. This cushions the movement and eliminates the noise. A 6-foot foam sleeve costs about $3.

## Humming (Electric Heaters Only)

A low electrical hum from an electric water heater is the heating element vibrating at 60 Hz (the frequency of household current). This is relatively common and usually harmless.

**How serious is it?** Minimal. It indicates the element is slightly loose in its mounting.

**The fix:** Turn off the breaker, then use an element wrench to tighten the heating element slightly. A quarter turn is usually enough. Do not overtighten --- the threads are in the tank wall, and stripping them is a much bigger problem than a little humming.

## When Noise Means Replacement

A noisy water heater is not automatically a dying water heater, but combine noise with these other signs and replacement becomes the smart call:

- Unit is over 12 years old
- Rusty water from hot taps only
- Multiple noises occurring simultaneously
- Noise persists after flushing and valve checks
- Visible corrosion on tank or fittings
- Recurring need for [emergency repairs](/blog/24-hour-water-heater-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/)

The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) sets the standards for water heater installation, and a replacement is a good opportunity to bring an older installation up to current code.

## The Prevention Plan

Most water heater noises are preventable with basic annual maintenance:

1. Flush the tank once a year (15 minutes, free)
2. Test the T&P valve once a year (30 seconds, free)
3. Check the anode rod every 3 years (30 minutes, $20-40 for replacement)
4. Inspect all visible connections and valves for leaks (5 minutes, free)
5. Listen for new sounds and investigate promptly (they rarely improve on their own)

A water heater that runs quietly is a water heater that is working properly. Take the noise seriously, diagnose it correctly, and most of the time the fix is simple and cheap.
