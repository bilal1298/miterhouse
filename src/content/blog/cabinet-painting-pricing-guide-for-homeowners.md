Cabinet painting costs $3,000–$7,000 for a professional job on a standard kitchen, or $200–$500 in materials to do it yourself. Before you commit to a [full kitchen overhaul](/blog/80s-kitchen-remodel-honest-advice-for-your-home/), a fresh coat on structurally sound cabinets may be all you need. It's the highest-impact, lowest-cost kitchen update available — and the gap between what you pay and what it looks like is absurd.

## What Drives the Price

### Piece Count

Professionals price by the piece — each door, drawer front, and end panel. Cabinet boxes are a lump sum.

- **Small kitchen (10–15 doors/drawers):** $1,800–$3,500
- **Medium kitchen (20–30 doors/drawers):** $3,000–$6,000
- **Large kitchen (35+ doors/drawers):** $5,000–$10,000

Per-piece pricing runs $50–$100 per door or drawer front. Pantry doors cost more. Small drawer fronts cost less.

### Cabinet Condition

Smooth, intact finishes are straightforward. Peeling veneer, grease buildup, or previous bad paint jobs add $500–$1,500 for extra prep: stripping old paint, repairing veneer delamination, filling gouges, and re-profiling damaged door details.

### Paint Quality

**Standard acrylic-alkyd ($40–$60/gallon):** Benjamin Moore Advance or Sherwin-Williams ProClassic. Self-leveling, durable. Two gallons covers most kitchens. The sweet spot for most projects.

**Premium hybrid ($55–$80/gallon):** Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel. Harder finish, better adhesion, faster recoat. Worth the upgrade for dark colors where finish quality is critical.

**Professional lacquer/conversion varnish ($80–$150/gallon):** Spray-booth application only. Hardest, most durable finish available. Factory-smooth look. What high-end cabinet shops use.

### Regional Variation

Major metros (NYC, LA, SF, Chicago): 20–40% above national average. Mid-size cities and suburbs: roughly average. Rural areas: 10–20% below average but fewer specialists. Materials cost the same everywhere — labor drives the gap.

## DIY vs. Professional vs. Hybrid

**DIY saves serious money.** Materials for a full kitchen: $200–$500. Professional quote for the same kitchen: $3,000–$7,000. The gap is entirely labor, because cabinet painting is extremely labor-intensive.

**But finish quality separates the two.** A professional spraying doors in a dust-free booth produces a flawless surface that's nearly impossible to replicate in a garage with a rented sprayer. The biggest DIY problems: brush marks on flat panels, orange peel from bad spray technique, dust nibs from particles landing in wet paint, and adhesion failure from skipped prep.

If you're going white or light neutral, imperfections are less visible. Going dark (navy, charcoal, black)? Every flaw shows. Match your color choice to your skill level.

**The hybrid approach splits the difference.** Paint the cabinet boxes yourself and send doors to a professional spray shop. Saves 30–40% compared to full professional service while getting the factory finish on the most visible parts.

Professional door-only spray service: $40–$80 per door including prep and two coats. For a 25-door kitchen: $1,000–$2,000 for the doors, plus your labor on the boxes.

## Prep That Cannot Be Skipped

### Degreasing

Kitchen grease is invisible and everywhere. Years of cooking deposits a film on every surface, especially upper cabinets near the range. TSP (trisodium phosphate) solution works. Scrub every surface, rinse with clean water, dry completely.

If the home was built before 1978, test for lead paint before sanding. The [EPA's lead safety guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/lead) apply.

### Sanding

120-grit sandpaper or sanding sponge on every surface. You're creating tooth for the primer — not removing the existing finish. Sand with the grain. Wipe all dust with a tack cloth.

For slick factory finishes (thermofoil, high-gloss lacquer), use liquid deglosser before sanding to give the sandpaper something to grip.

### Priming

Non-negotiable. A bonding primer (shellac-based Zinsser BIN, or a dedicated cabinet primer like Stix) bridges the old surface and the new paint. Skip it, and even the best paint chips.

One coat of primer, lightly sanded with 220-grit after it cures, gives you a surface that paint bonds to permanently.

## The Painting Process

**Brush and roll:**
1. Paint cabinet boxes first — they're more forgiving since doors cover most of the frame
2. Small foam roller for flat areas, quality 2-inch angled brush for edges and profiles
3. Thin coats. Two thin beats one thick every time
4. Full dry time between coats (most cabinet paints need 16–24 hours)
5. Paint door fronts flat on sawhorses. Backs first, dry, flip, then fronts

**Spray:**
1. Set up a spray area in a garage or covered outdoor space
2. HVLP sprayer at manufacturer-recommended PSI and tip size
3. Keep the gun 8–12 inches from the surface at consistent speed
4. 2–3 light coats, not one heavy coat
5. Dry between coats per product spec

## Timeline

| Phase | Professional | DIY |
|---|---|---|
| Prep (clean, sand, prime) | 1–2 days | 2–3 weekends |
| Painting | 2–3 days | 2–3 weekends |
| Cure before reinstall | 3–7 days | 7–14 days |
| Reinstall and adjust | Half day | Half day |
| **Total** | **1–2 weeks** | **3–5 weeks** |

During the project, your kitchen is functional but unpleasant — no doors on cabinets, tape and drop cloths everywhere, paint smell. Plan meals accordingly.

## Bathroom Cabinets

Bathroom vanities are simpler — 2–6 doors and 2–4 drawers. But moisture adds a wrinkle:

- Use moisture-resistant primer
- Semi-gloss or high-gloss finish (better moisture resistance than satin)
- Extra attention around the sink where water splashes
- Ensure good ventilation after painting — humidity interferes with cure

Professional bathroom vanity painting: $300–$1,000. DIY: $50–$150 in materials.

## Making It Last

A well-done cabinet paint job lasts 8–15 years. What determines longevity:

- **Prep quality** — proper cleaning, sanding, and priming prevent peeling
- **Paint quality** — cabinet-grade paints resist chipping and yellowing
- **Cure time** — rushing reinstall before full hardening causes sticking and permanent fingerprints
- **Soft-close hinges** — reduce door slamming that chips paint at frame contact points. If you're painting cabinets, spending $3–$5 per hinge on soft-close upgrades is one of the best investments you can make

Keep a small jar of the original paint for touch-ups. A small artist's brush handles chips before they become eyesores.
