The average plumbing service call costs $150 to $450, and about half of those calls are for problems a homeowner could fix in under an hour with $20 in parts. The other half involve work that genuinely requires a licensed professional, specialized tools, or permits. The trick is knowing which half you are looking at before you start.

Here is a practical framework for deciding when to service and repair your plumbing yourself versus when a professional is the smarter investment.

## The Decision Framework

Ask yourself three questions before touching any plumbing repair:

**1. Can I shut off the water to this fixture (or the whole house) if something goes wrong?**
If yes, your risk is limited. If no -- for example, the shutoff valve is seized or there is no individual shutoff -- fix that first or call a plumber.

**2. Is the repair visible and accessible?**
Under-sink work, exposed basement pipes, and fixture-level repairs are fair game. Behind-wall, under-slab, and in-ceiling repairs usually need professional equipment and demolition.

**3. Does local code require a permit or licensed work?**
Gas lines, main sewer lines, water heater installations, and new plumbing runs almost always require permits. Work done without permits can cause problems at resale and may void your insurance.

If all three answers favor DIY, go for it. If any of them point toward professional work, get quotes.

## What You Should Absolutely DIY

These repairs are straightforward, low-risk, and overpriced when you hire them out.

### Toilet Internals

Running toilets, weak flushes, and phantom fills are almost always caused by a worn flapper, a faulty fill valve, or a corroded flush valve seat. Universal repair kits cover all three for $12 to $20. The entire repair takes 15 to 30 minutes and requires no tools.

A plumber charges $125 to $250 for this same fix. That is a $100+ premium for a job that involves no risk and no skill beyond following instructions.

### Faucet Repairs

Dripping faucets waste 3,000 or more gallons per year. The fix is usually a cartridge, O-ring, or seat replacement specific to your faucet brand. Parts run $8 to $30. The swap takes 15 to 45 minutes. Search your faucet model number on YouTube for an exact walkthrough.

### Drain Cleaning

Chemical drain cleaners are expensive, corrosive to pipes, and often ineffective. A $25 drain snake handles most clogs, and a $10 plunger handles the rest. For bathroom drains, the clog is almost always hair at the stopper or in the P-trap. Remove, clean, reassemble.

### Supply Line Replacement

The braided steel hoses connecting your faucets, toilets, and appliances should be replaced preventively every 8 to 10 years. Each one costs $5 to $15 and installs with a wrench. This is one of the most cost-effective preventive measures in your entire house.

### Garbage Disposal Jams

When a disposal hums but does not spin, it is jammed. The Allen wrench socket on the bottom lets you manually rotate the flywheel to free it. This is a 2-minute fix that plumbers charge a full service call for.

## Where Professionals Earn Their Fee

These are situations where DIY either creates more problems than it solves or carries genuine safety risks.

### Sewer Line Diagnosis and Repair

If multiple drains are backing up simultaneously, the problem is in your main sewer line. Diagnosis requires a camera inspection ($150 to $300), and repairs may involve hydro-jetting ($300 to $600), lining ($4,000 to $8,000), or excavation ($3,000 to $15,000). This is heavy equipment work that requires permits and must comply with [building codes](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/).

### Water Heater Service

Routine maintenance like flushing sediment or replacing an anode rod is DIY-friendly. But gas valve replacement, thermocouple work, and new installations involve gas connections and venting that require licensed work. Our guide on [water heater installation](/blog/installing-tank-water-heater-what-to-know-first/) covers the complete scope.

### Hidden Leaks

A leak behind a wall presents two problems: finding it and fixing it. A plumber with an acoustic leak detector or thermal camera can pinpoint the location without opening every wall. The repair itself may be simple, but the diagnosis requires equipment you do not own.

### Gas Line Work

No exceptions here. Gas line work requires a licensed plumber or gas fitter. The [NFPA fire code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) and local building codes govern gas installations because failures can be fatal. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your utility company.

### Repiping

If your home has galvanized pipes built before 1960 or polybutylene pipes from the 1980s and early 1990s, a whole-house repipe is eventually necessary. This is a $4,000 to $15,000 project that affects every wall in the house. It needs proper planning, permits, and a crew that can work efficiently to minimize the time your house is torn up.

## The Real Cost Comparison

Here is what common plumbing repairs actually cost, DIY versus professional:

| Repair | DIY Cost | Pro Cost | DIY Time |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| Running toilet | $12 - $20 | $125 - $250 | 20 min |
| Dripping faucet | $8 - $30 | $150 - $300 | 30 min |
| Clogged drain | $0 - $25 | $150 - $300 | 15 min |
| Faucet replacement | $80 - $250 | $250 - $500 | 1 - 2 hrs |
| Toilet replacement | $100 - $350 | $300 - $600 | 1 - 2 hrs |
| Supply valve replacement | $8 - $15 | $100 - $200 | 30 min |
| Water heater flush | $0 | $100 - $200 | 30 min |

On the DIY-friendly repairs, you save $100 to $300 per job. Over a decade of homeownership, that adds up to thousands.

## How to Get Good Service When You Do Hire

Not all plumbers are equal, and the worst time to find a good one is during an emergency at midnight.

**Build your contact list now.** Ask neighbors, check local review sites, and ask at your local independent hardware store (not the big box -- the local guys know who does good work).

**Get three written quotes** for any job over $300. Be skeptical of the lowest bid -- it often means the plumber is not including something, will upsell during the job, or is cutting corners.

**Verify licensing and insurance.** Your state licensing board has a lookup tool. If a plumber cannot give you a license number, walk away.

**Ask about warranty.** Reputable plumbers warranty their work for at least one year. Some offer two years or more on larger jobs.

**Get it in writing.** A verbal quote means nothing when the invoice arrives. A written estimate that specifies the scope of work, materials, and price protects both parties.

## Stocking Your Plumbing Toolkit

For the repairs that are worth DIY-ing, keep these basics on hand:

- Two adjustable wrenches (8-inch and 12-inch)
- Channel-lock pliers
- Basin wrench for under-sink work
- Plunger (flange style for toilets)
- 25-foot hand drain snake
- Teflon tape
- Pipe thread compound
- Spare wax ring and closet bolts
- Universal toilet repair kit

Total investment: $100 to $150. You will use every one of these tools within the first year of homeownership.
