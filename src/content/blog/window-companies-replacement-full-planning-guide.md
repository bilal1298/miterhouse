Choosing a window replacement company is the single biggest decision in a window project, and it is where most homeowners either save thousands or overspend dramatically. A whole-house window replacement (15-20 windows) typically costs between $8,000 and $25,000 installed, but quotes from different companies for the same house can vary by $10,000 or more.

That price range is not mainly about the windows themselves. It reflects wildly different business models, overhead structures, and sales tactics across the industry. Understanding those differences is how you get quality windows at a fair price.

## The Three Types of Window Replacement Companies

Window companies fall into three broad categories, and each comes with different pricing dynamics:

### National Brands (Renewal by Andersen, Pella, Window World, etc.)

**Typical pricing:** $800-$1,800 per window installed.

These companies manufacture their own windows and handle installation with either in-house crews or dedicated subcontractors. They invest heavily in marketing and employ commissioned salespeople who visit your home for a presentation.

**Advantages:** Consistent product quality, robust warranties (often lifetime on glass and 20+ years on frames), one company handles everything from measurement to installation to warranty claims.

**Watch out for:** High-pressure sales tactics. The "this price is only good today" approach is standard. Initial quotes are often inflated so the "discount" feels generous. The actual price after negotiation is usually 30-50% below the first number they show you. Never accept the first offer in a single-appointment visit.

### Local/Regional Window Installers

**Typical pricing:** $400-$1,000 per window installed.

These companies buy windows from manufacturers (often the same factories that supply the national brands) and install them with their own crews. They operate with lower overhead — no national advertising budget, no commissioned salesforce, no showroom in every mall.

**Advantages:** Often the best value. You can choose from multiple manufacturers and product lines. The owner or manager is usually directly involved in the work. References are local and verifiable.

**Watch out for:** Quality varies widely. Some are excellent craftsmen who have been doing this for decades. Others are general contractors who dabble in windows. Check reviews, ask for references from jobs completed 2-3 years ago (so you can verify long-term quality), and confirm they carry proper insurance and licensing.

### General Contractors and Handymen

**Typical pricing:** $300-$700 per window installed.

These are not window specialists, but many general contractors and skilled handymen handle window replacements competently.

**Advantages:** Lowest cost, especially if they are already doing other work on your home.

**Watch out for:** Window installation technique matters. Proper flashing, insulation around the frame, and shimming for level and plumb are critical for preventing water intrusion and air leaks. A contractor who does not specialize in windows may skip steps that a dedicated window company would not. Warranties are typically limited to workmanship for 1-2 years.

## What Goes Into the Price Per Window

Understanding the cost components helps you evaluate whether a quote is fair:

**The window itself: $150-$800 per unit.** This is the manufacturer's cost for the window. Vinyl double-hung windows in standard sizes start around $150. Fiberglass and wood-clad windows run $400-$800+. Triple-pane glass, specialty coatings, and non-standard sizes add cost.

**Installation labor: $100-$300 per window.** Retrofit (insert) installation, where the new window fits inside the existing frame, is faster and cheaper. Full-frame replacement, where the old frame is removed down to the rough opening, costs more but allows for proper inspection of the framing and flashing underneath.

**Trim and finishing: $50-$150 per window.** Interior trim (casing, stools, aprons) and exterior trim (capping, caulking, brickmould) complete the installation. Some companies include this; others charge extra.

**Overhead and profit: 30-60% markup.** Every company adds margin for overhead (vehicles, insurance, office, marketing) and profit. The difference between a $500/window company and a $1,200/window company is mostly this markup, not the window quality.

## How to Compare Quotes Effectively

Getting apples-to-apples quotes requires specificity. Ask each company to provide:

1. **Exact window brand and model number.** "Vinyl double-hung" is not specific enough. Model numbers let you compare the actual product.
2. **Glass specification.** Low-E coating, argon fill, double vs. triple pane. These affect energy performance and cost.
3. **Installation method.** Retrofit/insert vs. full-frame replacement. Different levels of work.
4. **What's included.** Interior trim, exterior capping, old window disposal, screen replacement, permits if required.
5. **Warranty terms.** What is covered, for how long, and by whom (manufacturer warranty vs. installer warranty).

The [International Residential Code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) sets minimum requirements for window energy performance, structural integrity, and egress (minimum opening size for bedrooms). Any reputable company should be installing windows that meet or exceed these standards.

## Red Flags That Save You Thousands

Years of homeowner feedback have surfaced consistent warning signs:

**"This price is only good today."** Walk away. Any company that will not honor a quote for at least 30 days is relying on pressure, not value, to close the sale.

**Quoting without measuring.** A reliable company sends a measurer before quoting. Each window needs exact dimensions. Ballpark quotes based on "standard sizes" lead to expensive change orders.

**No physical address or showroom.** Not every good company has a showroom, but they should have a verifiable business address, proper licensing, and insurance certificates they can share.

**Massive upfront deposits.** 10-20% to order materials is reasonable. 50% or more before work begins is a risk you should not take. Structure payments so the final payment happens after installation is complete and inspected.

**Vague warranty language.** "Lifetime warranty" means nothing without specifics. Lifetime of what — the window, the building, or the original purchaser's ownership? What is covered — glass seal failure, hardware, frame warping, installation defects? Get it in writing.

## Retrofit vs. Full-Frame Replacement

This is the biggest technical decision in a window project, and it significantly affects cost:

**Retrofit (insert) installation ($400-$900/window):**
The existing window frame stays in place. The new window is sized to fit inside it. Faster installation (30-45 minutes per window), less disruption, lower cost. Works well when the existing frame is in good condition — solid, square, and not rotted.

**Full-frame replacement ($700-$1,500+/window):**
The entire old window, including the frame, is removed down to the rough opening in the wall. This allows inspection of the framing, proper installation of housewrap and flashing, and fresh insulation. Takes 1-2 hours per window. Necessary when frames are rotted, out of square, or when you want to change the window size.

My recommendation: if your home is older than 30 years or you see any signs of water damage around window frames, full-frame replacement is worth the extra cost. It is the only way to know what is happening behind the frame, and hidden rot only gets worse over time.

## Energy Savings: Realistic Expectations

Window companies love to quote dramatic energy savings. The reality is more modest:

Replacing single-pane windows with modern double-pane Low-E windows typically reduces heating and cooling costs by 10-25% in most climates. For a home spending $2,400 per year on energy, that is $240-$600 in annual savings.

At $15,000 for a whole-house replacement, the payback period on energy savings alone is 25-60 years. Windows are rarely a good pure financial investment based on energy savings. Replace them for comfort (no more drafts), noise reduction, aesthetics, and because degraded windows cause water damage that costs far more to repair than the windows themselves.

The [EPA WaterSense program](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) focuses on water efficiency, but the Energy Star program (same parent agency) certifies windows for energy performance. Look for the Energy Star label as a baseline for energy-efficient glass.

## The Contract Checklist

Before signing, make sure the contract includes:

- [ ] Exact window specifications (brand, model, size, glass package)
- [ ] Installation method (retrofit vs. full-frame)
- [ ] What happens if hidden damage is found during installation (price for additional framing repair)
- [ ] Timeline with start date and estimated completion
- [ ] Payment schedule (with a meaningful final payment after completion)
- [ ] Cleanup and disposal of old windows included
- [ ] Manufacturer warranty details
- [ ] Installer workmanship warranty details
- [ ] Permit responsibility (who pulls permits if required)
- [ ] Interior and exterior trim scope

If your home has a basement with windows that need attention, the scope may overlap with [basement finishing considerations](/blog/basement-finishing-costs-what-homeowners-should-know/) — particularly egress window requirements for any bedroom space below grade.

## Bottom Line

Get three quotes minimum, compare them on specifics not just price, and do not let sales pressure drive your decision. A quality local installer with good references will almost always deliver better value than a national brand with heavy marketing. Budget $500-$1,000 per window for a solid mid-range replacement with professional installation, and push for full-frame replacement on any windows showing signs of age or water damage.

The windows themselves are a 20-30 year purchase. The installation quality determines whether you get the full life out of them. Prioritize the installer as much as the product.
