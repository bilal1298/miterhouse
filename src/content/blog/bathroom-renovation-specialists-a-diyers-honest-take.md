A contractor who does nothing but bathrooms will finish your remodel in 2–3 weeks where a general contractor quotes 3–4. They'll nail the waterproofing, the tile layout, and the fixture rough-in because that's all they do, every day. But the moment your project expands beyond the bathroom — a wall comes down, a linen closet gets added from the hallway — the specialist starts subcontracting work they don't regularly handle. And that's where the decision gets interesting.

## What Makes a Specialist Different

**Speed.** Bathroom specialists do the same scope repeatedly. Their process is standardized, their material ordering is dialed in, and their crews don't waste time figuring out sequencing. That efficiency often saves a full week compared to a generalist.

**Tighter pricing (sometimes).** Specialists buy fixtures, tile, and vanities in volume and maintain supplier relationships. Some pass that volume pricing to you. But premium specialists also charge more because expertise commands a premium — don't assume specialist automatically means cheaper.

**Deeper bathroom-specific knowledge.** Waterproofing, shower pan construction, tile layout for proper drainage, ventilation requirements, fixture rough-in specs — a specialist handles these daily. A generalist encounters them a few times a year.

**Less flexibility when scope grows.** Want to relocate a wall into the adjacent bedroom or add a hallway linen closet? A specialist may need to bring in subs they don't regularly work with. A general contractor absorbs scope expansion more naturally.

## Three Types of Bathroom Specialists

### Design-Build Bathroom Companies

Handle everything from design through construction. They employ or partner with a bathroom designer who selects fixtures, tile, layout, and finishes before construction starts. Projects start at $15,000–$20,000 for a guest bath, $25,000–$50,000+ for a primary.

**Best when:** You want a single point of contact and are changing the layout or doing a complete gut. Worth the design fee if you're not confident making finish selections on your own.

### Production Remodelers

Companies like Bath Fitter and Re-Bath specialize in quick, standardized updates. They install manufactured tub/shower surrounds and vanities using proprietary systems. Projects often complete in 1–3 days.

**Costs:** $5,000–$15,000 for a tub-to-shower conversion or surround replacement. $8,000–$20,000 for a more complete update with vanity and flooring.

**Best when:** You prioritize speed and simplicity over customization. Results are clean and functional but limited in material and design options.

**Watch out for:** High-pressure sales tactics (rampant in this segment) and per-linear-foot pricing that can exceed custom tile work in larger spaces. Always get the total project price in writing before signing anything.

### Independent Tile and Bath Specialists

Solo operators or small crews (2–3 people) who specialize in tile work, shower construction, and bathroom installations. Often the most skilled tilework craftspeople you'll find.

**Costs:** Labor at $40–$75/hour, or project bids. A shower surround in subway tile: $2,000–$4,000 labor. A full bathroom tile job (floor + shower + backsplash): $3,000–$8,000 labor.

**Best when:** You've already selected your own materials and want expert installation. Excellent for shower/tub areas where waterproofing and tile are the primary concerns.

## Cost by Scope

| Scope | Cost Range | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh (paint, hardware, fixtures, mirror) | $1,000–$5,000 | 1–3 days |
| Surface update (new vanity, countertop, toilet, faucets) | $5,000–$12,000 | 3–7 days |
| Standard renovation (gut and rebuild) | $12,000–$25,000 | 2–4 weeks |
| Premium renovation (custom tile, layout changes) | $25,000–$50,000+ | 4–8 weeks |

The single biggest cost variable: whether you move plumbing. Relocating a toilet adds $1,000–$3,000. Moving a shower adds $1,500–$4,000 in plumbing alone. Keep the existing layout and replace everything in place — you'll save thousands.

## How to Vet a Specialist

### The Waterproofing Test

Ask how they handle shower waterproofing. A good answer includes specific product names — Schluter-KERDI, Laticrete Hydro Ban, Custom Building Products RedGard — and a description of process. A vague "we make sure it's waterproof" suggests insufficient attention to the most critical part of bathroom construction.

### The Tile Layout Test

Ask where they start tiling. How do they handle the last row at the ceiling? What's their approach to cuts at corners? Specialists who think through these details before starting deliver noticeably better results.

### Licensing and Insurance

Check that the contractor holds the appropriate license for your state. General liability should be $1 million minimum. Workers' comp is required in most states for companies with employees. Ask for certificates and verify directly with the insurer — some contractors provide outdated or falsified documents.

### Portfolio and References

A specialist should have dozens of completed bathroom projects. Ask for photos of at least 5 similar projects, contact info for 3 recent clients (within 6 months), and permission to visit a completed project. When calling references, ask about timeline accuracy, communication, how they handled surprises, and cleanup quality.

The [EPA's WaterSense program](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) certifies water-efficient fixtures. A specialist who recommends WaterSense products signals awareness of current standards and potential utility rebates.

## What You Can Handle Yourself

Even during a specialist renovation, these tasks cut costs:

**Demo ($500–$1,500 saved).** Removing old vanities, toilets, flooring, and non-structural drywall is straightforward. Wear a respirator, protect adjacent floors, and cut the water before touching fixtures.

**Painting ($200–$500 saved).** If the specialist isn't tiling every wall, handle the paint yourself after dusty work wraps up.

**Accessories ($150–$400 saved).** Towel bars, toilet paper holders, robe hooks, and mirrors go in after the specialist finishes.

**Material sourcing (15–25% savings).** Buying your own tile, vanity, and fixtures at Floor & Decor or local tile shops often beats the specialist's markup. Clarify upfront whether they accept owner-supplied materials — and understand that if your material is defective, the specialist isn't responsible for reinstallation labor.

## Red Flags

- **No written contract.** Any project over $1,000 needs a detailed written agreement.
- **Large upfront deposit.** 10–15% at signing is standard. 50%+ is a red flag.
- **No proof of insurance.** Walk away. One injury on your property without workers' comp makes you liable.
- **Pressure to decide immediately.** Legitimate specialists have enough pipeline that they don't need high-pressure tactics.
- **Lump-sum estimate with no line items.** "Bathroom renovation — $18,000" tells you nothing. Insist on itemized labor, materials, fixtures, and permits.
