Replacing a laminate countertop runs $400–$2,000 if you're staying with laminate, or $2,000–$6,000+ if upgrading to stone or quartz. The project is one of the fastest and most impactful kitchen upgrades you can make — most replacements take less than a day. Here's a line-by-line breakdown of every cost you'll encounter so there are no surprises on project day.

## Cost by Kitchen Layout

Your kitchen's layout determines how much countertop material you need. Here's what different configurations cost with standard post-form laminate:

| Layout | Approximate Sq Ft | Laminate Cost (Material) | Installed Cost |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------|----------------|
| Galley (one wall, 8 ft) | 14 sq ft | $170–$350 | $400–$700 |
| L-shaped (10 ft + 6 ft) | 28 sq ft | $340–$700 | $700–$1,400 |
| U-shaped (8 ft + 10 ft + 8 ft) | 45 sq ft | $550–$1,100 | $1,100–$2,200 |
| L-shaped with island | 45–55 sq ft | $550–$1,350 | $1,100–$2,700 |

These figures assume standard 25.5-inch depth countertops at $12–$25 per linear foot for post-form laminate.

## Line-by-Line Project Budget

Here's a complete budget worksheet for a typical L-shaped kitchen (28 sq ft of countertop):

### Demolition and Removal

| Item | DIY Cost | Professional Cost |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Plumbing disconnect | $0 (DIY) | $75–$125 |
| Old countertop removal | $0 (1–2 hours labor) | $100–$200 |
| Disposal/dump fee | $25–$50 | Usually included |

Laminate countertops are lightweight and easy to remove. Unscrew mounting clips from below, pry up with a flat bar (they're usually caulked to the wall), and haul out in sections. One person can handle a 6-foot section. The old particleboard substrate goes in the regular trash if cut into manageable pieces, or take it to the dump for $25–$50.

### New Countertop Materials

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Post-form laminate (two pieces for L-shape) | $200–$500 |
| Miter bolt kit (corner joint) | $15–$25 |
| End splash/end cap | $8–$15 per end |
| Sink cutout (if using drop-in sink, cut DIY) | $0 |
| Build-up strips (if replacing with custom) | $15–$30 |

**The corner joint** is the trickiest part of an L-shaped laminate installation. Post-form corners come in pre-mitered kits with draw bolts and sealant. Buy the right-hand or left-hand miter based on your layout — this is easy to get wrong. Measure which leg meets which at the corner and match the miter direction.

### Installation Labor (If Hiring)

| Task | Cost |
|------|------|
| Template and measure | Usually included |
| Cut to size | $50–$100 |
| Sink cutout | $50–$100 |
| Corner joining | $75–$125 |
| Mount and secure | $100–$175 |
| Caulk and finish | $25–$50 |
| **Total installation labor** | **$300–$550** |

### Plumbing Reconnection

| Item | Cost Range |
|------|-----------|
| Reinstall existing sink and faucet | $100–$175 |
| Install new sink and faucet | $150–$250 |
| Garbage disposal reconnect | $50–$100 |

### Backsplash Repair

The old countertop's built-in backsplash lip leaves a gap or damaged area when removed. Options:

- **New post-form countertop with built-in backsplash:** $0 extra (it's integrated)
- **Separate 4-inch laminate backsplash strip:** $30–$75
- **Caulk the gap and paint over:** $5–$10
- **Tile backsplash installation:** $500–$2,000 (a separate project, but often done simultaneously)

## Timing and Scheduling Considerations

**How long the project takes:**
- Post-form laminate swap (DIY): 4–8 hours
- Post-form laminate swap (professional): 3–5 hours
- Custom laminate fabrication and install: 1 week fabrication + 4–6 hours install
- Upgrade to quartz or granite: 7–14 days fabrication + 3–4 hours install

**When to schedule for best pricing:**
- Post-form laminate is always in stock at home centers. No lead time for materials.
- Custom laminate has a 5–10 business day lead time from order to delivery.
- Stone/quartz requires a template appointment first, then 7–14 business days for fabrication.
- Book any installation for Tuesday through Thursday — Monday and Friday slots fill first, and mid-week scheduling sometimes gets a small discount.

## When to Replace vs. Resurface

Resurfacing (applying new laminate over existing) is possible if:
- The existing countertop is structurally sound (no water damage, no warping)
- The particleboard substrate is intact
- You're okay with a slightly different edge profile (you're gluing laminate over the existing surface)

Resurfacing kits and sheets run $100–$400 for a standard kitchen. Contact cement and a J-roller are all you need. The result is functional but won't match the fit and finish of a new post-form countertop.

**Replace rather than resurface when:**
- Any section shows water swelling (particularly around the sink)
- The particleboard is delaminating or crumbling
- You're changing the sink type (top-mount to undermount or vice versa)
- The layout is changing at all
- You want a different edge profile

## Material Quality Tiers

Not all laminate is equal. Here's what the tiers actually mean:

**Standard grade (most residential):** 0.028-inch thick surface layer. Adequate for residential kitchens. Available in the widest range of patterns. This is what post-form countertops at Home Depot and Lowe's use.

**Premium grade:** 0.039-inch thick surface layer. More scratch-resistant, slightly better edge impact resistance. Available in select patterns, often the most realistic stone and wood looks.

**Commercial grade:** 0.048-inch thick surface layer. Designed for high-traffic commercial use. Overkill for residential but provides the best durability. Sometimes available through specialty suppliers.

The difference between standard and premium in daily residential use is marginal. Both handle normal kitchen activity fine. The premium grade shines in high-use areas and holds up better to surface scratches over time.

## Mistakes That Increase Your Cost

**Not verifying measurements before ordering.** Post-form laminate comes in set lengths. Ordering a 10-foot piece when you needed 10 feet 2 inches means buying the next size up (12-foot), adding $30–$60 in wasted material.

**Cutting from the top.** Circular saws cut on the upstroke, which chips the laminate surface if you cut from the top. Always cut with the finished side down — the bottom face gets the chip-out, which is hidden against the cabinets.

**Not supporting the cutout during sink hole cutting.** When the jigsaw completes the sink opening, the cutout piece drops. If it's still attached by a small tab, it tears the laminate edge. Screw a support strip across the cutout before finishing the last cut.

**Skipping the silicone at the corner joint.** Laminate corner miters are exposed particleboard meeting exposed particleboard. Without silicone sealant in the joint, moisture wicks into the substrate and causes swelling within months.

**Reusing old plumbing supply lines.** The braided stainless steel supply lines under your sink ($5–$10 each) should be replaced every 10 years — they're a leading cause of water damage when they fail. New countertop installation is the ideal time to swap them.
