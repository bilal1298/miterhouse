Removing old insulation from an attic costs $1 to $3 per square foot for professional vacuum removal, putting a typical 1,000-square-foot attic at $1,000 to $3,000. If the insulation contains hazardous materials — asbestos vermiculite, heavy rodent contamination, or mold — the cost jumps to $3 to $8 per square foot due to specialized containment and disposal requirements.

Most homeowners do not budget for insulation removal because they assume new insulation simply goes over the old. Sometimes that works. But there are several situations where removal is necessary, and knowing the costs upfront prevents surprises.

## When Removal Is Necessary

Adding new insulation over old is perfectly acceptable when the existing insulation is clean, dry, and structurally intact. Removal becomes necessary in these specific circumstances:

**Rodent or pest contamination.** If you find droppings, urine staining, nesting material, or dead animals in the insulation, it needs to come out. Rodent-contaminated insulation is a health hazard — mouse and rat droppings can carry hantavirus, and the particles become airborne when disturbed. Professional removal with HEPA-filtered equipment is strongly recommended.

**Mold growth.** Insulation that has gotten wet — from a roof leak, condensation issue, or ice dam — often develops mold. Fiberglass does not feed mold, but it traps moisture against wood framing that does. Removing the insulation, treating the mold, fixing the moisture source, and then re-insulating is the correct sequence.

**Vermiculite insulation.** If your attic contains loose, granular, pebble-like insulation that is grayish-brown or silver-gold, it may be vermiculite. A significant percentage of vermiculite insulation installed before 1990 came from a mine contaminated with tremolite asbestos. Do not disturb it yourself — have it tested first ($200 to $400 for lab analysis), and if positive, hire a licensed asbestos abatement contractor.

**Major air sealing work.** If you need to seal dozens of air leaks in the attic floor — around wiring penetrations, plumbing vents, top plates, recessed lights, and ductwork — the existing insulation must be moved or removed to access the ceiling plane beneath it. Some contractors remove all insulation, do the air sealing, then blow in new insulation as a complete package.

**Changing insulation type.** If you are converting from fiberglass batts to blown-in cellulose (for better performance), removing the batts first gives a cleaner result and ensures the blown-in material fills all cavities completely.

## Cost Breakdown by Insulation Type

The type of insulation being removed significantly affects the cost and method.

### Blown-In Insulation (Cellulose or Fiberglass)

**Professional cost:** $1.00 to $2.00 per square foot.

**Method:** A commercial insulation vacuum — a large machine mounted on a trailer or truck — sucks the loose material through a 4-inch hose, depositing it directly into collection bags. A crew of two can clear a 1,000-square-foot attic in 4 to 8 hours.

**For 1,000 sq ft:** $1,000 to $2,000.

This is the most common attic insulation removal scenario and the most straightforward.

### Fiberglass Batt Insulation

**Professional cost:** $1.00 to $2.50 per square foot.

**Method:** Batts are rolled up, bagged in heavy-duty trash bags, and carried out through the attic access. It is labor-intensive, dusty, and physically uncomfortable — fiberglass itches and irritates skin, eyes, and lungs.

**For 1,000 sq ft:** $1,000 to $2,500.

Batt removal is slightly more expensive per foot than blown-in because it is more labor-intensive and cannot be mechanized.

### Contaminated Insulation

**Professional cost:** $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot for rodent/mold contamination; $5.00 to $8.00+ per square foot for asbestos-containing materials.

**Method:** Contaminated insulation removal requires containment (sealing the attic from living spaces), HEPA-filtered vacuums, personal protective equipment, and proper disposal at certified facilities. Asbestos work requires a licensed abatement contractor and typically includes air monitoring during and after work.

**For 1,000 sq ft:**
- Rodent contamination: $2,500 to $5,000
- Asbestos vermiculite: $5,000 to $8,000+

### Spray Foam Insulation

**Professional cost:** $2.00 to $4.00 per square foot.

**Method:** Spray foam adheres to surfaces and must be scraped, cut, or ground off. It is the most difficult insulation to remove and the most expensive. In some cases — particularly closed-cell foam bonded to roof decking — complete removal is impractical and the best approach is to leave it in place and work around it.

**For 1,000 sq ft:** $2,000 to $4,000.

## Can You Remove Insulation Yourself?

Blown-in insulation is the most practical for DIY removal. You can rent a commercial insulation vacuum from some home centers or equipment rental companies for $200 to $400 per day. You will also need:

- **Respirator (N95 minimum, P100 recommended):** $20 to $40
- **Tyvek suit:** $10 to $15
- **Safety goggles (sealed):** $10 to $15
- **Heavy-duty bags for collection:** $20 to $30
- **Work lights:** $15 to $30 (attics are dark and you need both hands free)

**Total DIY cost for 1,000 sq ft:** $300 to $600 including rental and disposal fees.

The work is physically demanding. Attics in summer can exceed 130 degrees. Plan to work in early morning, take frequent breaks, and have plenty of water. Working alone in an attic is a safety risk — always have someone in the house who knows you are up there.

**DIY limitations:** Do not attempt DIY removal of contaminated insulation. Rodent-contaminated material requires HEPA filtration you will not get from a rental vacuum. Asbestos is legally required to be handled by certified professionals in most jurisdictions — and for good reason.

## Disposal Costs

Clean insulation goes to a standard landfill. Disposal fees vary by location but typically run $50 to $150 per truckload. Many insulation contractors include disposal in their per-square-foot pricing.

Contaminated insulation disposal costs significantly more:
- Rodent-contaminated: $100 to $300 per load at approved facilities
- Asbestos-containing: $500 to $1,500 for specialized disposal, priced by weight

Check your local waste management regulations — some municipalities require specific disposal methods even for clean insulation.

## The Removal-Plus-Reinstall Package

Most insulation contractors offer a package deal: remove old insulation, air seal the attic floor, and blow in new insulation. This is typically the best value because:

- The crew is already there with equipment.
- Air sealing is only practical when the attic floor is exposed.
- New insulation goes in immediately, minimizing the time your home is uninsulated.

**Typical package price for 1,000 sq ft (remove + air seal + blow in cellulose to R-49):**
- $2,000 to $4,500 professionally installed.

Compared to doing removal ($1,500), air sealing ($700), and insulation ($1,200) as three separate projects, the package saves 15 to 25 percent and avoids scheduling three separate crews.

## Is Removal Worth It?

If the existing insulation is clean and dry, adding new material on top is almost always the better financial decision — it avoids the $1,000 to $3,000 removal cost entirely. Removal makes sense only when the insulation is genuinely compromised (contaminated, wet, moldy) or when you need to access the attic floor for air sealing or rewiring work that cannot be done through the existing insulation.

The one exception: if you are planning to finish the attic as living space, all floor insulation comes out regardless because you are converting the thermal boundary from the attic floor to the roof plane. But that is a full renovation project, not an insulation removal project, and the cost structure is entirely different.
