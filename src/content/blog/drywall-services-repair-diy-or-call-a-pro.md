Drywall repair services typically cost $150 to $400 per patch for small to medium repairs (nail holes, doorknob damage, small cracks), $300 to $800 for larger patches (fist-sized holes, water damage areas), and $500 to $2,500 for extensive work like replacing full sheets or repairing sagging ceilings. Most homeowners spend between $250 and $750 for a service call that addresses multiple small repairs in one visit.

Whether you should DIY or hire out depends entirely on the size and type of damage. Small stuff is genuinely easy to fix yourself. Larger repairs involve skills that take time to develop.

## Types of Drywall Damage and What They Actually Cost to Fix

### Small Holes (Nail Pops, Screw Holes, Picture Hangers)

**What it is:** Holes under 1/2 inch, screw pops where fasteners push through the compound, and dents from everyday life.

**Pro cost:** $75 to $150 per visit, usually with a minimum trip charge. Most drywall contractors will not come out for a single nail pop — they batch these with other work.

**DIY cost:** Under $20 in materials (a tub of lightweight spackle and a putty knife you probably already own).

**DIY difficulty:** Extremely easy. Fill the hole, let it dry, sand flat, prime, paint. The only mistake to avoid is overfilling — spackle shrinks slightly as it dries, so a slight crown is fine, but a big glob means more sanding.

**Verdict:** Always DIY.

### Medium Holes (1 to 6 Inches — Doorknob Punches, Anchor Pull-Outs)

**What it is:** The classic doorknob hole, failed wall anchor attempts, or damage from moving furniture. The drywall paper is torn and there is a hole through the gypsum core.

**Pro cost:** $150 to $350 per patch, including texturing and priming.

**DIY cost:** $15 to $40 in materials (a mesh patch kit, joint compound, and sandpaper).

**DIY difficulty:** Moderate. Self-adhesive mesh patches work well for holes up to about 4 inches. Peel, stick over the hole, spread joint compound over the mesh, let it dry, apply a second coat wider than the first, sand, and prime.

For holes larger than 4 inches, the mesh-only approach sags. You need a solid backing — either a piece of plywood held behind the hole with a drywall screw or a commercial backer plate. This adds a step but is still manageable.

**Verdict:** DIY-friendly with a basic patch kit. Practice on a scrap piece if it is your first time.

### Large Holes (6+ Inches — Failed TV Mounts, Plumbing Access, Impact Damage)

**What it is:** Holes too large for a patch kit. These require cutting out the damaged section, installing a new piece of drywall, taping the seams, and finishing with multiple coats of joint compound.

**Pro cost:** $300 to $800 depending on size and location. Ceiling repairs cost more than wall repairs because of the overhead working position and the difficulty of matching ceiling texture.

**DIY cost:** $30 to $75 in materials (drywall sheet, joint tape, joint compound, screws, a drywall saw).

**DIY difficulty:** This is where the learning curve steepens. The cutting and fitting is straightforward, but the taping and finishing — getting a repair to disappear completely — takes three or more coats of compound, careful sanding between each, and a primer coat before paint. Rushing the process (applying too-thick coats, sanding before fully dry, or skipping coats) leaves visible seams.

**Verdict:** DIY if you have patience for a multi-day process. Hire out if the repair is in a highly visible area and you want invisible results.

### Water Damage Repair

**What it is:** Stained, softened, or buckled drywall from roof leaks, plumbing failures, or condensation. Water-damaged drywall may also have mold behind it.

**Pro cost:** $400 to $1,500+ depending on the extent of damage and whether mold is present. This does not include fixing the water source — that is a separate plumbing or roofing job.

**DIY cost:** $50 to $200 in materials, but only after the moisture source is fixed.

**DIY difficulty:** High, and not because of the drywall work itself. The critical issue is what is behind the damaged area. Water-damaged drywall needs to be cut out far enough to reach dry material. Behind it, you may find wet insulation, mold on the framing, or ongoing moisture that will destroy the new drywall too.

**Verdict:** Fix the moisture source first (hire a plumber or roofer if needed). Then assess the extent of damage. If the wet area is under 4 square feet and there is no visible mold, DIY is reasonable. Larger areas or any mold presence warrants a professional who can assess structural conditions and handle [lead paint concerns in older homes](https://www.epa.gov/lead).

### Ceiling Cracks and Sagging

**What it is:** Hairline cracks along seams, nail pops in ceilings, or sections where the drywall has pulled away from the joists and sags.

**Pro cost:** Cracks and pops: $150 to $400. Sagging sections that need re-fastening: $300 to $800. Full sheet replacement on a ceiling: $500 to $1,200.

**DIY difficulty for cracks:** Moderate. Tape over hairline cracks with mesh tape and skim-coat with compound. The challenge is matching ceiling texture — if your ceiling has knockdown or orange peel texture, replicating it requires practice with a hopper gun or knockdown knife.

**DIY difficulty for sagging:** Hard. You need to re-screw the drywall into joists (not just into the drywall — find the joists), which means overhead work while pushing a heavy panel against gravity. Two people minimum, and a drywall lift rental ($40-$60 per day) makes it dramatically easier.

## The Professional Drywall Repair Process

Here is what a skilled drywall repair tech does that differentiates their work from a quick DIY patch:

### Step 1: Assess and Prep

They examine the damage, check for underlying issues (moisture, structural movement, insulation problems), and determine the extent of the repair. Then they protect floors and furniture with drop cloths and set up dust containment if sanding will be involved.

### Step 2: Cut and Remove Damaged Material

Damaged drywall is cut back to the nearest studs on each side. This gives a solid nailing surface for the new piece and ensures the entire damaged area is removed. Pros use a drywall saw or oscillating multi-tool for clean cuts.

### Step 3: Install New Drywall

The replacement piece is cut to fit tightly and screwed to the framing. Screws are set just below the paper surface without breaking through — that dimple gets filled with compound later.

### Step 4: Tape and First Coat

Paper tape (not mesh — paper tape is stronger on seams) is embedded in a thin coat of joint compound along every seam. This coat fills the tape and creates the first smooth layer.

### Step 5: Second and Third Coats

Each subsequent coat extends wider than the previous one, feathering the compound out 6 to 12 inches beyond the seam. This gradual transition is what makes the repair invisible. Each coat must dry completely (typically overnight) before the next.

### Step 6: Sand, Prime, and Match

Final sanding with 120 to 150 grit paper creates a smooth surface. Dust is vacuumed and wiped. A coat of drywall primer seals the compound (which absorbs paint differently than the surrounding wall). Then paint is applied to match.

For areas with textured walls, the texture must be replicated before painting. This is where many DIY repairs fall short — matching existing texture is a skill that takes practice and the right tools.

## Indoor Air Quality During Drywall Repair

Drywall dust is a real concern during sanding. While gypsum dust is not toxic, it irritates lungs and coats everything. The [EPA's guidance on indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) recommends:

- Wear an N95 respirator during sanding
- Seal off the work area with plastic sheeting and painter's tape
- Use a shop vacuum with a HEPA filter for cleanup
- Ventilate the area before removing containment

Professional drywall services use dust-containment vacuums that attach directly to their sanders, dramatically reducing airborne dust. This is worth paying for if you have household members with asthma or allergies.

## How to Find and Vet Drywall Repair Services

**Where to look:** Drywall repair is offered by dedicated drywall contractors, general handymen, and painting companies. For small repairs, a skilled handyman is often the most cost-effective option. For full sheet replacement or ceiling work, a drywall specialist provides better results.

**What to ask:**
- Do you match existing wall texture? (If the answer is no, move on.)
- Is priming included, or just the patch?
- What is your minimum service charge?
- Can I see photos of previous repair work?

**Pricing structure:** Some contractors charge by the patch (flat rate per repair), others charge hourly ($50 to $85 per hour). For multiple small repairs, hourly often works in your favor. For a single large repair, a flat rate is usually better.

If you are planning to paint after the drywall work, coordinating both jobs saves time and money. ## When to Skip the Repair and Replace the Sheet

Sometimes patching is the wrong approach. Replace the entire drywall sheet when:

- **More than 30 percent of the sheet is damaged.** Patching 60 percent of a $12 sheet of drywall costs more in compound and labor than replacing it.
- **The damage is from ongoing moisture.** If the drywall has been wet for more than 48 hours, mold is likely present inside the gypsum core even if you cannot see it. Cut it out and replace.
- **You are already remodeling the room.** If you are updating a bathroom or kitchen, replace the drywall with moisture-resistant (green board) or mold-resistant (purple board) drywall while the walls are open.
- **There is evidence of structural movement.** Cracks that reappear after repair indicate the framing is moving. Patching a symptom while ignoring the cause wastes time and money. A structural assessment may be needed.

The goal of any drywall repair — whether DIY or professional — is an invisible result. If you can see where the patch is after painting, the repair is not done. A good drywall technician delivers walls that look like they were never damaged, and that is worth paying for when the damage exceeds your skill level.
