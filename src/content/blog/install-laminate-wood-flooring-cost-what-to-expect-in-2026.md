Installing laminate wood flooring costs $5 to $13 per square foot total — materials, underlayment, and labor combined — with most homeowners spending $6 to $9 per square foot for a mid-range product professionally installed. On a 500-square-foot main living area, that puts you at $3,000 to $4,500 out the door. The material itself runs $2 to $6 per square foot; the rest covers underlayment, labor, trim, and the unglamorous prep work that determines whether your floor lasts 8 years or 25.

Those numbers are 2026 pricing, and they reflect a market that has stabilized after several years of supply-chain volatility. Laminate wood flooring remains one of the most cost-effective ways to get the look of hardwood without the hardwood price tag, and the products available today are meaningfully better than what was on the shelf even five years ago.

## Breaking Down Every Cost

Let me walk through each line item so you know exactly where your money goes:

### Material ($2 to $6 per square foot)

The price range is wide because "laminate wood flooring" covers products ranging from paper-thin, hollow-sounding budget planks to thick, textured, virtually-indistinguishable-from-real-wood premium boards.

The specification that matters most is **AC rating** — the abrasion class that indicates durability:
- **AC3** ($0.75-$2.00/sq ft): Light commercial / moderate residential. Fine for bedrooms and closets. Will show wear in high-traffic areas within 5-8 years.
- **AC4** ($2.00-$4.00/sq ft): General commercial / heavy residential. Holds up to kids, dogs, and daily life in main living areas. This is the minimum I recommend for any room with real foot traffic.
- **AC5** ($4.00-$8.00/sq ft): Heavy commercial. You will never wear through this in a residential setting. The question at this price is whether you should be looking at LVP or engineered hardwood instead.

**Thickness** is the second critical spec. Budget laminate at 6-7mm feels hollow and plasticky underfoot. At 10-12mm, the floor feels solid, absorbs sound better, and is more forgiving of minor subfloor imperfections. The thicker product costs more, but the daily experience of walking on it is noticeably better.

### Underlayment ($0.15 to $1.00 per square foot)

Every laminate floor needs underlayment unless the planks have it pre-attached. For concrete subfloors, you need underlayment with a built-in moisture barrier. For wood subfloors, standard foam underlayment works for most installations.

Do not cheap out on underlayment for a concrete slab installation. A $0.15 foam pad without a moisture barrier over concrete will cause your laminate to swell and buckle within months as ground moisture migrates through the slab. The $0.50 upgrade to moisture-barrier underlayment is the cheapest insurance on the entire project.

### Old Flooring Removal ($1 to $3 per square foot)

If you are replacing existing flooring, removal costs depend on what you are pulling up:

- **Old laminate (click-lock):** $1.00-$1.50/sq ft — disassembles relatively quickly
- **Old laminate (glued):** $1.50-$2.50/sq ft — comes up in pieces, adhesive residue needs scraping
- **Carpet and pad:** $0.75-$1.50/sq ft — rolls up, but staple removal from the subfloor is tedious
- **Vinyl sheet:** $1.00-$2.00/sq ft — may leave adhesive residue. If the home pre-dates 1985, test for [asbestos in the adhesive](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos) before disturbing it
- **Ceramic tile:** $2.00-$4.00/sq ft — labor-intensive, dusty, and often damages the subfloor beneath

The cheapest approach, when possible, is to install new laminate over existing smooth, flat, firmly-attached flooring. Laminate can go over existing vinyl or laminate if the surface is flat and the total floor height after adding another layer does not create problems at transitions and doors.

### Subfloor Preparation ($0.50 to $3.00 per square foot)

This is the line item that varies most between projects and the one most likely to surprise you. A perfectly flat subfloor in good condition adds minimal cost. A subfloor with high spots, low spots, moisture issues, or damage can double the overall project price.

The [IRC building code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) does not specify subfloor flatness requirements for floating floors, but every laminate manufacturer does — typically 3/16-inch variation over a 10-foot span. Exceeding this tolerance voids the warranty and causes real performance problems: gaps between planks, cracking at joints, and a floor that bounces or flexes underfoot.

### Installation Labor ($2 to $5 per square foot)

Professional installation labor for laminate flooring includes:
- Laying the underlayment (if not pre-attached)
- Installing the planks with proper expansion gaps
- Cutting planks to fit around walls, doorways, pipes, and other obstacles
- Undercutting door jambs
- Installing transition strips between rooms and at changes in flooring material
- Replacing or installing baseboards/quarter-round to cover expansion gaps

The lower end of the labor range ($2-$3/sq ft) is for simple, rectangular rooms with minimal obstacles. Complex room shapes, many doorways, angled walls, stairs, and tight spaces push toward $4-$5/sq ft.

### Trim and Transitions ($200 to $800 per project)

Often quoted separately from the main installation:
- **Baseboards:** $1.50-$4.00 per linear foot installed (remove and reinstall existing, or install new)
- **Quarter-round/shoe molding:** $0.75-$2.00 per linear foot installed
- **T-molding transitions:** $15-$40 each
- **Reducer strips** (where laminate meets a lower surface): $15-$40 each
- **Stair nosing:** $25-$50 per step

## Room-by-Room Considerations

Not every room in your house is equally suited for laminate:

**Living rooms and bedrooms (ideal):** Dry, moderate traffic, and the areas where you want the wood look most. Install AC4-rated laminate with 10mm+ thickness for the best experience.

**Kitchens (proceed with caution):** Spills happen. Modern water-resistant laminate (WR or waterproof-core products) handles incidental moisture, but a dishwasher leak or plumbing failure that sits for hours will still damage laminate from the bottom up. If your kitchen sees heavy spill exposure, [vinyl sheet flooring](/blog/install-vinyl-sheet-flooring-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/) or LVP is the safer long-term choice.

**Bathrooms (generally not recommended):** Too much moisture. Standing water around toilets, tubs, and sinks finds its way to laminate edges and seams. LVP or porcelain tile is the right material for bathrooms.

**Basements (only with moisture precautions):** Concrete slab moisture is the enemy. Mandatory moisture testing before installation, moisture-barrier underlayment, and ideally a waterproof-core laminate product. Even with precautions, basement humidity can cause edge swelling during humid months. A dedicated dehumidifier helps.

**Stairs (possible but complex):** Laminate on stairs requires adhesive and specialty stair nosing — it is not a floating installation. The cost per step ($40-$100) is high relative to the area covered, and the result depends heavily on installer skill. Get references specifically for stair installations.

## Acclimation: The Step Everyone Wants to Skip

Laminate wood flooring must acclimate to your home's temperature and humidity before installation. This is not optional — it is in every manufacturer's installation instructions, and skipping it is the single most common cause of post-installation problems.

The standard acclimation period is 48 to 72 hours. Bring the unopened boxes into the room where the flooring will be installed, stack them flat (not on their sides), and leave them at normal room temperature and humidity. The laminate needs to reach equilibrium with your home's environment before you lock the planks together.

If you install laminate that is too cold or too dry relative to the room's normal conditions, the planks will expand as they acclimate in place, creating buckling and lifting. If the laminate is too warm or too humid (stored in a hot garage, for example), the planks will contract after installation, opening gaps at the seams.

## Getting Accurate Quotes

When requesting installation quotes, provide:
- The room's square footage (measure length times width for each area)
- The current flooring type (so the installer knows what removal is needed)
- The subfloor type (concrete slab or wood-framed)
- Any known issues (moisture, uneven areas, previous water damage)
- Whether you have already purchased the laminate or want the installer to supply it

Some installers offer package pricing that includes materials, which can save money through their trade discounts. Others prefer you to supply the materials and quote labor only. Get both options if possible and compare the totals.

Three quotes is the minimum. The quotes should be within 15-20% of each other. A quote dramatically lower than the others usually means something is being left out — subfloor prep, removal, transitions, or trim.

## The DIY Value Proposition

Laminate flooring is the most popular DIY flooring project for a reason: the click-lock system genuinely does not require professional skills to assemble. The planks lock together like a puzzle, no adhesive needed, no special fasteners.

What a DIY installation saves you on a 500-square-foot project:

| Cost Element | DIY | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| Materials (mid-range) | $1,500 | $1,500 |
| Underlayment | $150 | $150 |
| Old flooring removal | $0 (your labor) | $500-$750 |
| Subfloor prep | $50-$100 (materials only) | $250-$500 |
| Installation labor | $0 | $1,000-$2,500 |
| Trim/transitions | $200 (materials) | $400-$600 |
| **Total** | **$1,900-$1,950** | **$3,800-$6,000** |

That is $1,900 to $4,000 in savings. The tradeoff is your time (2-3 full days for 500 square feet as a first-timer) and the risk of mistakes. Common DIY mistakes — failing to leave expansion gaps, installing the first row crooked, or cutting planks too short — are fixable but cost you time and sometimes an extra box of flooring.

If you have never installed laminate before, start with a small room (a closet or small bedroom) to learn the system before tackling the main living area. The skills transfer directly, and the small room lets you make your learning mistakes in a low-visibility area.

If you have experienced [moisture damage to laminate flooring](/blog/moisture-damage-laminate-flooring-what-homeowners-should-know/) before, pay extra attention to subfloor preparation and underlayment selection this time. The most common cause of repeat laminate failure is reinstalling over the same moisture problem that destroyed the previous floor.

## What 2026 Pricing Looks Like in Practice

For a real-world example, here is what a mid-range laminate installation costs in a typical suburban home in 2026:

**Project:** Replace carpet in a 400-square-foot living room with mid-range laminate (10mm, AC4, click-lock with pre-attached underlayment)

- Carpet removal and disposal: $400
- Subfloor prep (minor leveling, secure loose boards): $200
- Laminate material (420 sq ft including 5% waste): $1,260 at $3.00/sq ft
- Installation labor: $1,200 at $3.00/sq ft
- Remove and reinstall existing baseboards (80 linear feet): $240
- Two transition strips at doorways: $70
- **Total: $3,370**

That is a solid, durable floor that will look good and perform well for 15-20 years. It is also about one-third the cost of comparable engineered hardwood and half the cost of solid hardwood — the fundamental value proposition that keeps laminate relevant in a market increasingly dominated by LVP.

Choose your material based on the room, hire based on references (not the lowest bid), and do not skip the subfloor prep. Those three decisions determine whether your laminate flooring replacement lasts 8 years or 25.
