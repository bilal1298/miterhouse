Quartz countertop installation in a standard kitchen costs $3,000 to $7,500 in 2026, with the slab material accounting for about 60% of that total and fabrication plus labor covering the rest. For a bathroom vanity, the same quality quartz runs $800 to $2,500 installed, depending on vanity size and edge complexity.

The quartz market has matured significantly over the past five years, and competition among fabricators has brought prices down from the premium-only territory quartz once occupied. Here is a complete pricing guide so you can plan your budget with confidence.

## Understanding Quartz Pricing in 2026

Quartz pricing has a structure that confuses many homeowners because the "per square foot" number you see advertised rarely includes everything.

**Advertised price ($45–$90/sq ft):** This is typically the slab cost only — material before any cutting, shaping, or installation. It is the hook that gets you in the door.

**Fabrication cost ($10–$25/sq ft):** Cutting to your template dimensions, polishing edges, creating cutouts. This is real work that requires specialized CNC equipment and skilled operators.

**Installation cost ($10–$20/sq ft):** Delivery, setting, seaming, sealing, and sink mounting. The physical labor of getting the finished pieces into your kitchen and making everything tight and level.

**All-in installed price ($55–$150/sq ft):** This is the number that matters for your budget. Always ask for the all-in price when comparing quotes.

Here is a quick reference for a 45-square-foot kitchen at different quality levels:

| Quality Level | All-in per sq ft | Total (45 sq ft) |
|---------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Entry-level | $55–$75 | $2,475–$3,375 |
| Mid-range | $75–$110 | $3,375–$4,950 |
| Premium | $110–$150 | $4,950–$6,750 |

## Brand Comparison: What You're Actually Paying For

Not all quartz is the same, and brand differences extend beyond marketing.

**Caesarstone:** One of the original quartz brands, known for reliable consistency and a wide color library. Mid-range to premium pricing. Their warranty is 10 years residential, which is shorter than some competitors.

**Cambria:** American-made, often the premium option. Known for striking marble-look patterns and thick-slab options. Cambria only sells through authorized dealers, which limits price shopping but ensures consistent fabrication quality. Lifetime residential warranty.

**Silestone:** Broad range from budget to premium. They pioneered bacteriostatic quartz surfaces using silver ion technology. Good entry-level options. 25-year warranty.

**MSI (Q Premium Natural Quartz):** Strong value play. Good color selection, competitive pricing, and widely available through big-box retailers and independent fabricators. 10-year warranty.

**LG Viatera and Hanstone:** Solid mid-range options with good availability. 15-year residential warranty.

The practical differences between brands at the same price point are minimal for daily kitchen use. All major brands resist staining, scratching, and heat comparably. Where they differ most is in the realism of their natural stone patterns and the specifics of warranty coverage.

## Kitchen vs. Bathroom Installation Costs

Bathroom quartz installations are proportionally more expensive per square foot because of tighter spaces and more complex sink cutouts, but the total project cost is lower due to smaller surface area.

**Kitchen quartz installation:**
- 35–65 sq ft typical
- $2,500–$7,500 total
- Standard rectangular cutouts
- Usually 1–2 seams
- Easier access for installers

**Bathroom vanity quartz installation:**
- 6–25 sq ft typical
- $800–$2,500 total
- Often requires undermount oval or vessel sink cutout
- Usually seamless (single piece)
- Tighter working conditions

For bathrooms, one cost advantage is that you can often use remnant pieces — leftover sections from larger kitchen jobs. Remnants cost 30 to 50% less than ordering a new slab, and a bathroom vanity often requires only 6 to 15 square feet, well within remnant sizing.

## The Hidden Costs I Wish I Had Known

When I helped my brother plan his quartz kitchen counters, we thought the quoted price was the complete picture. It was not. Here is what added up beyond the slab and installation.

**Plumbing reconnection: $250.** We knew the plumber needed to disconnect the sink for installation day, but we did not realize reconnecting included replacing supply lines (required due to corrosion) and adjusting the garbage disposal height for the new, thicker countertop.

**Dishwasher clearance fix: $150.** The quartz was 1/2 inch thicker than the old laminate, and the dishwasher would not slide back in. A handyman had to lower the dishwasher legs and trim the mounting bracket.

**Backsplash gap repair: $350.** The old tile backsplash was installed tight against the previous countertop. The new quartz sat slightly higher, leaving a visible gap at the bottom of the backsplash. Rather than live with caulk filling a half-inch gap, we had the bottom row of tile reinstalled.

**Outlet relocation: $400.** Two electrical outlets that were right at countertop height ended up partially behind the new backsplash height we planned. Moving them up cost more than expected because one was on a shared circuit that required rewiring. The [NFPA electrical code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) requires specific outlet placement relative to countertop surfaces.

Total surprise costs: $1,150 on a $5,200 quoted project — a 22% overage. That taught me to always budget 15 to 20% contingency, which I now recommend to everyone.

## How to Get the Best Quartz Countertop Deal

These strategies work regardless of which brand or fabricator you choose:

**Get quotes during slow season.** January through March is typically the slowest period for countertop fabricators. You may get 5 to 10% off or a faster timeline simply because they have capacity.

**Ask about package deals.** If you are replacing countertops in both the kitchen and bathroom, bundling saves on delivery, templating, and sometimes material cost.

**Compare fabricator-direct vs. big-box.** Home Depot and Lowe's both offer quartz installation services, but they subcontract to local fabricators and add a markup. Getting a quote directly from the same fabricator the big-box store would use often saves 10 to 15%.

**Consider prefab quartz for simple layouts.** Prefabricated quartz slabs come in standard sizes (25.5" x 96" is common) with edges already finished. They work for straight runs and simple L-shapes and cost 20 to 30% less than custom fabrication. The trade-off is limited color selection and you cannot get custom cutouts.

**Negotiate edge profiles.** Some fabricators include bullnose or beveled edges at the same price as eased — it costs them almost nothing extra on CNC equipment. Ask before assuming you need to pay an upgrade fee.

## Caring for Your Quartz Investment

Once your quartz is installed, keeping it in top condition is genuinely easy — that is a key selling point of the material.

**Daily cleaning:** Warm water and mild dish soap. Wipe with a soft cloth. That is it.

**Stubborn spots:** A paste of baking soda and water left on for 10 minutes handles dried-on food, coffee stains, and most discoloration.

**What to avoid:** No bleach-based cleaners, no abrasive pads, no harsh solvents. These degrade the resin that binds the quartz. Also avoid prolonged direct heat — always use trivets or hot pads for pots and pans from the stovetop.

**Sealing:** Never needed. This is not granite. Quartz is non-porous and does not absorb liquids. If a fabricator tries to sell you a quartz sealer, find a different fabricator.

Quartz countertops are one of the [best upgrades for kitchen value](/blog/home-additions-and-renovations-what-homeowners-should-know/) because they combine low maintenance with high visual impact. As the [ICC residential building code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) notes, all countertop installations should meet structural support requirements — verify your cabinets can handle the load before installation day.

## Bottom Line

Budget $3,500 to $6,500 for a standard kitchen quartz countertop installation in 2026, plus 15 to 20% contingency for the inevitable supporting work. Get three all-in quotes that specify the exact slab, edge profile, and scope of supporting work. Visit the slab or see full-size samples before committing.

Quartz delivers the look of natural stone with near-zero maintenance — and at this point in the market, it is competitively priced enough that the maintenance-free benefit alone justifies any premium over granite for most homeowners.
