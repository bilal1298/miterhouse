Replacing kitchen countertops costs $1,500–$6,000 for most projects, with the average homeowner spending around $3,000–$4,000 for quartz or granite installed on existing cabinets. That number assumes you're keeping the same layout and cabinet structure — change those, and costs climb significantly. The replacement cost breaks down into four distinct phases, each with its own budget line.

## Phase 1: Removal of Old Countertops ($0–$500)

What removal costs depends entirely on what's there now.

**Laminate on particleboard:** The easiest removal. Disconnect plumbing, remove sink, unscrew mounting screws from below, and lift off the countertop in sections. DIY in 1–2 hours. Professional removal: $100–$200.

**Tile countertop:** More labor-intensive. Tile and cement board substrate need to be broken up and removed, and the plywood base checked for damage. DIY in 3–5 hours (messy, wear safety glasses and a dust mask). Professional removal: $200–$400.

**Granite or quartz slab:** Heavy (15–20 lbs per square foot) and fragile when unsupported. Professional removal is recommended: $200–$500. These slabs are often adhered with silicone caulk and may break during removal — don't plan on reusing them. If you DIY, you need 2–3 helpers and extreme caution around the sink cutout (the weakest point).

**Solid surface (Corian):** Similar to laminate removal. Usually screwed and caulked from below. Professional removal: $150–$300.

After removal, inspect the cabinets underneath. Water damage around sinks is common, especially in kitchens over 15 years old. Damaged cabinet bases need repair ($100–$400) before new countertops go on. Ignoring rotten wood under a new countertop is expensive short-term thinking.

## Phase 2: Material Selection and Pricing

Here's what each material actually costs for a standard 35-square-foot kitchen countertop (L-shaped, 25.5-inch depth, one sink cutout):

| Material | Material Only | Fabrication + Install | Total Installed |
|----------|--------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Laminate (post-form) | $200–$500 | $150–$400 | $350–$900 |
| Laminate (custom) | $400–$900 | $300–$600 | $700–$1,500 |
| Butcher block | $400–$800 | $0–$200 (DIY) | $400–$1,000 |
| Solid surface | $700–$1,800 | $500–$1,200 | $1,200–$3,000 |
| Granite (mid-range) | $1,000–$2,500 | $700–$1,500 | $1,700–$4,000 |
| Quartz (mid-range) | $1,200–$3,000 | $800–$1,500 | $2,000–$4,500 |
| Marble | $1,500–$4,000 | $800–$1,800 | $2,300–$5,800 |

The "fabrication + install" line covers template measurement, CNC cutting, edge profiling, sink cutout, delivery, and setting. For stone and quartz, this work happens at the fabricator's shop, not in your home.

**How to get better material pricing:**

- Visit local fabricators directly rather than going through a big-box retailer. Fabricators sell remnant pieces (leftover from larger jobs) at 30–50% off. If your kitchen is small enough, a remnant piece may cover the entire project.
- Ask about "builder-grade" or "commercial" stone slabs. These are perfectly functional slabs with less dramatic veining or slight color variations. Same material, same durability, 20–30% less cost.
- Time your project for January–March, when fabricators tend to be slower and more willing to negotiate.

## Phase 3: The Installation Itself

Professional installation day is fast but requires planning:

**Before the installers arrive:**
- Plumbing disconnected (you or your plumber)
- Old countertop removed (if not included in the installation quote)
- Cabinets clean, level, and any repairs complete
- Clear path from driveway to kitchen for heavy slabs
- Cooktop and range disconnected if applicable

**Installation timeline:**
- Template to installation: 7–14 business days
- Installation itself: 2–4 hours for most kitchens
- Post-install: Wait 24 hours before using the sink area (silicone caulk cure time)

**What the installer handles:**
- Setting and leveling the countertop
- Bonding seams with color-matched adhesive
- Caulking the countertop-to-wall junction
- Installing undermount sink clips (if applicable)
- Basic cleanup

**What the installer does NOT handle (and you need to arrange):**
- Plumbing reconnection ($100–$250 for a plumber)
- Cooktop or range reinstallation ($75–$200 for the appropriate tradesperson)
- Backsplash installation ($500–$2,500 separately)
- Sealing (granite and marble — the fabricator may do the initial seal, but ask)

## Phase 4: Finishing Details

These "extras" are easy to overlook but affect the final budget:

**New sink.** If you're changing countertop materials, the sink often changes too. Going from a laminate top-mount sink to an undermount sink with quartz requires a new sink ($150–$500), new faucet holes (drilled through the countertop or mounted behind it on a deck plate), and potentially new drain connections.

**New faucet.** If your existing faucet is over 10 years old, replacing it during the countertop swap saves a future service call. A quality kitchen faucet runs $150–$400. Installation is straightforward if the supply lines are accessible.

**Backsplash update.** Removing the old countertop usually damages the backsplash, especially where it meets the countertop edge. Budget $500–$2,000 for a tile backsplash or $100–$300 for a simple painted or caulked finish.

**Garbage disposal reconnection.** If your undermount sink configuration changes, the disposal may need a new mounting kit ($15–$25) or a completely new unit ($80–$200 plus $100–$200 installation if you don't DIY).

## Total Real-World Examples

**Budget replacement (laminate to laminate):**
- Removal: DIY, $0
- Post-form laminate countertop installed: $600
- New faucet: $175
- Plumbing reconnection: DIY, $0
- Total: **approximately $775**

**Mid-range upgrade (laminate to quartz):**
- Removal: $150
- Quartz countertop fabricated and installed: $3,200
- Undermount sink and faucet: $450
- Plumber reconnection: $175
- Backsplash tile: $1,200
- Total: **approximately $5,175**

**Premium replacement (granite to quartz, same layout):**
- Removal: $350
- Quartz countertop fabricated and installed: $4,000
- Keep existing sink and faucet: $0
- Plumber reconnection: $150
- Seal and repaint backsplash: $200
- Total: **approximately $4,700**

## Common Mistakes in Countertop Replacement

**Not checking cabinet condition before ordering.** Water damage, uneven cabinets, or inadequate support all need to be fixed before template measurement. Discovering these issues on installation day means a postponed install and wasted labor charges.

**Choosing material based on photos alone.** Always see a physical slab sample or visit the supplier's yard. Screen and print colors are unreliable, especially for natural stone where each slab varies. Bring a cabinet door or paint sample for comparison.

**Forgetting to measure for appliance fit.** New countertop thickness may differ from old. A granite slab (typically 3 cm/1.18 inches) is thicker than laminate (typically 1.5 inches including substrate but thinner at the actual edge). This can affect how your range, dishwasher, and refrigerator fit against the counter edge.

**Skipping the backsplash budget.** The old backsplash rarely survives countertop replacement intact. Budgeting $0 for backsplash repair is unrealistic unless you're replacing laminate with laminate using the same dimensions.
