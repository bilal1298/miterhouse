Kitchen remodel services break down into roughly a dozen distinct trades and specialties, and understanding which ones you need — and which ones you can skip or DIY — is the difference between a $10,000 targeted refresh and a $60,000 full gut. Most homeowners do not need every service available; they need the right combination for their kitchen's specific problems and their budget.

Here is a clear breakdown of each service category, what it costs, and when it actually makes sense to hire a specialist.

## The Core Kitchen Remodel Services

### 1. Design and Planning Services

**What it covers:** Kitchen layout planning, material selection guidance, 3D renderings, and construction documents.

**Cost:** $1,500 to $5,000 for a standalone kitchen designer. Many design-build firms include design in their overall project fee. Big-box stores (Home Depot, Lowe's, IKEA) offer free design services, though these are oriented toward selling their cabinet lines.

**When you need it:** If you are changing the kitchen layout — moving walls, relocating plumbing, or significantly rearranging the work triangle — professional design is worth every dollar. A designer catches spatial problems, code issues, and flow inefficiencies that you will live with for decades if they go unaddressed.

**When you can skip it:** If you are keeping the existing layout and updating surfaces and fixtures, you probably do not need a designer. You know where the sink should stay, the stove is fine where it is, and you just want better cabinets and countertops.

### 2. Demolition

**What it covers:** Removing old cabinets, countertops, flooring, backsplash, drywall if needed, and hauling everything away.

**Cost:** $500 to $3,000 depending on kitchen size and the extent of removal. Full gut demolition costs more than selective demo (removing just countertops and backsplash, for example).

**DIY potential:** High. Demolition is one of the most accessible DIY tasks in a kitchen remodel. The work is physical but not technical. You need a pry bar, reciprocating saw, work gloves, safety glasses, and a dumpster rental ($300 to $500 for a 10-yard roll-off). Remove cabinets from the top down, disconnect plumbing before touching the sink area, and turn off electrical circuits before removing light fixtures.

Caution: in homes built before 1978, [test for lead paint](https://www.epa.gov/lead) before demolition. If lead is present, follow EPA renovation, repair, and painting (RRP) rule procedures — or hire a certified lead-safe contractor for the demo phase.

### 3. Cabinet Services

**Options and costs:**
- **Cabinet refacing:** $4,000 to $10,000. Keeps existing cabinet boxes, replaces doors and drawer fronts, applies new veneer to visible surfaces. Takes 3 to 5 days.
- **Cabinet refinishing/painting:** $2,000 to $6,000. Professional spray-painting of existing cabinets. Takes 5 to 8 days for a proper job with primer, paint, and cure time.
- **Stock cabinet replacement:** $3,000 to $8,000 for materials plus $2,000 to $5,000 for installation. Available immediately from home centers. Limited size options may not fit non-standard openings.
- **Semi-custom cabinets:** $5,000 to $15,000 for materials plus installation. Built to order with more configuration options. Lead time: 4 to 8 weeks.
- **Custom cabinets:** $12,000 to $30,000+ for materials plus installation. Built to exact specifications. Lead time: 8 to 16 weeks.

Cabinets account for the largest single expense in most kitchen remodels — roughly 30 to 40% of the total project cost. This is where strategic decisions have the biggest budget impact.

### 4. Countertop Services

**Cost:** $1,500 to $8,000+ depending on material and kitchen size. See our detailed guides on [quartz countertop costs](/blog/how-much-does-quartz-countertop-installed-really-cost/) and [kitchen countertop installation pricing](/blog/kitchen-countertop-install-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/).

**Timeline:** 3 to 6 weeks from template to installation for stone countertops.

**Key coordination point:** Countertop templating cannot happen until cabinets are fully installed and leveled. Backsplash installation should wait until countertops are in place. Getting this sequence wrong adds weeks and potentially hundreds of dollars in corrective work.

### 5. Plumbing Services

**What it covers:** Sink installation, faucet installation, dishwasher connections, garbage disposal, ice maker lines, pot filler installation, gas line relocation (for gas ranges/cooktops).

**Cost:** $500 to $3,000 for standard plumbing within the existing layout. Relocating supply lines and drains (moving the sink to a new location) costs $1,500 to $5,000 depending on distance and accessibility.

**When you need a licensed plumber:** Any time you are moving supply lines, relocating drains, connecting gas, or modifying venting. Simple fixture swaps (replacing a faucet on existing connections) are manageable as DIY.

### 6. Electrical Services

**What it covers:** Outlet additions/relocations, under-cabinet lighting, pendant/recessed lighting, range hood ventilation wiring, dedicated circuits for major appliances, GFCI outlet installation.

**Cost:** $1,000 to $5,000 depending on scope. Adding a dedicated 20-amp circuit for a microwave runs $200 to $400. Recessed lighting installation costs $150 to $300 per can. Under-cabinet LED lighting costs $300 to $800 professionally installed.

Per [NFPA NEC code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70), kitchen countertop outlets must be GFCI-protected and spaced no more than 48 inches apart. A remodel is the right time to bring electrical up to current code.

## Specialty Services You May or May Not Need

### Flooring

**Cost:** $1,500 to $5,000 for a typical kitchen floor (100 to 150 sq ft). Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) at $3 to $7 per square foot installed is the most popular kitchen flooring in 2026 — it is waterproof, durable, and installs quickly. Tile runs $5 to $15 per square foot installed and is more durable but also more expensive and disruptive to install.

**Timing note:** If you are replacing kitchen flooring, do it before the cabinets go in. Floating floors (LVP, laminate) should not be installed under cabinets — the weight prevents proper expansion and contraction. Tile can go under or around cabinets, but installing first creates a cleaner result.

### Backsplash

**Cost:** $800 to $3,000 for subway tile; $2,000 to $5,000 for natural stone or mosaic tile. Peel-and-stick tile alternatives run $200 to $600 and are a reasonable DIY option for budget renovations.

**Timing:** Always installed after countertops are in place. The backsplash tile sits on top of the countertop surface with a thin caulk line at the joint.

### Painting

**Cost:** $300 to $800 for professional kitchen painting (walls and ceiling). This is easy to DIY and one of the best opportunities to save money — a gallon of quality paint costs $40 to $60 and a kitchen can usually be done in a weekend.

### Appliance Installation

**Cost:** $100 to $300 per appliance for professional installation. Most standard appliances (refrigerator, dishwasher, range) are straightforward to install yourself. Built-in appliances (wall ovens, cooktops, built-in microwaves) benefit from professional installation due to precise fitting requirements and electrical connections.

## How to Sequence Kitchen Remodel Services

Getting the order of operations right prevents expensive rework:

1. **Design and planning** — finalize everything before demo starts
2. **Demolition** — clear the space
3. **Structural work** — any wall modifications
4. **Rough plumbing and electrical** — in-wall work before drywall closes
5. **Drywall patching and repair**
6. **Flooring installation**
7. **Cabinet installation**
8. **Countertop templating** — after cabinets are set
9. **Countertop fabrication** (off-site)
10. **Countertop installation**
11. **Backsplash installation** — after countertops
12. **Plumbing fixtures** — sink, faucet, disposal
13. **Electrical fixtures** — lighting, outlet covers
14. **Appliance installation**
15. **Final painting and touch-up**
16. **Final inspection** (if permits were pulled)

Skipping or reordering these steps is how projects get expensive. Installing backsplash before countertops means potential rework. Setting cabinets before rough electrical means cutting into new cabinets for outlet access.

## Bundled vs. A La Carte Services

You have two main approaches to hiring:

**Full-service general contractor:** One company manages everything. They hire and coordinate subcontractors for each trade. You pay a premium (15 to 25% markup on sub costs) for the convenience of a single point of contact. Best for complex remodels involving multiple trades.

**A la carte / self-managed:** You hire each specialist directly — a cabinet installer, a countertop fabricator, a plumber, an electrician, a tile setter. You save the GC markup but take on the scheduling and coordination burden yourself. Best for simpler projects with 2 to 3 trades involved.

A hybrid approach works well for mid-range projects: hire a general contractor for the structural, plumbing, and electrical work, then handle cosmetic items (painting, hardware installation, backsplash) yourself or with individual specialists.

For compliance with residential building standards, the [ICC code](https://www.iccsafe.org/products-and-services/i-codes/2024-i-codes/irc/) is the reference point your contractor should be working from for structural, plumbing, and electrical work.

## Bottom Line

Most kitchen remodels require 4 to 6 distinct services from the list above. A mid-range project that updates cabinets, countertops, backsplash, and fixtures while keeping the layout intact typically costs $15,000 to $35,000 and takes 4 to 8 weeks.

Know which services you actually need, get the sequence right, and hire specialists for the work that requires licenses and expertise. Handle the rest yourself if you have the time and basic skills. That combination delivers the best result per dollar spent.
