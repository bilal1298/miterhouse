A new countertop costs between $600 and $6,000 for a typical kitchen, depending entirely on the material you choose and whether you hire professional installation. Laminate at the low end runs $15 to $40 per square foot installed. Quartz and granite in the middle hit $50 to $150 per square foot. Marble and exotic stone at the top push past $200 per square foot.

The material is the story here — it accounts for 50 to 70 percent of the total project cost. Labor, removal, and extras fill in the rest. Here is a realistic budget breakdown for every major countertop material available in 2026.

## Countertop Cost by Material

For a standard 40-square-foot kitchen countertop (roughly a 10-foot L-shaped counter with a 4-foot return):

| Material | Cost/SF (Installed) | 40 SF Kitchen Total | Maintenance Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laminate | $15–$40 | $600–$1,600 | Low |
| Tile | $20–$50 | $800–$2,000 | Medium (grout) |
| Butcher block | $40–$80 | $1,600–$3,200 | High |
| Solid surface (Corian) | $40–$90 | $1,600–$3,600 | Low |
| Concrete | $65–$135 | $2,600–$5,400 | Medium |
| Granite | $50–$120 | $2,000–$4,800 | Low–Medium |
| Quartz (engineered) | $55–$150 | $2,200–$6,000 | Very low |
| Quartzite (natural) | $80–$180 | $3,200–$7,200 | Medium |
| Marble | $75–$200 | $3,000–$8,000 | High |

These are installed prices including fabrication, template, and basic installation. They do not include old countertop removal, plumbing disconnection/reconnection, or backsplash work — those are separate line items covered below.

## What Is Not Included in the Per-Square-Foot Price

The per-square-foot countertop price gets all the attention, but the ancillary costs add 20 to 40 percent to the project total. Here is what to budget for on top of the counter itself.

### Old Countertop Removal

Removing your existing countertop costs $200 to $600 for professional demo, depending on the material. Laminate is quick to pull out — an hour or two. Granite or stone requires careful handling because of the weight (20 to 25 pounds per square foot) and disposal is more expensive.

**DIY option:** Removing a laminate countertop yourself takes 2 to 3 hours with a pry bar, drill, and a helper. Stone removal is heavier and risks damaging cabinets — most people hire this out. See our [countertop removal cost guide](/blog/countertop-removal-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/) for detailed pricing.

### Plumbing Disconnection and Reconnection

The sink, faucet, garbage disposal, and dishwasher connections need to come apart before the old counter comes out and go back together after the new one is set. A plumber charges $150 to $350 for disconnection and reconnection. If you are changing sink type (going from drop-in to undermount, for example) or moving the faucet location, add $200 to $500 for modifications.

A handy homeowner can handle standard plumbing disconnection and reconnection in about an hour. It is not complex — just shut off valves, disconnect supply lines and drain, then reverse the process.

### Sink Cutout

Most fabricators include one standard sink cutout in their per-square-foot price, but verify this. Additional cutouts (second sink, soap dispenser holes, cooktop cutout) often cost $50 to $200 each.

Undermount sink installation costs more than drop-in because the fabricator must polish the exposed edge of the cutout. This adds $100 to $300 to the fabrication cost.

### Edge Profiles

Basic edge profiles (eased, flat polished) are typically included. Decorative profiles — bullnose, ogee, waterfall, mitered — add $10 to $30 per linear foot. On a 15-foot countertop edge, that is $150 to $450 extra for a fancy profile.

### Backsplash

A 4-inch stone or quartz backsplash is sometimes included with countertop fabrication — it is cut from the same slab. A full-height backsplash (counter to upper cabinets) adds $20 to $60 per linear foot for stone or $15 to $40 for tile.

## How to Estimate Your Specific Cost

Follow these steps for a reasonably accurate budget:

**Step 1: Measure your countertop area.** Measure the length and depth of each section. Standard counter depth is 25.5 inches (including the overhang). Multiply length by depth for each section, convert to square feet, and add them together. Add 10 percent for waste and cuts.

**Step 2: Pick your material and multiply.** Use the cost-per-square-foot ranges above. Use the midpoint for budgeting unless you have specific quotes.

**Step 3: Add the extras.** Old counter removal ($200 to $600), plumbing ($150 to $350), edge profiles (if upgrading from standard), and backsplash if desired.

**Step 4: Add a 10 percent contingency.** Something always comes up — a cabinet that needs shimming, a plumbing fitting that is corroded, a wall that is not square.

### Example Budget: Average Kitchen

- Countertop area: 40 SF
- Material: mid-range granite at $75/SF installed = $3,000
- Old counter removal: $350
- Plumbing disconnect/reconnect: $250
- Undermount sink cutout and polishing: $200
- Standard edge profile: included
- **Subtotal: $3,800**
- 10% contingency: $380
- **Total budget: $4,180**

## Ways to Save Without Sacrificing Quality

**Remnant pieces.** Granite and quartz fabricators often have remnant slabs from larger projects. If your countertop is small enough (bathroom vanities, laundry rooms, small kitchens), a remnant can save 30 to 50 percent on the stone cost. Call local fabricators and ask what they have in stock.

**Level 1 granite.** Granite is graded by rarity and color — Level 1 (most common colors) is 30 to 40 percent cheaper than Level 3+ exotic slabs. A Level 1 Uba Tuba or New Venetian Gold is still genuine granite with full durability, just not a rare color.

**Standard edges.** Decorative edge profiles are purely aesthetic. A simple eased edge looks clean and modern and costs nothing extra.

**Keep the existing layout.** If your old countertop layout works, keeping the same footprint avoids any cabinet modifications, plumbing moves, or electrical work. The new counter templates off the existing one.

**Do your own demolition.** Removing laminate countertops is straightforward DIY. Even if you hire a fabricator for everything else, pulling out the old counter yourself saves $200 to $400.

**Shop during promotional periods.** Big box stores and fabricators run countertop promotions (often in spring and fall) that include free installation with material purchase or discounted fabrication. The Remodeling Magazine [Cost vs. Value report](https://www.remodeling.hw.net/cost-vs-value/2025/) can help you gauge whether the investment makes sense for your market.

## How Long Countertop Replacement Takes

**Laminate:** Pre-formed laminate can be purchased and installed in a single day. Custom laminate fabrication takes 1 to 2 weeks.

**Granite and quartz:** Template day, then 1 to 3 weeks for fabrication, then installation day (4 to 8 hours). Total project timeline: 2 to 4 weeks from template to completion.

**Concrete:** Custom poured countertops take 2 to 4 weeks including curing time.

**Butcher block:** Pre-made slabs can be installed in 1 to 2 days. Custom fabrication adds 1 to 2 weeks.

You will be without a kitchen sink for at least a day — plan meals accordingly. Most installers set the counter and reconnect the plumbing the same day, but complications happen.

## The Bottom Line

For most homeowners, the sweet spot is mid-range granite or entry-level quartz at $50 to $80 per square foot installed. You get genuine stone (or engineered stone) durability, attractive appearance, and low maintenance for roughly $2,500 to $4,000 for a typical kitchen — a project that returns 60 to 70 percent of its cost at resale and makes a visible daily difference in your kitchen.

Laminate at $15 to $40 per square foot remains the practical choice for rental properties, budget renovations, and situations where the countertop is not the kitchen's main event. At the other end, quartz and marble above $100 per square foot only make financial sense if you plan to stay in the home long enough to enjoy them — the resale return diminishes on ultra-premium materials.
