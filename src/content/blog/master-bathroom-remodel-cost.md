A master bathroom remodel costs anywhere from $3,000 for surface-level updates to $60,000 or more for a full gut-and-rebuild. The right budget depends almost entirely on what tier of work you're doing — and the tiers are genuinely different projects with different trade-offs.

I've broken this down the way a contractor would quote it: by project scope, not by wishful thinking. Below you'll find real cost ranges for each tier, a per-square-foot breakdown, the biggest variables that move the number, and how to build a budget before you call anyone.

---

## The Three Tiers of Master Bathroom Remodels

Not every remodel is the same job. A cosmetic refresh keeps the bones and swaps the surfaces. A mid-range remodel often means new fixtures, a new vanity, and tile work. A gut renovation tears it to the studs and starts over. The cost difference between them isn't incremental — it's a different order of magnitude.

| Project Tier | Typical Cost Range | Cost Per Sq Ft | What's Included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic Refresh | $3,000–$8,000 | $50–$100 | Paint, hardware, mirror, light fixtures, toilet, accessories |
| Mid-Range Remodel | $10,000–$25,000 | $150–$250 | New vanity, tile floors/shower surround, updated fixtures, basic layout kept |
| High-End Renovation | $30,000–$60,000+ | $300–$500+ | Custom tile, freestanding tub, frameless glass, heated floors, relocated plumbing |

Most master bathrooms run 80–120 square feet. At those sizes, a mid-range remodel lands between $12,000 and $30,000, which tracks with what local contractors are actually quoting in 2026.

## Tier 1: Cosmetic Refresh ($3,000–$8,000)

A cosmetic refresh is the right move when the layout works, the plumbing is solid, and you mostly want it to look better. You're not moving walls or drains — you're swapping everything visible.

**What's typically included:**

- Paint (walls and ceiling): $200–$600 depending on room size and whether you hire it out
- New toilet: $300–$800 installed (a decent elongated comfort-height toilet runs $200–$400, plus $100–$200 for a plumber)
- Vanity light fixture: $150–$500 installed
- Mirror replacement: $100–$600
- New faucets: $150–$400 installed per fixture
- Hardware (towel bars, TP holder, hooks): $100–$300
- Accessories and paint: $200–$500

If you [tackle parts of this yourself](/blog/diy-bathroom-remodel-cost/), you can pull the total down to $2,000–$4,000. DIY-friendly tasks: painting, swapping hardware, replacing a mirror, installing a new light fixture (if you're comfortable with basic electrical). Leave plumbing connections to a licensed plumber unless you know what you're doing — a failed supply line under a vanity creates a much bigger project.

**What a cosmetic refresh won't fix:** old tile that's cracked or discolored, a cramped layout, a shower that needs re-waterproofing, or inadequate ventilation. If those are your actual problems, a refresh just papers over them.

## Tier 2: Mid-Range Remodel ($10,000–$25,000)

This is the most common scope for a master bath project. You're keeping the general layout — toilet stays where it is, shower stays in the same corner — but you're replacing most of the major surfaces and fixtures.

**Typical line items:**

| Item | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Vanity (double, 60") with installation | $1,200–$4,500 |
| Countertop (quartz or cultured marble) | $500–$2,000 |
| Tile flooring (materials + labor) | $1,500–$4,000 |
| Shower surround tile (materials + labor) | $2,000–$6,000 |
| Shower fixture/valve replacement | $400–$1,200 |
| Toilet replacement | $300–$800 |
| Mirror(s) | $200–$800 |
| Light fixtures | $300–$1,000 |
| Exhaust fan | $200–$500 |
| Permits | $200–$500 |
| Labor (general contractor overhead) | $3,000–$7,000 |

The biggest single cost lever at this tier is tile. A 90-square-foot bathroom with a 3×6 subway tile in the shower is a very different job from the same bathroom tiled floor-to-ceiling in large-format 24×48 porcelain. Labor for large-format tile runs higher because cuts are harder and setting is more precise. Budget $12–$25 per square foot for tile labor alone on complex patterns or large format; simple subway tile runs $8–$15 per square foot installed.

For a detailed walkthrough of how these numbers come together, see our [bathroom remodel cost estimate guide](/blog/bathroom-remodel-cost-estimate/).

**Where mid-range remodels go over budget:** waterproofing surprises. When you open up an old shower surround, you sometimes find water damage behind the backer board. Replacing rotted studs and re-waterproofing correctly adds $500–$2,500 to the job. It's not optional — skip it and you'll be back in two years with a worse problem.

## Tier 3: High-End Gut Renovation ($30,000–$60,000+)

A gut renovation takes the bathroom to the studs. You're relocating fixtures, adding luxury finishes, and rebuilding the room from scratch. This is the tier where you get a freestanding soaking tub, a curbless walk-in shower with a linear drain, radiant heated floors, and a custom double vanity.

**What drives costs at this tier:**

**Plumbing relocation** is the single biggest variable. Moving a toilet costs $500–$1,500. Moving a drain — which is required for most shower relocations or tub swaps — means cutting into the subfloor and potentially the concrete slab if you're on the ground floor. Slab work runs $1,000–$3,000 per fixture. If you're on a second floor with wood framing, it's less invasive but still adds up.

**Walk-in shower** with frameless glass enclosure, a linear drain, and large-format tile: $8,000–$20,000 depending on size and tile selection. For a detailed breakdown, see our [walk-in shower remodel cost guide](/blog/walk-in-shower-remodel-cost/).

**Freestanding soaking tub:** $1,500–$6,000 for the tub itself, plus $500–$1,200 for the floor-mount filler and installation. Freestanding tubs look great in photos but require more floor space than alcove tubs — don't specify one unless the room can actually breathe around it.

**Heated tile floors (electric radiant):** $8–$15 per square foot for the mat, plus $200–$500 for a thermostat and electrician time. For an 80-square-foot bathroom, budget $900–$1,700 installed. It's one of the most cost-effective luxury upgrades per dollar spent — minimal structural work, real daily comfort.

**Custom cabinetry vs. stock:** A stock 60" double vanity from a big-box store runs $600–$1,200. A semi-custom version from a kitchen/bath dealer: $1,500–$3,500. A fully custom built-in: $4,000–$10,000+. Custom is worth it when the layout demands it — odd dimensions, built-in storage towers, integrated linen cabinet. Otherwise, semi-custom gets you most of the look for half the price.

| Category | Low End | High End |
|---|---|---|
| Demolition | $800 | $1,500 |
| Plumbing (fixture relocation) | $2,000 | $5,000 |
| Electrical (GFCI, heated floor, new circuits) | $1,500 | $3,500 |
| Waterproofing | $800 | $1,800 |
| Tile (floors + shower, materials + labor) | $6,000 | $18,000 |

Most homeowners doing a genuine high-end gut land between $35,000 and $55,000. Numbers above $60,000 typically involve luxury materials (natural stone, heated towel rails, steam shower), a large room, or significant structural changes.

## What Moves the Number Most

Five variables have more impact on final cost than anything else:

**1. Whether you move plumbing.** Keeping fixtures in their current locations saves $2,000–$8,000. Moving them — especially on a slab foundation — is the fastest way to blow a budget.

**2. Tile selection and square footage.** Tile is both a material cost and a labor multiplier. A 100-square-foot shower in natural stone costs 3–4x more than the same shower in large-format porcelain. Porcelain performs comparably for most applications.

**3. Labor market.** In major metros (NYC, San Francisco, Seattle), labor rates run 30–50% higher than national averages. In the Midwest and South, you'll often come in under the ranges quoted here.

**4. Permit requirements.** Some jurisdictions require permits for any plumbing or electrical work; others only require them for structural changes. Skipping a required permit creates title problems when you sell. Budget $200–$600 and pull the permits.

**5. What you find in the walls.** Mold remediation adds $500–$3,000. Replacing a deteriorated subfloor adds $500–$1,500. These aren't rare — they're routine in bathrooms older than 15 years. Build a 15% contingency into any mid-range or gut renovation budget.

## Will You Get Your Money Back?

A master bathroom remodel is not a pure financial investment — no renovation is — but it's among the better-returning projects in the house. Mid-range master bath remodels recoup roughly 60–70% of cost at resale. Upscale remodels come in slightly lower because luxury finishes appeal to a narrower buyer pool.

The practical calculus: if you're selling within two years, do the mid-range work that makes the room competitive, not the high-end project that adds marginal resale value over what the neighborhood supports. If you're staying for five or more years, the daily use math changes significantly.

## How to Get an Accurate Quote

Before you call a contractor, have these decisions made:

- **Scope:** Is this cosmetic, mid-range, or gut? Are you moving any plumbing?
- **Fixtures:** Have a specific toilet, vanity, and shower fixture in mind, even if just a price-tier preference.
- **Tile:** Narrowing down to a category (subway, large-format porcelain, natural stone) gives a contractor enough to quote realistically.
- **Timeline:** Are you flexible, or do you have a hard deadline? Rush jobs cost more.

Get three quotes minimum. Ask each contractor to provide a line-item breakdown, not a lump-sum number. The breakdown tells you where the money is going and makes apples-to-apples comparison possible. Any contractor unwilling to provide one is a red flag.

The cheapest quote is rarely the right quote in a bathroom — plumbing, waterproofing, and tile are all areas where cutting corners creates expensive problems years later. Look for the contractor who can explain their process, not just their price.
