Knowing how to shut off your water heater is a basic homeowner skill that can prevent serious damage in an emergency and save you from unnecessary service calls. Whether you are dealing with a leak, leaving for vacation, or prepping for maintenance, the shutdown process is straightforward — but the order of operations matters, especially with gas units where doing things in the wrong sequence creates safety risks.

The entire shutdown takes under 5 minutes once you know where your controls are. The restart takes 5 to 15 minutes depending on your heater type. Doing this wrong is one of the most common ways homeowners damage their water heater or create a dangerous situation, so take a minute to read through the correct procedure for your specific unit.

## Find Your Water Heater Type First

Before you touch anything, identify what you are working with. This determines the shutdown sequence.

**Gas water heaters** have a gas control valve (a box near the bottom of the tank with a dial or knob), a thermocouple or thermopile wire, and a gas supply line with a shutoff valve. The gas supply line is typically a yellow flexible connector or black iron pipe.

**Electric water heaters** do not have a gas valve or burner. They are powered by a dedicated 240-volt circuit with a double-pole breaker in your electrical panel. The wiring connects at a junction box on the side or top of the heater.

**Tankless water heaters** (gas or electric) have their own shutdown procedures specific to the brand and model. Refer to the manufacturer's manual for tankless units.

## Shutting Off a Gas Water Heater

Follow this sequence exactly. The order prevents gas buildup and protects the tank.

### Step 1: Turn the Thermostat to Pilot or Off

Locate the gas control valve on the front of the heater near the bottom. It has a temperature dial and typically a separate on/off/pilot selector. Turn the selector to "pilot" if you want the pilot light to stay on (for short shutdowns like vacations), or to "off" for complete shutdowns like maintenance or leak situations.

Turning to "pilot" keeps the pilot flame burning but stops the main burner from firing. This is the right choice for vacations or seasonal shutdowns because relighting the pilot later is easy.

Turning to "off" extinguishes everything. This is necessary for maintenance, repairs, or if you smell gas.

### Step 2: Turn Off the Gas Supply Valve

Follow the gas line from the heater back toward the wall or floor. You will find a shutoff valve — usually a ball valve with a lever handle or a gate valve with a round handle. Turn it perpendicular to the pipe (ball valve) or clockwise until snug (gate valve) to stop gas flow.

This step is mandatory for maintenance work, leak situations, or anytime you are disconnecting the gas line. For a simple vacation shutdown where you have set the control to "pilot," turning off the gas supply is optional but provides an extra layer of safety.

### Step 3: Turn Off the Cold Water Supply

The cold water supply valve is on the pipe entering the top of the heater (typically on the right when facing the unit, marked with a blue handle or tag). Turn it clockwise to close.

If you cannot find the dedicated valve or it is stuck, shut off the main water supply to the house instead. A stuck water heater shutoff valve is common on older installations — do not force it, as the valve can break and flood the area. Plan to replace it during your next [plumbing maintenance visit](/blog/home-plumbing-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/).

### Step 4 (Emergency Only): Drain the Tank

If the heater is leaking from the tank itself, you need to drain it to stop the water damage. Connect a garden hose to the drain valve, route it to a floor drain or outside, open a hot water faucet in the house to break the vacuum, and open the drain valve. See our detailed [flushing guide](/blog/how-often-should-i-flush-my-water-heater-honest-guide/) for the full draining procedure.

## Shutting Off an Electric Water Heater

Electric water heaters are simpler because there is no gas to manage, but the electrical component demands respect.

### Step 1: Turn Off the Breaker

Go to your electrical panel and find the breaker labeled "water heater" (it should be a double-pole 30-amp breaker for most residential units). Flip it to the off position. If your panel is not labeled, look for a 30-amp double-pole breaker — these are typically used exclusively for water heaters and AC units.

Do not rely on turning down the thermostat as a substitute for cutting power. The thermostat controls when the elements heat, but power is still flowing to the unit, and a thermostat malfunction could cause the elements to fire unexpectedly.

### Step 2: Turn Off the Cold Water Supply

Same as with a gas heater — close the valve on the cold water pipe entering the heater.

### Step 3 (If Needed): Drain the Tank

Follow the same procedure as for gas heaters. The critical difference: make absolutely sure the breaker is off before draining. Running electric heating elements in an empty or partially empty tank destroys them within minutes. Replacement elements cost $15 to $40 each, but the labor to swap them adds $100 to $200 if you hire a plumber.

## Common Shutdown Scenarios

### Going on Vacation (1 to 4 Weeks)

For gas heaters, turn the control to "pilot" or set the thermostat to the lowest setting (often labeled "vacation" or "VAC"). This keeps the pilot lit for easy restart while minimizing gas consumption. You will save roughly $5 to $15 per week in energy costs.

For electric heaters, you can turn off the breaker entirely. There is no pilot to maintain. Restart is as simple as flipping the breaker back on.

In both cases, leave the cold water supply on. Draining the tank for a short absence is unnecessary and creates more hassle on your return.

### Leak Detected

Shut off both the fuel/power source and the cold water supply immediately. If water is pooling, drain the tank. Then assess the source of the leak. Common leak locations and their implications:

- **TPR valve discharge pipe:** The temperature-pressure relief valve is doing its job. Possible causes include a failed thermostat (overheating), excessive pressure from the municipal supply, or a failed expansion tank.
- **Fittings at the top of the tank:** Usually a loose connection or failed dielectric union. Tightening or replacing the fitting often solves it.
- **Drain valve:** A weeping drain valve is common after flushing. Try opening and closing it to clear debris, or cap it with a brass hose cap.
- **Tank body (bottom or sides):** Internal corrosion has compromised the tank. This is not repairable — you need a [new water heater](/blog/installing-tank-water-heater-what-to-know-first/). Check our guide on [whether water heater leaks are normal](/blog/is-it-normal-for-water-heater-to-leak-honest-guide/) for more detail.

### Before Maintenance

For [routine flushing](/blog/how-often-should-i-flush-my-water-heater-honest-guide/), shut off the heat source and wait 1 to 2 hours for the water to cool below scalding temperature before draining. For anode rod replacement or thermostat work, shut off both the heat source and the cold water supply.

### Extended Shutdown (Winterizing)

If you are closing a seasonal home, drain the tank completely, open all hot water faucets to drain the lines, and shut off the main water supply. For gas heaters, turn the control to "off" and close the gas supply valve. For electric heaters, turn off the breaker. This prevents frozen pipes from cracking and damaging the heater and plumbing.

## Restarting After a Shutdown

**Electric heaters:** Turn on the cold water supply, let the tank fill completely (you will know it is full when water flows steadily from an open hot faucet), then turn on the breaker. Per [NFPA safety codes](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70), never energize an empty electric water heater.

**Gas heaters:** Turn on the cold water supply, let the tank fill, then relight the pilot following the instructions on the label affixed to the heater. Modern gas heaters use a piezo igniter — press and hold the pilot button while pressing the igniter until the pilot lights, then continue holding the pilot button for 30 to 60 seconds before releasing. Turn the control from "pilot" to "on" and set your desired temperature.

If the pilot will not light after three attempts, stop. You may have a [faulty igniter](/blog/water-heater-igniter-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/) or a gas supply issue. Continuing to press the igniter while gas flows can create a dangerous situation. Wait 10 minutes for any accumulated gas to dissipate, ensure adequate ventilation, and try once more. If it still fails, call a licensed plumber or your gas utility.

## Know Your Heater Before You Need To

The worst time to figure out how to shut off your water heater is during an emergency at 2 AM with water spreading across the floor. Take 5 minutes right now to locate your gas valve or breaker, find the cold water shutoff, and confirm the drain valve has a hose connection. Label anything that is not obvious. When you need this knowledge, you will be glad you did the prep work when things were calm and dry.
