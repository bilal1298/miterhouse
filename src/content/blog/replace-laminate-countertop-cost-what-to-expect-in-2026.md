Replacing laminate countertops with new laminate costs $500–$2,000 for a typical kitchen, making it the most affordable countertop replacement option by a significant margin. If you're upgrading from laminate to a different material like quartz or granite, expect $2,500–$6,000+. The beauty of laminate-to-laminate replacement is the simplicity — it's one of the few countertop swaps most homeowners can handle themselves in a weekend.

## Laminate-to-Laminate Replacement Costs

### Post-Form Laminate (Pre-Built)

Post-form countertops come as finished pieces with the laminate already applied and the front edge and backsplash lip pre-formed. You buy them at home centers in standard lengths (4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 feet) and cut to fit.

- **Material cost:** $12–$30 per linear foot (standard 25.5-inch depth)
- **Miter kit for corners:** $15–$25
- **End caps and end splash kits:** $5–$15 each
- **Sink cutout template:** Usually comes with the sink
- **Total materials for a 10-foot L-shaped kitchen:** $250–$550

Post-form laminate is available in 100+ patterns at major retailers. The Formica 180fx, Wilsonart HD, and VT Industries lines offer convincing stone and wood-look patterns that are dramatically better than laminate from even 10 years ago.

### Custom Laminate

For non-standard depths, unusual shapes, or premium laminate patterns, you'll need custom fabrication.

- **Material cost:** $25–$50 per square foot (materials + fabrication)
- **Installation labor:** $15–$25 per linear foot
- **Total for the same 10-foot L-shaped kitchen:** $800–$1,800

Custom laminate gives you access to hundreds of additional patterns, edge profile options (square, beveled, bull-nose), and integration with undermount sinks (using solid surface buildup at the sink edge).

### What's Included in the Price

A complete laminate replacement quote should cover:

- Old countertop removal and disposal
- New countertop fabrication or sourcing
- Cutting to size (including sink and cooktop openings)
- Joining sections at corners
- Securing to cabinets
- Caulking at walls
- Basic cleanup

**What's typically NOT included:**
- Plumbing disconnect/reconnect ($100–$250)
- New sink or faucet (if desired)
- Backsplash repair or replacement
- Cooktop/range disconnect and reinstall
- Disposal hookup

## Upgrading From Laminate to Other Materials

If you're ready to step up from laminate, here's what the upgrade path looks like for a standard 35-square-foot kitchen:

| Upgrade To | Added Cost Over Laminate | Key Trade-Off |
|-----------|-------------------------|---------------|
| Butcher block | +$600–$1,500 | Warm look, requires maintenance |
| Solid surface (Corian) | +$1,200–$2,500 | Seamless appearance, repairable scratches |
| Granite (mid-range) | +$1,500–$3,000 | Natural beauty, annual sealing |
| Quartz (mid-range) | +$2,000–$3,500 | Low maintenance, consistent color |
| Marble | +$2,500–$5,000+ | Luxury look, high maintenance |

The upgrade from laminate to quartz is the most popular path — and for good reason. Quartz delivers the "solid surface" visual impact, requires minimal maintenance, and offers enough color/pattern variety to match any kitchen style. The $2,000–$3,500 premium over laminate for a standard kitchen pays for itself in durability and resale value.

## DIY Laminate Replacement: Step by Step

This is one of the few countertop projects where DIY genuinely makes sense and saves significant money.

### Tools You Need

- Circular saw with a fine-tooth blade (60+ teeth)
- Jigsaw for sink cutout
- Drill/driver
- Belt sander or file for edge trimming
- Level (4-foot)
- Tape measure and speed square
- Caulk gun and silicone caulk
- Safety glasses and dust mask

Total tool investment (if buying): $150–$300. Most homeowners already own the drill and jigsaw.

### The Process

**Remove the old countertop.** Turn off water supply valves. Disconnect supply lines and drain under the sink. Remove any clips or screws holding the countertop from below. Lift the old top off — laminate on particleboard weighs 30–50 pounds for a 6-foot section, manageable for one person.

**Inspect the cabinets.** Check for water damage around the sink area. If the cabinet base is soft, warped, or shows mold, repair before installing new countertops. A new countertop on a rotten cabinet base is lipstick on a problem.

**Measure and cut the new countertop.** For post-form laminate, measure the wall-to-wall distance at the back of the cabinets. Cut with a circular saw from the back (underside up) to prevent chipping on the visible surface. Leave 1/16-inch gap at walls for expansion — caulk covers this.

**Join corner sections.** Apply connector bolts (draw bolts) through routed channels on the underside. Apply silicone at the joint before tightening. Wipe excess silicone immediately. The joint should be tight with no visible gap from above.

**Cut the sink opening.** Position the sink template on the countertop. Trace. Drill a starter hole just inside the line. Cut with a jigsaw from above, cutting just inside the line (you can't add material back, but you can file the edge wider). Support the cutout piece as you finish — a falling cutout can crack the laminate edge.

**Secure to cabinets.** Screw through corner brackets into the countertop from below. Don't over-tighten — laminate countertops can bow if pulled too aggressively against an uneven cabinet top.

**Reconnect plumbing.** Reinstall the faucet and sink (or install new ones), reconnect supply lines and drain. Turn on water and check for leaks.

**Caulk.** Run a bead of color-matched or clear silicone along the countertop-to-wall joint and around the sink rim.

Total DIY time: 4–8 hours for a straightforward L-shaped kitchen. Budget a full weekend for your first project.

## When Laminate Is the Smart Choice

Laminate gets dismissed as the "cheap option," but there are legitimate reasons to choose it intentionally:

**Rental properties.** Laminate delivers a clean, updated look at minimal cost. If tenants damage it, replacement is inexpensive.

**Short-term ownership.** If you're selling in 2–3 years, the $3,000+ premium for quartz may not return at sale — especially if the rest of the kitchen is dated. A fresh laminate countertop for $500 paired with new hardware and paint creates a better overall impression than quartz on tired cabinets.

**Phase one of a larger renovation.** If you plan a full kitchen remodel in 3–5 years but need functional countertops now, laminate bridges the gap without wasting money on a premium surface that'll be ripped out.

**Bathrooms.** Laminate vanity tops (especially prefab options with integrated sinks) are completely appropriate for guest bathrooms and secondary baths where the countertop isn't the design focus.

## Modern Laminate: Not Your Parents' Formica

Today's high-pressure laminates are dramatically better than what was standard 20 years ago:

- **Texture:** Matte, honed, leather, and brushed finishes that mimic real stone texture
- **Patterns:** Photographic reproductions of Carrara marble, concrete, quartzite, and exotic woods that are convincing from normal viewing distance
- **Durability:** Scratch resistance has improved significantly — premium lines handle normal kitchen use without visible wear
- **Antimicrobial options:** Some lines include antimicrobial surface treatment (useful for food prep areas)
- **Sustainability:** Many modern laminates are made from recycled content and are Greenguard certified for low emissions

The gap between laminate and natural stone has narrowed in appearance, though it remains significant in terms of heat resistance (no hot pots directly on laminate), resale perception, and longevity.
