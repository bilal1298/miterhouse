Every house has plumbing problems. The question is whether the repair costs you $10 in parts or $500 in service calls. Most home plumbing repairs are simpler than people think, and the ones that genuinely require a professional are easy to identify once you know what to look for.

This guide walks through the 10 most common home plumbing repairs, ranked from easiest to hardest, with honest assessments of cost, difficulty, and when to put down the wrench and pick up the phone.

## The Easy Fixes (Under 30 Minutes, Under $20)

### Fixing a Running Toilet

A running toilet wastes 200 or more gallons per day and is almost always caused by one of three things: a worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve, or a faulty flush valve seal. A universal toilet repair kit costs $10 to $20 and includes all three parts. Shut off the supply valve, flush to empty the tank, and swap the parts. No tools required beyond your hands.

### Clearing a Slow Drain

Before you reach for chemical drain cleaner (please do not), try a drain snake. A 25-foot hand-crank snake costs $20 to $40 and clears most household clogs. For bathroom sinks, the clog is almost always hair wrapped around the stopper mechanism. Remove the stopper, clean it, and you are done.

### Replacing a Showerhead

Unscrew the old one. Wrap the threads with Teflon tape (three wraps clockwise). Screw on the new one. The [EPA's WaterSense program](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) certifies showerheads that use 2.0 gallons per minute or less without sacrificing pressure. A good one costs $15 to $40.

### Fixing a Dripping Faucet

Most dripping faucets need a new cartridge or O-ring. Identify your faucet brand and style, then order the specific replacement cartridge. YouTube videos for your exact model walk you through the 15-minute swap. Parts cost $10 to $30 depending on the brand.

## The Moderate Fixes (1-3 Hours, $20-$150)

### Replacing a Kitchen or Bathroom Faucet

New faucets come with detailed instructions and most of the hardware you need. The hardest part is working in the cramped space under the sink. A basin wrench ($15) makes the job dramatically easier. Budget 1 to 2 hours and expect at least one trip back to the hardware store for a fitting you did not anticipate.

Cost: $80 to $250 for a decent faucet, plus $15 to $30 in supply lines and fittings.

### Replacing a Toilet

Toilets are held down by two bolts and connected by a single supply line. Removing the old one and setting the new one takes about an hour for someone who has not done it before. The tricky part is setting the wax ring correctly. Buy two wax rings (they are $3 each) so you have a backup if the first set does not seal.

You will need a helper for lifting. A standard toilet weighs 60 to 80 pounds. Our guide on [toilet repair](/blog/plumbing-toilet-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/) covers more on this topic.

### Replacing Supply Valves

The angle stops (shutoff valves) under sinks and behind toilets degrade over time. If you find one that will not turn or leaks when you try to close it, replace it. This requires shutting off the main water supply. Compression-fit replacement valves install without soldering and cost $8 to $15 each.

### Installing a Garbage Disposal

If you already have the electrical connection and the drain setup, swapping a garbage disposal takes about an hour. If you need to add the electrical outlet (usually a switched outlet under the sink), hire an electrician for that part. The disposal itself runs $80 to $300 depending on horsepower and features.

## The Jobs That Need a Pro

### Anything Behind a Wall

If the leak or problem is behind drywall, in the ceiling, or under a concrete slab, call a plumber. They have camera inspection equipment to find the exact location without unnecessary demolition, and they carry the insurance to cover damage to your home.

### Sewer and Main Line Work

Your main sewer line connects your home to the municipal sewer or septic system. Problems here (tree roots, bellied pipe, offsets) require specialized equipment and almost always require permits. A camera inspection costs $100 to $300 and tells you exactly what you are dealing with before anyone starts digging.

### Gas Line Repairs

Gas line work requires a licensed plumber or gas fitter in every jurisdiction. The risk of explosion or carbon monoxide poisoning is not theoretical -- it is the reason these laws exist. Safety standards from [OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/) and local codes are non-negotiable.

### Water Heater Installation

While some of the preparation is DIY-friendly (draining the old unit, clearing the area), the actual gas or electrical connections need a licensed professional. See our complete guide on [water tank heater installation](/blog/installing-water-tank-heater-what-to-know-first/).

### Repiping

If your home has galvanized or polybutylene pipes, a whole-house repipe is a major project that affects every wall and ceiling in the house. This is a multi-day professional job costing $4,000 to $15,000. Get three quotes and check references.

## How to Prevent the Most Expensive Repairs

The best plumbing repair is the one you never need. These maintenance habits prevent most emergency calls:

**Quarterly:** Pour a gallon of hot (not boiling) water down each rarely-used drain to keep the P-traps from drying out and sewer gas from entering.

**Twice a year:** Check under every sink for drips, moisture, or soft spots. A slow leak under a cabinet can cause thousands in damage before you notice.

**Annually:** Flush your water heater by connecting a garden hose to the drain valve and running water until it is clear. This removes sediment that reduces efficiency and shortens the tank's life.

**Every 5 years:** Replace washing machine supply hoses, dishwasher supply hoses, and toilet supply lines. These are the quiet time bombs of home plumbing.

**Before winter:** Disconnect garden hoses, shut off exterior hose bibs (if you have interior shutoffs for them), and insulate any exposed pipes in unheated spaces.

## Building Your Home Plumbing Toolkit

You do not need a plumber's truck worth of tools. These handle 80% of home plumbing repairs:

| Tool | Cost | What It Does |
|------|------|-------------|
| Adjustable wrench (10") | $12 | Everything with nuts and bolts |
| Channel-lock pliers (2 sizes) | $25 | Gripping and turning pipe fittings |
| Basin wrench | $15 | Reaching faucet nuts in tight spaces |
| Hand-crank drain snake (25') | $25 | Clearing clogs without chemicals |
| Plunger (flange type) | $10 | First response for toilet clogs |
| Teflon tape | $3 | Sealing threaded connections |
| Pipe cutter | $15 | Clean cuts on copper pipe |
| Hacksaw | $12 | Cutting PVC and other materials |

Total: roughly $120 for a kit that will serve you for years.

## When the Estimate Comes In

If you do call a plumber, make the interaction productive. Describe the problem clearly, including when it started and what you have already tried. Ask for a written estimate that breaks out parts and labor. Ask whether the quoted price includes pulling any required permits.

For jobs over $500, always get at least three quotes. The range between the lowest and highest bid on the same job can be 100% or more. The lowest bid is not always the best value, and the highest is not always the best quality. Check licenses at [UL](https://www.ul.com/) or your state licensing board, verify insurance, and read reviews.

For emergencies, see our guide on [emergency plumbing repair](/blog/emergency-plumbing-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/) for what to do before the plumber arrives. And for pipe-specific issues, our [pipe repair guide](/blog/plumbing-pipe-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/) breaks down each pipe type and the repairs that apply.

The more you understand about your home's plumbing, the less you spend on it over time. Start with the easy fixes, build your confidence, and you will know exactly when a job needs professional hands.
