Painting is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost home improvements you can make — and also one where the range between "what you expected to pay" and "what it actually costs" can be surprisingly wide. The average painting cost depends on whether you are doing interior or exterior work, hiring a pro or doing it yourself, and how much prep work the surfaces need. Here is a grounded pricing guide covering all the common scenarios.

## Interior Painting Costs

### Professional Interior Painting

Professional interior painting is typically priced per square foot of wall area or per room. Here are realistic 2026 numbers:

**Per square foot (wall area):** $2–$6/sq ft. This includes two coats, basic prep (filling small holes, light sanding), primer where needed, and cleanup. A standard 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has roughly 384 square feet of wall area (minus doors and windows, effectively about 320 paintable square feet).

**Per room pricing:**

| Room Type | Budget Range | Mid-Range | Premium |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| Bedroom (12x12) | $350–$500 | $500–$800 | $800–$1,200 |
| Living room (15x20) | $500–$800 | $800–$1,200 | $1,200–$1,800 |
| Kitchen | $400–$700 | $700–$1,100 | $1,100–$1,500 |
| Bathroom | $250–$400 | $400–$600 | $600–$900 |
| Hallway/stairwell | $300–$600 | $600–$1,000 | $1,000–$1,500 |

**Whole house interior (average 3-bedroom):** $3,000–$7,000 for walls and ceilings. Add $1,000–$3,000 for trim, doors, and woodwork.

What drives the price toward the higher end: tall ceilings (anything over 9 feet requires ladders or scaffolding), heavy prep work (peeling paint, multiple patch repairs, wallpaper removal), dark-to-light color changes requiring extra coats, and high-end paint selection.

### DIY Interior Painting Costs

Doing it yourself cuts the cost by roughly 70–80%, with the tradeoff being your time.

**Paint:** $30–$70 per gallon. One gallon covers approximately 350–400 square feet with one coat, so a 12x12 bedroom needs about 2 gallons for two coats. Budget vs. premium paint matters more than most people realize — a $45–$55 gallon from a quality brand (Benjamin Moore, Sherwin-Williams, PPG) covers better, lasts longer, and is easier to work with than a $25 gallon.

**Supplies for a typical room:**
- Paint: $60–$140 (2 gallons)
- Roller covers (3-pack): $12–$18
- Roller frame and extension pole: $15–$25
- 2.5" angled brush (for cutting in): $12–$20
- Painter's tape: $5–$8 per roll (plan on 2–3 rolls per room)
- Drop cloths: $10–$20
- Spackle, putty knife, sandpaper: $10–$15

**Total DIY cost per room: $125–$250** — roughly one-third to one-fifth of professional pricing.

**Time estimate:** A first-timer should budget 6–10 hours for a standard bedroom including prep, two coats, and cleanup. An experienced DIYer can do it in 4–6 hours. The prep and cutting-in take more time than the rolling.

## Exterior Painting Costs

Exterior painting costs more than interior due to the prep work required, the need for specialized equipment, and exposure conditions.

### Professional Exterior Painting

**Average cost for a 2-story home (1,500–2,500 sq ft exterior surface):** $3,500–$8,000. This includes pressure washing, scraping loose paint, caulking gaps, priming bare wood, two coats of quality exterior paint, and trim painting.

Factors that increase exterior cost:
- **Height.** Two-story homes cost 30–50% more than single-story due to ladder and scaffolding work
- **Surface condition.** Extensive scraping and priming on peeling surfaces adds $1,000–$3,000
- **Material.** Wood siding needs more prep than vinyl or fiber cement. Stucco requires different products and application methods.
- **Lead paint.** Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. Per [EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) regulations](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program), contractors must be RRP-certified when disturbing lead paint, adding $500–$2,000 in compliance costs.
- **Number of colors.** A single body color plus one trim color is standard. Three or more colors (body, trim, accent, door) increases labor significantly.

For detailed exterior painting numbers, see our [exterior painting cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-exterior-home-painting-really-cost/) and [outdoor painting cost guide](/blog/outside-painting-cost-what-to-expect-in-2026/).

### DIY Exterior Painting

DIY exterior painting saves money but is substantially more work than interior and carries fall risks when working at height.

**Paint:** $40–$80 per gallon for quality exterior paint. A 2-story home needs 10–20 gallons depending on surface texture and porosity. Budget $400–$1,200 for paint alone.

**Equipment rental:** A pressure washer rental runs $75–$150/day. If you need scaffolding: $150–$300/week.

**Total DIY cost for a typical home: $600–$2,000** — roughly 20–30% of professional pricing. Time investment: 40–80 hours spread over multiple weekends.

## What Drives Professional Pricing

Understanding how painters calculate their quotes helps you evaluate bids and identify when you are overpaying.

### Labor (60–70% of the Total)

Professional painters charge $25–$50 per hour per painter in most markets, though you rarely see this on the quote — it is built into the square-foot or per-room pricing. A two-person crew working an 8-hour day costs the painting company $400–$800 in direct labor. Overhead, insurance, and profit margin bring the company's daily cost to $600–$1,500.

### Materials (10–20% of the Total)

Painters buy paint at contractor pricing — roughly 30–40% below retail. Even so, premium paint on a whole-house interior job costs $500–$1,500. Painters mark up materials 15–30%, which is standard practice.

### Prep Work (10–25% of the Total)

This is where quotes diverge the most. A painter who spends three hours prepping a room (filling holes, sanding, taping, caulking) produces noticeably better results than one who rolls over everything in 45 minutes. Ask specifically what prep work is included in the quote.

## How to Evaluate Painting Quotes

Get at least three written quotes. When comparing:

**Make sure you are comparing the same scope.** Does each quote include the same number of rooms? Ceilings? Trim and doors? Two coats or one? Confirm these details in writing.

**Ask about the paint.** Quality varies enormously. If one quote is significantly cheaper, they may be using builder-grade paint that covers poorly and wears quickly. Ask for the specific product — brand and product line.

**Ask about prep.** "We prep everything" is not an answer. Ask: "Do you fill nail holes? Sand between coats? Caulk gaps between trim and walls? How do you handle existing damage?" The answers distinguish a quality painter from a quick-job operator.

**Check for lead paint protocols.** If your home was built before 1978, ask whether the painter is [EPA lead-safe certified](https://www.epa.gov/lead). This is a legal requirement, not optional, and per the [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/), disturbing lead paint improperly creates serious health hazards, particularly for children.

**Timeline matters.** A painter who promises a 5-room interior in one day is cutting corners on prep, dry time between coats, or both. Two coats of quality paint require 2–4 hours of dry time between them (longer in humid conditions). A realistic timeline for 5 rooms: 3–4 days for a two-person crew.

## Where to Spend and Where to Save

**Spend more on paint.** The difference between a $30 gallon and a $50 gallon is roughly $100–$200 per room — a small percentage of the total project cost. Premium paint covers better (often one-coating dark colors), levels smoother, resists scuffing and cleaning better, and lasts years longer. This is the one place where spending more genuinely pays off.

**Spend more on prep.** Whether DIY or professional, prep work determines the final result more than paint quality or rolling technique. Every unfilled hole, unscraped edge, and uncaulked gap shows through the finished paint.

**Save on labor for simple rooms.** Rooms with few windows, no high ceilings, and minimal trim are easy to paint yourself. The savings are significant and the skill required is minimal.

**Save on color consultations.** Paint stores offer free color consultations. Buy sample pots ($5–$8 each) and test colors on the actual wall. Digital color tools are unreliable — they cannot account for your room's lighting.

## Hidden Costs to Watch For

Several costs catch homeowners by surprise:

- **Wallpaper removal:** $1–$3/sq ft if the previous owner wallpapered. Strippable wallpaper is quick; paste-backed vinyl over unprimed drywall is a nightmare that may require skim coating.
- **Drywall repair:** Small holes and cracks are usually included in prep. Large holes, water damage, or significant texture matching costs extra — $100–$400 per area.
- **Ceiling painting:** Often quoted separately. Ceilings take longer than walls due to the overhead position and the need for flawless coverage (any miss shows in raking light).
- **Moving furniture:** Most painters expect you to move furniture or charge $50–$200 to do it. Heavy items need to go at least to the center of the room, covered with drop cloths.

## The Painting Math That Matters

When deciding between DIY and hiring out, run this calculation:

Professional cost for your project minus DIY material cost equals the labor premium. Divide that by the hours the project will take you as a DIY effort. That gives you the effective hourly rate you are "earning" by doing it yourself.

Example: Professional quote of $2,500 for a 3-bedroom interior. DIY materials: $600. Labor premium: $1,900. Estimated DIY time: 30 hours. Effective rate: $63/hour.

For most homeowners, that math works strongly in favor of DIY for interior painting. For exterior painting at height, the math may favor hiring out when you factor in equipment rental, time, and risk.

The bottom line on average painting cost: interior rooms run $350–$800 each when professionally painted, or $125–$250 in materials for DIY. Exterior work ranges from $3,500–$8,000 for professional service. The quality of the result depends far more on prep work and paint quality than on who holds the roller.
