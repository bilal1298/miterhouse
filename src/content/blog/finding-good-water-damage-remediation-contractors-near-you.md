Your basement is flooding at 2 a.m., you're panicking, and the first Google result for "water damage company near me" is a predatory operator who will overcharge you by thousands. This is the worst possible time to vet a contractor — and the water damage remediation industry knows it. Companies count on your panic to close deals fast, before you ask questions. Here's how to avoid that trap and hire someone legitimate, whether you're in crisis mode right now or planning ahead.

## The Problem: You're Making a $5,000 Decision in a Panic

Water damage remediation for a single room runs $1,500–$4,000. Multi-room or whole-floor damage hits $3,000–$10,000 for remediation alone, before reconstruction. And you're making this hiring decision while water is actively destroying your home, your adrenaline is through the roof, and you've never dealt with this trade before.

The fix is simple in concept: find one or two vetted companies before you ever need them. Put their numbers in your phone right now. When the pipe bursts or the [plumbing emergency](/blog/emergency-plumbing-repairs-a-homeowners-guide/) escalates to flooding, you call a name you already trust instead of Googling through tears.

If you're reading this during an active emergency, skip to the "Red Flags" section — knowing what to avoid is more actionable right now than knowing what to look for.

## What Remediation Companies Actually Do

A remediation company handles the emergency response and drying phase — they're not general contractors who rebuild. Their scope:

- **Water extraction** using truck-mounted or portable equipment
- **Structural drying** with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers
- **Moisture monitoring** throughout the drying process
- **Antimicrobial treatment** to prevent mold growth
- **Content pack-out** — moving and storing your belongings during restoration
- **Mold remediation** if mold is already present
- **Demolition of damaged materials** — cutting out wet drywall, pulling up ruined flooring

They do not handle reconstruction — hanging new drywall, painting, installing new flooring, and finish work. Some larger companies offer both or subcontract the rebuild, but these are separate scopes with separate pricing.

## How to Find Legitimate Companies

### Certification Is the Baseline

The water damage remediation industry has a primary certifying body: the Institute of Inspection Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC). Look for companies whose technicians hold the IICRC's Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) certification. Any company without it is not worth your time.

Additional certifications that indicate higher competency:
- **Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT)** — for mold remediation
- **Applied Structural Drying (ASD)** — advanced drying techniques
- **Commercial Drying Specialist (CDS)** — for larger or more complex losses

### Insurance and Licensing

Verify before signing anything:
- General liability insurance ($1 million minimum per occurrence)
- Workers' compensation for all employees
- State contractor's license if required in your state
- EPA certification for lead-safe practices if your home is pre-1978

Ask for certificates of insurance directly — don't accept the contractor's word. A legitimate company provides these readily.

### Where to Search

**Your insurance company's preferred vendor list.** Most insurers maintain approved remediation companies. These firms have been vetted, agreed to the insurer's pricing guidelines, and can often bill the insurance company directly. Using a preferred vendor simplifies claims, though you're never required to use one.

**IICRC's directory.** Search the IICRC website for certified firms in your area.

**Referrals from your plumber.** Plumbers respond to water emergencies regularly and know which remediation companies do quality work.

**Neighborhood groups and forums.** First-hand experience reports are more valuable than online reviews, which can be manipulated.

## Red Flags: Who to Avoid

The water damage remediation industry has a higher-than-average rate of predatory operators.

**Storm chasers.** Companies that show up at your door unsolicited after a weather event, often with out-of-state plates. They may do adequate emergency work but are often unavailable for follow-up, don't carry proper local licenses, and disappear if problems arise.

**High-pressure tactics.** "We need to start right now or your whole house will be ruined." While speed matters with water damage, a legitimate company will take 15 minutes to explain their process, provide an estimate, and answer your questions before starting work. Anyone who refuses to pause for a conversation is not trustworthy.

**No written estimate or contract.** Emergency work sometimes starts before a full estimate is possible — that's normal. But the company should provide a written scope and estimated cost within 24 hours. Never let work continue open-ended with no documentation.

**Refusing to work with your insurance.** Most reputable companies deal with insurance daily and welcome the process. A company that wants cash only or refuses to provide documentation compatible with insurance claims is a red flag.

**Dramatically low estimates.** Water damage remediation has fairly standardized pricing based on the IICRC's S500 standard and industry pricing guides (like Xactimate, which most insurers use). A company bidding 40% below everyone else is cutting corners — inadequate drying, skipping antimicrobial treatment, or underscoping the demolition.

## What Legitimate Remediation Costs

| Service | Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Water extraction (per room) | $150–$500 |
| Structural drying (per day, including equipment) | $200–$800 |
| Drywall demolition (per linear foot) | $3–$8 |
| Carpet removal and disposal | $1–$3 per sq ft |
| Antimicrobial treatment | $100–$500 |
| Moisture monitoring (per visit) | $75–$200 |
| Content manipulation (moving/protecting items) | $200–$600 |
| Mold remediation (if needed) | $500–$5,000+ |

A single-room water damage remediation — extraction, demolition, structural drying, and antimicrobial treatment — runs $1,500–$4,000. Multi-room or whole-floor damage runs $3,000–$10,000 for remediation alone, before any reconstruction.

## The Insurance Process

Dealing with insurance during water damage is its own challenge.

**File immediately.** Call your insurance company within 24 hours of discovering the damage. Most policies require prompt notification.

**The adjuster visit.** Your insurer sends an adjuster to assess damage and determine coverage. This may happen same-day for severe cases or within a few days for moderate damage.

**Preferred vendor vs. your choice.** Insurance companies may recommend (or strongly push) their preferred vendors. You have the legal right to use any licensed, insured remediation company. Using a non-preferred vendor means more direct negotiation with the adjuster, but it doesn't affect your coverage.

**Documentation.** The remediation company should provide detailed records including moisture readings (before and after), photos of all work, an itemized invoice, and a certificate of completion. This documentation supports your claim.

**Deductible.** Your standard homeowners deductible applies — $500–$2,500 for most policies. The remediation company gets paid the full amount; you pay the deductible to the insurer.

## Questions to Ask Before Hiring

1. "Are your technicians IICRC-certified? Which certifications do they hold?"
2. "Can you provide your certificate of insurance including general liability and workers' comp?"
3. "Do you work directly with insurance companies? Are you familiar with Xactimate pricing?"
4. "What is your response time for emergencies?"
5. "Will you provide a written scope of work and estimate within 24 hours?"
6. "Who will be supervising the work on-site?"
7. "How do you determine when drying is complete? What moisture readings do you target?"
8. "Do you provide a warranty on your remediation work?"
9. "Will you handle reconstruction, or do I need a separate contractor?"
10. "Can you provide references from recent water damage jobs?"

A company that answers these confidently and specifically is likely competent. Vague or evasive answers mean you should cross them off your list.

## After Remediation: The Reconstruction Phase

Once remediation is complete and the structure is verified dry, reconstruction begins as a separate project:
- New drywall installation, taping, mudding, and sanding
- Baseboard and trim replacement
- Painting
- New flooring installation
- Cabinet repair or replacement if affected

For related repair guidance, see our guides on [water heater repair](/blog/water-heater-plano-repair-diy-or-call-a-pro/) and [water heater troubleshooting](/blog/water-heater-constantly-running-a-diyers-honest-take/) — water heater failures are one of the most common causes of residential water damage.

The [NFPA 70 electrical code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) has specific requirements for electrical systems after water exposure. Any wiring, outlets, or panels that were submerged or exposed to significant moisture need professional evaluation before power is restored to those circuits.
