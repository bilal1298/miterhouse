You'll spend $200 to $3,000 on a bathroom vanity countertop depending on material, plus $150–$500 for labor if you hire it out. But here's the thing — a prefab countertop swap is one of the most approachable bathroom upgrades out there. Most take a single Saturday from start to finish, and the visual impact is immediate.

## Material Costs: Prefab vs. Custom

### Prefab (Budget-Friendly)

Prefab tops come ready to install with the sink cutout already made. They fit standard vanity widths (24, 30, 36, 48, and 60 inches).

- **Cultured marble:** $100–$300. Integrated sink and backsplash, light, available everywhere. Looks decent but scratches over time.
- **Laminate:** $50–$200. Cheapest option. Many patterns including stone imitations. Not waterproof at seams — fine for a powder room, skip it in full baths.
- **Solid surface (Corian-type):** $200–$500. Repairable, seamless, decent durability. A solid middle ground.
- **Prefab granite:** $200–$600. Real stone, pre-cut and polished. Limited colors but genuine stone at a fraction of custom cost.

### Custom Fabricated (Mid to High End)

Measured, cut, and finished to your exact vanity dimensions. Any sink type, any edge profile, any configuration.

- **Granite:** $400–$1,200. Heat-resistant, unique patterns. Needs annual sealing.
- **Quartz:** $400–$1,500. Non-porous, no sealing, consistent color. The most popular bathroom vanity upgrade for good reason.
- **Marble:** $500–$2,000. Elegant but high-maintenance — etches from acidic bathroom cleaners. Best for powder rooms with minimal exposure.
- **Quartzite:** $600–$2,500. Harder than granite, resists etching better than marble. Stunning but the price is real.
- **Concrete:** $500–$2,000. Custom-poured and sealed. Industrial look, heavy, needs periodic sealing.

### The Sink Compatibility Factor

Your countertop choice dictates sink options. Prefab cultured marble includes an integrated sink — nothing else to buy. Custom stone needs a separate sink ($50–$400) plus a cutout ($100–$200 from the fabricator).

Undermount sinks require support clips and adhesive, adding installation complexity. Drop-ins just sit in the cutout. Vessel sinks (bowls on top) need only a small drain hole, making them popular for DIY countertop installs.

## Labor Costs

**DIY (prefab):** Free. The learning curve is shallow. You need silicone caulk, a drill, an adjustable wrench, a level, and possibly a jigsaw.

**Professional prefab installation:** $100–$250 if added to another service call.

**Custom stone template and install:** $200–$500 for installation (fabrication cost is separate). Most fabricators fold installation into the total quote — confirm this when comparing.

**Plumbing reconnection:** $100–$200 if done as a standalone plumber visit.

## DIY Installation: Prefab Countertop Swap

This is the process for replacing a countertop on an existing vanity — the most common scenario.

**1. Cut the water.** Shut off both supply valves under the sink. Open the faucet to drain remaining water. Bucket under the P-trap.

**2. Disconnect plumbing.** Remove the P-trap (hand-tight fittings usually come apart without tools). Disconnect supply lines from the faucet. If reusing the faucet, remove it carefully from the old top.

**3. Remove the old countertop.** Check for screws from underneath holding the top to the vanity. Cut through caulk along the wall with a utility knife. Lift the old top off — stone tops need a helper.

**4. Level the cabinet.** Set a 2-foot level on the vanity in multiple directions. Shim if needed. An unlevel cabinet causes countertop rocking, poor drainage, and visible gaps at the backsplash. This step is non-negotiable.

**5. Dry-fit.** Set the new countertop in place without adhesive. Check wall contact, front overhang, and drain alignment with the cabinet plumbing.

**6. Install the faucet first.** Much easier to install the faucet, drain assembly, and supply connections before the countertop is permanently set. Flip the top upside down on a padded surface and install all hardware.

**7. Set the countertop.** Bead of silicone along the top edges of the vanity cabinet. Set the countertop in position. Press firmly and check alignment. For wall-mounted backsplash pieces, silicone the bottom edge and press into place.

**8. Reconnect plumbing.** Supply lines to the faucet (hand-tight plus a quarter turn). Reassemble the P-trap. Turn on water and check every connection for leaks.

**9. Caulk.** Silicone where countertop meets wall, where backsplash meets wall, and around the sink rim for drop-ins. Painter's tape for clean lines, smooth with a wet finger.

The whole process takes 2–4 hours for a single vanity. A double vanity adds about an hour.

## Custom Stone: How the Process Works

Going with custom granite or [quartz](/blog/quartz-countertop-installation-pricing-guide-for-homeowners/)? The process is different because you're not doing the fabrication yourself:

1. Choose your material at a stone yard or showroom
2. Fabricator sends a templater after your vanity is installed and leveled
3. Templating takes 30–45 minutes
4. Fabrication takes 5–10 business days
5. Fabricator returns with the finished piece and installs it — usually 1–2 hours

The templating step is critical. The fabricator creates an exact pattern of your vanity dimensions, wall angles, and sink/faucet locations. The vanity must be fully installed and level before they template.

Stone is heavy — a 48-inch granite vanity top weighs 50–80 pounds. Getting it through doorways without chipping an edge requires two people. Also check that the cabinet can handle the weight, especially floating vanities and open-shelf designs.

## Mistakes That Cause Headaches

**Not checking for square walls.** Bathroom walls are rarely straight. If the countertop is cut perfectly square and the wall bows 1/4 inch, you'll have a visible gap. Custom fabrication accounts for this; prefab tops may need scribing to match.

**Overtightening faucet connections.** Brass compression fittings crack when over-torqued. Hand-tight plus a quarter to half turn is all you need. If it leaks, tighten an eighth turn at a time.

**Skipping the leak test.** Run both hot and cold for at least 5 minutes after connecting everything. Check every joint — drain tailpiece, P-trap, and supply connections. Check again 24 hours later.

**Forgetting the overflow.** If the sink has an overflow hole, it must connect to the drain assembly. Some prefab integrated sinks have this built in. For separate sinks, verify the overflow connection comes with the drain kit.

## Picking the Right Material for Your Bathroom

- **Tight budget, rental, or kids' bath:** Cultured marble prefab. Clean look, holds up fine, cheap to replace in 5 years.
- **Mid-range primary bath:** Quartz in a neutral tone with a quality undermount sink and single-hole faucet. Clean, modern, zero maintenance.
- **High-end guest or primary bath:** Natural stone (marble or quartzite) with an undermount sink and wall-mounted faucet. The combination that makes a bathroom feel finished.

The vanity countertop is one of the few bathroom elements where spending more produces a noticeably better daily experience. A $400 quartz top feels meaningfully different from a $100 cultured marble top — and you touch it every single day.

For most homeowners, the sweet spot is a prefab quartz or solid-surface top installed DIY, with professional plumbing help if the connections are unfamiliar. Total: $300–$600, one Saturday, and a bathroom that feels updated without a full [renovation budget](/blog/bathroom-remodel-cost-estimate/).
